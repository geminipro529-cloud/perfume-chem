  1. I-IRIS F-TEC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: as-is (FTEC)

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          PerfumersWorld FTEC — standardized iris/orris accord. Iris 
                  character built primarily from ionones (alpha, beta, allyl), 
                  methyl ionones, and possibly Orris Root CO2 or irone synthetics.
  Synonyms:       I-Iris FTEC, Iris Note
  CAS:            N/A (proprietary blend)
  Formula:        Blend — key molecules likely include alpha-irone, allyl ionone, 
                  methyl ionone, and possibly Orris butter fractions
  MW:             N/A
  BP:             N/A
  VP:             Moderate (heart note)
  CLP:            N/A
  ODT:            N/A

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Ionone/irone complex — iris family accord
  Olfactophore:   The iris odor archetype requires multiple related but distinct 
                  molecules: alpha-irone (the TRUE iris molecule), allyl ionone 
                  (violet-woody), methyl ionone (powdery-violet), and alpha/beta 
                  ionone (violet-fruity). The FTEC should contain this as a 
                  pre-balanced iris concept.
  Mechanism:      Kraft: iris/orris is the most prized smell in perfumery because 
                  it requires alpha-irone — a molecule found only in orris root 
                  (Iris pallida/germanica) at ~0.01% yield after 3+ years of 
                  drying. The FTEC is an attempt to approximate this complex 
                  without using pure alpha-irone or Orris Absolute.
  Key SAR Rule:   Iris = irone + ionones + powdery context. No single molecule 
                  gives "iris" — it's always an accord.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Powdery, violet-woody, slightly earthy, "cold" floral, 
                  elegant, cosmetic-powdery, tender
  Tenacity:       Heart → base; moderate persistence
  Behavior:       A ready-made iris impression. Useful as a starting point or 
                  when you need iris presence without building the full accord from 
                  individual ionones. Compare with ORRIS F-TEC (which may be a 
                  different blend or potency).

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART — Note de coeur (iris/violet family)
  Carles Method:  Pre-built iris heart note. Use alongside your individual ionones 
                  for depth, or alone for quick iris.
  Pairing Rule:   I-Iris FTEC + allyl ionone + methyl ionone = reinforced iris.
                  I-Iris FTEC + musks = powdery iris skin scent.
                  I-Iris FTEC + vetiver = earthy iris (classic Dior combination).

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Iris prêt" (ready iris). A convenience material that provides 
                  the iris concept without extensive blending.
  Craft Note:     Compare I-IRIS FTEC with ORRIS F-TEC and Molecule Iris in your 
                  collection to understand the differences — all three provide 
                  "iris" but likely through different chemistry.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  CENTER → slightly NARCOTIC, cold
  Effect:         Elegant, sophisticated, powdery, cool, refined.
  Map Quadrant:   Center-lower (cold narcotic, elegant)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      FTEC (pre-blended)
  Typical %:      3-10% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     15%
  Best with:      Allyl ionone, methyl ionone, vetiver, musks, cedar
  Avoid:          Nothing significant
  Handle as:      Your "instant iris." Use alone or as a base to enhance with 
                  individual ionones for greater depth and control.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  2. ISO E SUPER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: neat

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          7-Acetyl-1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8-octahydro-1,1,6,7-tetramethyl-
                  naphthalene (mixture of isomers — key isomer: (4aS,7S,8aR))
  Synonyms:       Iso E Super, IES, Patchouli Ethanone, Molecule 01, 
                  Arborone, Isocyclemone E 
  CAS:            54464-57-2 (mixture)
  Formula:        C₁₆H₂₆O
  MW:             234.38 g/mol
  BP:             ~285°C
  VP:             ~0.03 Pa (25°C)
  CLP:            4.90
  ODT:            0.04 µg/L air (but highly variable between individuals — 
                  MASSIVE anosmia in ~30% of population)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Octahydronaphthalenones — woody-amber synthetic
  Olfactophore:   The octahydronaphthalene (decalin) skeleton with a methyl ketone 
                  at C7. The key odorant isomer is one specific stereoisomer (the 
                  "alpha-isomer") within the commercial mixture (~15% of the 
                  total mixture is responsible for 85% of the odor).
  Mechanism:      Ohloff/Kraft: ISO E Super is one of perfumery's most 
                  fascinating molecules because:
                  1. It creates a "halo effect" — a surrounding aura of woody-
                     ambery scent that seems to hover around the wearer rather 
                     than project directionally
                  2. Individual perception varies enormously — some people smell 
                     it strongly, others are completely anosmic (specific anosmia)
                  3. The commercial product is a 4-isomer mixture but the 
                     olfactory activity comes primarily from one isomer
                  
                  Kraft: IES activates receptors in a way that creates 
                  "subliminal presence" — it registers as "something pleasant" 
                  without being identifiable. This is why Molecule 01 (100% IES) 
                  works as a standalone fragrance — people detect "you smell good" 
                  without identifying any specific note.
  Key SAR Rule:   Decalinone skeleton = woody-amber "halo." Perception is 
                  genetically variable. Commercial mixture ≠ pure odorant.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Woody, smooth, ambery, slightly cedar-like, velvety, warm, 
                  "cashmere," subtle, "the scent of being well-dressed"
  Tenacity:       Heart → base; extraordinary persistence, moderate diffusion
  Behavior:       The "invisible enhancer." At low doses (2-5%), adds woody 
                  smoothness without being detectable. At high doses (10-30%), 
                  becomes the "Molecule 01" effect — a woody-amber skin scent. 
                  Can be used at extreme levels (>30%) in avant-garde concepts.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART → BASE — Fond (woody/amber family)
  Carles Method:  Breaks Carles' framework — IES is simultaneously top, heart, 
                  and base. It creates an atmospheric presence rather than 
                  fitting into a linear evaporation curve.
  Pairing Rule:   IES at 5-10% + anything = "improved version of that thing."
                  IES + musks = modern "skin scent."
                  IES + vetiver = sophisticated woody.
                  IES + ambrox = molecular amber.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "L'invisible" (the invisible one). The material that improves 
                  everything without being identifiable. Roudnitska would likely 
                  have embraced it — it embodies his principle that the best 
                  perfumery is when the art is invisible.
  Craft Note:     The most-used material in modern niche perfumery. Sometimes 
                  overused — at >15% it can create anosmic fatigue in sensitive 
                  individuals.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  CENTER (seemingly neutral — bridges all quadrants)
  Effect:         Subliminal, pleasant, "you smell good," unidentifiable.
  Map Quadrant:   Dead center (ambient enhancement)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Neat (isomer mixture)
  Typical %:      5-20% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     50%+ (Molecule 01 is 100%)
  Best with:      EVERYTHING — universal enhancer
  Avoid:          Nothing — but beware anosmia: ask others to evaluate
  Handle as:      Your "invisible improvement." Use at 5-10% as a default in 
                  every formula. Push to 15-30% for "Molecule 01" style effects. 
                  Test on others — you may be partially anosmic yourself.

  3. ISOBUTYL QUINOLINE (10%)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: 10% solution

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          2-(2-Methylpropyl)quinoline
  Synonyms:       Isobutyl Quinoline, IBQ, Leather Quinoline
  CAS:            13223-63-3 (mixture of isomers commonly sold)
  Formula:        C₁₃H₁₅N
  MW:             185.27 g/mol
  BP:             ~285°C
  VP:             ~0.05 Pa
  CLP:            3.86
  ODT:            0.001 µg/L air (EXTREMELY potent)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Quinolines — nitrogen heterocyclic aromatics (leather family)
  Olfactophore:   The quinoline ring (fused benzene-pyridine bicyclic) with an 
                  isobutyl chain. The NITROGEN in the pyridine ring is the key — 
                  nitrogen heterocycles produce uniquely "leathery, tarry, smoky" 
                  aromas that no oxygen-containing compound can replicate.
  Mechanism:      Kraft: The "leather" smell in perfumery comes primarily from 
                  nitrogen compounds. Quinolines (especially alkyl-substituted) 
                  mimic the pyrolysis products of protein — which is why leather 
                  (tanned animal skin) and smoke (burned organic matter) smell 
                  similar: both produce nitrogen heterocycles.
                  
                  Ohloff: IBQ is THE quintessential leather material. A few drops 
                  create convincing leather. The isobutyl group adjusts the 
                  volatility and character — more "refined leather" vs. 
                  unsubstituted quinoline's "raw tar."
                  
                  WARNING: Extremely potent. One of the most powerful 
                  "transforming" materials — a single drop in a formula can shift 
                  the entire character to "leather."
  Key SAR Rule:   Nitrogen heterocycle = leather/tar. Quinoline specifically = 
                  refined leather. Alkyl substituents fine-tune character.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Leathery, dry, tar-like, smoky, slightly medicinal, 
                  raw, dark, animalic, heavy
  Tenacity:       Heart → base; moderate persistence, enormous character impact
  Behavior:       Creates instant "leather" impression. Used in tiny quantities 
                  alongside birch tar for leather accords. At overdose levels, 
                  becomes "chemical plant" — harsh and unpleasant.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART — Modifier (leather/animalic family)
  Carles Method:  Leather modifier. Not a structural note — a CHARACTER note. 
                  A few drops transform a woody composition into a leather one.
  Pairing Rule:   IBQ + birch tar = classic leather accord.
                  IBQ + suederal = smoky suede.
                  IBQ + labdanum + styrax = dark leather.
                  ALWAYS AT TRACE LEVELS.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Cuir" (leather). The raw material of leather perfumery. 
                  Roudnitska didn't favor leather heavily, but acknowledged its 
                  power as a compositional tool.
  Craft Note:     Like guaiacol, IBQ is a "transformation" material — one drop 
                  changes everything. EVALUATE BEFORE ADDING.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  NARCOTIC, warm → STIMULATING (complex — raw/dark)
  Effect:         Masculine, raw, powerful, animalic, dominant.
  Map Quadrant:   Right side (dark narcotic, animalic)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      10% solution (thankfully — makes dosing easier)
  Typical %:      0.01-0.1% of concentrate (neat equivalent)
  Max safe %:     0.5% (neat)
  Best with:      Birch tar, suederal, labdanum, styrax, guaiacol
  Avoid:          Overdose — instantly makes everything smell "chemical"
  Handle as:      EXTREME DANGER MATERIAL (olfactively). Even your 10% solution 
                  requires single-drop dosing. Add 1 drop, evaluate. The 
                  difference between "masterful leather" and "chemical disaster" 
                  is literally one drop.

  4. ISOPROPYL MYRISTATE (IPM)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: neat

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          Isopropyl tetradecanoate
  Synonyms:       IPM, Isopropyl Myristate
  CAS:            110-27-0
  Formula:        C₁₇H₃₄O₂
  MW:             270.45 g/mol
  BP:             ~315°C (decomposes)
  VP:             <0.001 Pa (essentially zero)
  CLP:            7.17 (very lipophilic — dissolves waxy/oily materials)
  ODT:            Odorless (>500 µg/L)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Functional — fatty acid ester solvent/carrier
  Olfactophore:   None — odorless lipophilic carrier
  Mechanism:      IPM is a cosmetic-grade emollient and solvent. In perfumery:
                  1. Dissolves waxy/lipophilic materials that DPG cannot (e.g., 
                     high-MW resinoids, concretes)
                  2. Provides a silky skin feel
                  3. Non-volatile — perfect as a fixed carrier
                  4. Excellent for dissolving macrocyclic musks that crystallize 
                     at room temperature
                  
                  Compare solvents:
                  - DPG: polar, dissolves most things, slightly hygroscopic
                  - IPM: nonpolar, dissolves waxy/oily things, silky feel
                  - DEP: intermediate polarity, traditional fixative
                  - TEC: intermediate polarity, modern fixative/film former
  Key SAR Rule:   Not an odorant. Lipophilic carrier complementary to DPG.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Odorless, oily-silky skin feel
  Behavior:       Invisible carrier for waxy/crystalline materials.

  CARLES / ROUDNITSKA / JELLINEK
  ──────────────────────────────
  Position:       FUNCTIONAL — outside the creative grid
  Role:           Solvent for lipophilic materials, emollient, carrier

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Neat
  Use:            Dissolve crystalline or waxy materials (Habanolide, Exaltolide, 
                  heliotropin, etc.). Use as a carrier for oil-based roll-ons 
                  or attar-style formulations.
  Handle as:      Your "oily solvent." Complementary to DPG. Use when DPG 
                  won't dissolve something. Also useful as the base for solid 
                  perfume or oil-based applications.

  1. JASMINE FO (Fragrance Oil)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: as-is (fragrance oil)

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          Commercial fragrance oil — synthetic jasmine accord. Likely 
                  contains: benzyl acetate, hedione (MDJ), cis-jasmone, 
                  indole (trace), linalool, and proprietary jasmine specialties.
  Synonyms:       Jasmine Fragrance Oil
  CAS:            N/A (proprietary blend)
  Formula:        Blend — key jasmine molecules defined below
  MW:             N/A
  BP:             N/A
  VP:             Moderate (top-heart)
  CLP:            N/A
  ODT:            N/A

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Floral accord — jasmine family (multi-molecular complex)
  Olfactophore:   Jasmine (Jasminum grandiflorum/sambac) is built from FIVE SAR 
                  families that must all be present:
                  1. ESTERS: Benzyl acetate (~25% of natural absolute) — sweet, 
                     fruity-floral top. SAR = benzyl ester = fruity floral.
                  2. JASMONOIDS: cis-Jasmone, methyl jasmonate — the "jasmine 
                     backbone." SAR = Z-pentenyl cyclopentanone.
                  3. INDOLIC: Indole (trace, ~2-3%) — the animalic/fecal depth 
                     that gives jasmine its "sexy" quality. SAR = NH-heterocycle.
                  4. TERPENOLS: Linalool, geraniol — fresh-floral context.
                  5. JASMOLACTONES: γ-Jasmolactone — fruity creamy nuance. 
                     SAR = γ-lactone.
  Mechanism:      Ohloff: Jasmine absolute is the most important floral absolute 
                  in perfumery, used in ~80% of all fine fragrances. Its 
                  complexity (250+ identified compounds) is why no single 
                  synthetic can replace it. A FO attempts to capture this 
                  complexity in a pre-balanced blend.
                  
                  Kraft: The critical challenge of synthetic jasmine is INDOLE — 
                  too little and it smells "flat/soapy," too much and it smells 
                  "fecal." Natural jasmine has ~2-3% indole, perfecly balanced by 
                  benzyl acetate's sweetness.
  Key SAR Rule:   Jasmine = benzyl ester + jasmonoid + TRACE indole + terpenol + 
                  lactone. All five needed. The FO should pre-balance these.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Sweet, floral, slightly fruity, warm, sensual, with possible 
                  indolic (animalic) depth depending on FO formulation. Less 
                  complex than natural absolute but serviceable.
  Tenacity:       Heart; moderate persistence
  Behavior:       A convenience material. The FO quality will determine whether 
                  it's useful as a standalone jasmine or merely as a rough sketch. 
                  Evaluate against your hedione + linalool + benzyl benzoate 
                  combination for comparison.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART — Note de coeur (floral/jasmine family)
  Carles Method:  Pre-built jasmine heart. Jean Carles placed jasmine at the 
                  absolute center of the floral heart — it was his "reference 
                  floral" against which all others were measured.
  Pairing Rule:   Jasmine FO + hedione (10-20%) = enhanced radiant jasmine.
                  Jasmine FO + rose materials = "soliflore pair" (the two queens).
                  Jasmine FO + musks = sensual floral musk.
                  Jasmine FO + orange blossom = classic wedding bouquet.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "La reine des fleurs" (queen of flowers). Jasmine was 
                  Roudnitska's core floral material — it anchors compositions 
                  from chypre to fougère to oriental.
  Craft Note:     A FO won't match natural jasmine absolute, but can serve as 
                  a starting point. Enhance with extra hedione for radiance and 
                  a trace of methyl ionone for depth.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  NARCOTIC, warm, erogenic
  Effect:         Sensual, feminine (traditional), warm, romantic, intoxicating.
  Map Quadrant:   Lower-right (narcotic, erogenic, sweet)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Fragrance oil (pre-blended)
  Typical %:      5-15% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     20%
  Best with:      Hedione, musks, rose materials, orange/linalool
  Avoid:          Evaluate the FO quality — if it smells cheap or chemical, use it 
                  only as a modifier, not as the main jasmine source
  Handle as:      Your "jasmine shortcut." Evaluate carefully. Enhance with hedione 
                  always. Consider building jasmine from scratch using hedione + 
                  linalool + benzyl benzoate + your ionones for more control.

  Your stock: neat

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          Ethyl 2,4,7-decatrienoate (or mixture including similar esters)
  Synonyms:       Kephalis, Scentenal-type (or classified sometimes alongside 
                  similar intense woody-ambery esters by Givaudan)
  CAS:            58985-01-2 (primary isomer)
  Formula:        C₁₂H₁₈O₂
  MW:             194.27 g/mol
  BP:             ~260°C
  VP:             ~0.1 Pa (25°C)
  CLP:            3.52
  ODT:            0.002 µg/L air (very potent)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Conjugated trienoate esters — woody-amber family
  Olfactophore:   A linear ester with a conjugated triene (three C=C double bonds) 
                  system. The extended conjugation creates a unique "woody-ambery-
                  roasted" character. The ethyl ester terminus provides just enough 
                  polarity for receptor interaction.
  Mechanism:      Kraft: Kephalis belongs to a class of "linear woody-ambers" — 
                  molecules that achieve woody character not through a terpenoid 
                  ring system (like sandalwood or cedar) but through an extended 
                  conjugated polyunsaturated chain.
                  
                  Ohloff: Conjugated polyene esters represent a newer SAR class 
                  identified after Scent & Chemistry's publication. They don't 
                  fit neatly into classical terpenoid categories. The tri-conjugated 
                  system provides both woody warmth AND diffusive radiance.
                  
                  Kephalis is noted for extreme radiance — it projects from skin 
                  far more than its VP would suggest, likely because the conjugated 
                  system interacts very efficiently with olfactory receptors 
                  (low ODT = high potency per molecule in air).
  Key SAR Rule:   Linear conjugated triene ester = radiant woody-amber. 
                  Chain conjugation as woody olfactophore (non-terpenoid).

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Intensely woody, smoky-amber, dry, radiant, warm, slightly 
                  "roasted" or "toasted," with an amber undertone
  Tenacity:       Heart → base; moderate-to-good persistence, outstanding diffusion
  Behavior:       Kephalis fills the room. One of the most diffusive woody 
                  materials — it creates a "woody aura" even at low concentrations. 
                  Useful whenever you need wood to project rather than just sit 
                  on skin.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART → BASE — Fond (woody/amber family)
  Carles Method:  Between heart and base — a projecting woody note that 
                  reaches further than most base woods.
  Pairing Rule:   Kephalis + ISO E Super = "molecular wood" duo.
                  Kephalis + ambrox = radiant amber.
                  Kephalis + cedarwood = warm cedar with projection.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Bois radiant" (radiant wood). Wood that projects and 
                  communicates, not wood that hides in the base.
  Craft Note:     Kephalis is the material for when you need people across the 
                  room to notice the woody element, not just the wearer.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  CENTER → NARCOTIC (warm, woody)
  Effect:         Warm, sophisticated, powerful, ambient.
  Map Quadrant:   Center-right (warm, projecting, slightly narcotic)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Neat
  Typical %:      1-5% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     10%
  Best with:      ISO E Super, cedarwood, ambrox, vetiver, patchouli
  Avoid:          Excessive dosing — overtakes lighter elements
  Handle as:      Your "projecting wood." Use for wood that wants to be noticed. 
                  Less than ISO E Super's subliminal approach — Kephalis 
                  projects actively. Try 2-3% alongside 5-10% IES.

                  notes of rose, jasmine, lavender, citrus, and most other 
                  florals. It's what gives naturals their "freshness." Without

                  group shifts the character from "sweet floral" (linalool) to 
                  "fresh herbal-fruity" (linalyl acetate). Esters are always 
                  fresher and fruitier than their parent alcohols.
  Mechanism:      Kraft: Linalyl acetate exemplifies the "alcohol → ester" SAR 
                  transformation: masking the OH as an ester REMOVES the sweet 
                  floral character and ADDS fruity-herbal freshness. This is one 
                  of perfumery's most reliable SAR rules.
                  
                  Linalyl acetate is the molecule that gives bergamot and lavender 
                  their fresh, bright, "aromatic" quality (bergamot = 30-45% 
                  linalyl acetate; lavender = 25-47%).
  Key SAR Rule:   Alcohol → ester = sweeter become fresher/fruitier. 
                  Linalool (sweet floral) → linalyl acetate (fresh herbal).

  Pairing Rule:   Linalyl acetate + linalool = natural lavender reconstruction.

                  Linalyl acetate + dihydromyrcenol = maximum freshness.
