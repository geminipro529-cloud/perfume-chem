━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  1. EBANOL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: neat

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          (E)-3-Methyl-5-(2,2,3-trimethylcyclopent-3-en-1-yl)pent-4-en-2-ol
  Synonyms:       Ebanol, Barosma Phenol (misnomer), synthetic sandalwood
  CAS:            67801-20-1
  Formula:        C₁₄H₂₄O
  MW:             208.34 g/mol
  BP:             ~285°C
  VP:             ~0.02 Pa (25°C)
  CLP:            4.21
  ODT:            0.003 µg/L air (extremely potent)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Sandalwood odorants — β-santalol family
  Olfactophore:   A secondary alcohol on an extended allylic chain attached to a 
                  trimethylcyclopentene ring. Kraft identifies the C=C-C-OH 
                  "allylic alcohol" fragment as the sandalwood olfactophore.
  Mechanism:      Ohloff/Kraft: The sandalwood odor requires three structural 
                  features simultaneously:
                  1. A compact, substituted 5-membered ring (the "anchor")
                  2. An extended sidechain of specific length (~7 bonds)
                  3. A terminal or subterminal hydroxyl (the "polar head")
                  
                  Ebanol satisfies all three. It mimics β-santalol's shape and 
                  polarity, but with a simplified cyclopentene core instead of 
                  santalene's bridged bicyclic system. Kraft ranks Ebanol among 
                  the most faithful synthetic sandalwoods — closer to genuine 
                  sandalwood than Bacdanol.
  Key SAR Rule:   5-membered ring + extended chain (~7 bonds) + terminal OH 
                  = sandalwood. Change any one → odor changes drastically.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Creamy, milky, soft woody, warm sandalwoody, slightly balsamic, 
                  very close to East Indian sandalwood heartwood
  Tenacity:       Heart → base; exceptional persistence (>24h on blotter)
  Behavior:       One of the best synthetic sandalwoods available. Smoother and 
                  creamier than Bacdanol. Provides the "creamy milky" quality 
                  that β-santalol gives to real sandalwood EO.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART → BASE — Fond (woody family)
  Carles Method:  Deep heart/base ingredient. Bridges floral heart into the 
                  woody base with creaminess.
  Pairing Rule:   Ebanol + iris = "cashmere" effect.
                  Ebanol + musks = creamy skin scent.
                  Ebanol + rose oxide = classic new sandalwood.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Bois crémeux" (creamy wood). The sandalwood role — warm, 
                  intimate, skin-like. Roudnitska valued sandalwood as the 
                  material that makes a fragrance feel "alive" against skin.
  Craft Note:     Ebanol can replace East Indian sandalwood in any formula at 
                  roughly equal dosage.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  NARCOTIC, warm, erogenic
  Effect:         Intimate, warm, sensual, comforting, skin-like.
  Map Quadrant:   Lower-right (warm, narcotic, sensual)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Neat
  Typical %:      3-10% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     25%
  Best with:      Iris materials, musks, vanilla, florals, cedarwood
  Avoid:          Nothing specific — universal blender
  Handle as:      Your best sandalwood building block. Use for creamy warmth 
                  and skin-like intimacy in the base.

  2. ETHANOL 96%
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: 96% (4% water azeotrope)

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          Ethanol
  Synonyms:       Ethyl Alcohol, Alcohol, Spirit, EtOH
  CAS:            64-17-5
  Formula:        C₂H₅OH
  MW:             46.07 g/mol
  BP:             78.4°C (96% azeotrope: 78.2°C)
  VP:             ~5900 Pa (25°C — EXTREMELY volatile)
  CLP:            -0.31 (miscible with water and most organics)
  ODT:            ~90 µg/L (faint alcohol odor, functionally invisible)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Functional — primary solvent vehicle
  Olfactophore:   None meaningful at typical use levels
  Mechanism:      Ethanol is the universal perfumery vehicle. Its role:
                  1. Dissolves virtually all aromatics (universal solvent)
                  2. Rapid evaporation projects the top note (spray effect)
                  3. The 96/4 ethanol-water azeotrope is convenient — 
                     anhydrous ethanol reverses to ~96% on exposure to air anyway
                  4. The 4% water helps dissolve some polar ingredients
  Key SAR Rule:   Not an odorant. Vehicle only.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Faintly alcoholic, vanishes within seconds of application
  Behavior:       Invisible carrier. Creates the spray/projection effect at 
                  application. The flash evaporation of ethanol is what people 
                  perceive as the "spritz" of a perfume.

  CARLES / ROUDNITSKA / JELLINEK
  ──────────────────────────────
  Position:       FUNCTIONAL — outside the creative grid
  Role:           Vehicle/solvent

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      96% (4% water — standard perfumery grade)
  Use:            Typical EDP: 80% ethanol, 15-20% concentrate, 0-5% water
                  Typical EDT: 85-90% ethanol, 8-12% concentrate
  Handle as:      Your primary vehicle. Always measure by weight, not volume.
                  Keep sealed — ethanol absorbs water from air.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  3. ETHYL MALTOL (10%)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: 10% solution

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          2-Ethyl-3-hydroxy-4H-pyran-4-one
  Synonyms:       Ethyl Maltol, Veltol Plus
  CAS:            4940-11-8
  Formula:        C₇H₈O₃
  MW:             140.14 g/mol
  BP:             ~160°C (sublimes)
  VP:             ~0.05 Pa
  CLP:            0.03 (polar, barely lipophilic)
  ODT:            0.0001 µg/L air (ULTRA-POTENT — one of perfumery's lowest ODTs)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Pyranones — sugar lactone analogs
  Olfactophore:   The 3-hydroxy-4-pyranone ring — a cyclic enol tautomer 
                  mimicking sugar pyranose. The hydroxyl-adjacent-to-carbonyl 
                  creates a strong chelating hydrogen bond donor/acceptor system 
                  that locks into sweet receptors with extraordinary potency.
  Mechanism:      Ohloff/Kraft: Ethyl maltol is 4-6× more potent as a sweetener 
                  in olfaction than maltol (methyl analog). The ethyl group 
                  increases lipophilicity just enough to improve receptor binding 
                  without pushing it out of the optimal CLP range.
                  
                  CAUTION: Ethyl maltol is so potent (ODT = 0.0001 µg/L) that 
                  even at 10%, a single drop can DOMINATE a formula. This is 
                  likely the molecule that caused the "chocolate overdose" in 
                  your DHI attempt — it amplifies sweetness exponentially.
  Key SAR Rule:   Hydroxy-pyranone = extreme sweetness. More potent than vanillin 
                  by ~100×. The "candy" note in any formula where it appears.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Sweet, caramellic, cotton candy, strawberry jam, warm sugar, 
                  cooked fruit, preserves
  Tenacity:       Heart → base; extreme persistence and diffusion despite low MW
  Behavior:       A SWEETNESS AMPLIFIER more than a distinct note. It makes 
                  everything around it taste/smell sweeter. At <0.5% of concentrate, 
                  it adds background sweetness. Above 1%, it becomes "candy."

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART → BASE — Modifier (gourmand family)
  Carles Method:  Universal sweetness modifier. Not a structural ingredient — 
                  a seasoning.
  Pairing Rule:   Use in TRACES (0.05-0.3% of concentrate).
                  Ethyl maltol + vanillin = "pastry shop."
                  Ethyl maltol + coumarin = "dolce" sweetness.
                  Ethyl maltol at >1% = "candy overdose."

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Sucrosité" (sugariness). Roudnitska would have used this 
                  sparingly if at all — his aesthetic was "beauty through restraint." 
                  Modern perfumery uses it heavily for mass appeal.
  Craft Note:     The #1 cause of "too sweet" in amateur formulas. Use literally 
                  one drop of your 10% solution in a 30mL batch as a starting point.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  NARCOTIC, warm, erogenic
  Effect:         Comforting, cozy, childlike, indulgent.
  Map Quadrant:   Lower-right (deeply narcotic, sweet)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      10% solution
  Typical %:      0.05-0.5% of concentrate (TRACE USE)
  Max safe %:     2% (but olfactively overpowering above 1%)
  Best with:      Vanillin, coumarin, tonka, fruity notes, maple lactone
  Avoid:          Fresh/citrus-heavy compositions (clashes badly)
  Handle as:      DANGER MATERIAL. Use the absolute minimum. Start with 0.1% of 
                  concentrate and evaluate. Your 10% solution still requires 
                  micro-dosing. One drop too many = candy overdose.

  4. ETHYLENE BRASSYLATE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: neat

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          1,4-Dioxacycloheptadecane-5,17-dione
  Synonyms:       Ethylene Brassylate, EB, Musk T, Astratone
  CAS:            105-95-3
  Formula:        C₁₅H₂₆O₄
  MW:             270.37 g/mol
  BP:             ~340°C (decomposes)
  VP:             ~0.0005 Pa (extremely low — true base note)
  CLP:            3.89
  ODT:            0.5 µg/L air (moderate for a musk)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Macrocyclic musks — dilactone subcategory
  Olfactophore:   17-membered ring with two ester (lactone) groups. The ring size 
                  is the key — rings of 14-17 atoms smell musky. The two ester 
                  oxygens add powdery/clean character.
  Mechanism:      Ohloff's macrocyclic musk rule: The muscone-like musky odor 
                  requires a flexible macrocyclic ring of 14-17 atoms. Below 14, 
                  the ring is too rigid (no musk odor). Above 17, the ring becomes 
                  too floppy (ambergris-like).
                  
                  Ethylene brassylate's two ester groups differentiate it from 
                  pure ketone musks (muscone) — the esters add a powdery, clean, 
                  slightly sweet character. This makes EB more "laundry musk" 
                  than "skin musk."
                  
                  Kraft: EB is the most cost-effective macrocyclic musk, making 
                  it the backbone of commercial musk accords. Its weakness is 
                  relatively high ODT (less potent than Habanolide or Exaltolide).
  Key SAR Rule:   17-membered dilactone ring = powdery, clean macrocyclic musk.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Sweet, powdery, clean musky, slightly metallic, waxy, 
                  "laundry fresh," light
  Tenacity:       Deep base — enormous persistence (>48h on fabric)
  Behavior:       The "volume" musk. Not the most refined or the most potent, 
                  but affordable enough to use at high percentages. Creates a 
                  musk "bed" that other musks sit on top of.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       BASE — Fond (musky family)
  Carles Method:  Base layer musk. The foundation of any musk accord.
  Pairing Rule:   EB (volume/base) + Galaxolide (powdery/heart) + Habanolide 
                  (animalic depth) = modern musk triad.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Propreté musc" (musk cleanliness). The clean, soft musk background.
  Craft Note:     Use EB as the "bulk musk" — the affordable foundation — then add 
                  smaller amounts of nobler musks (Habanolide, Ambrettolide) on top 
                  for complexity.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  CENTER → slightly NARCOTIC (warm, clean)
  Effect:         Clean, soft, comforting, neutral-warm.
  Map Quadrant:   Center (balanced, slight warm lean)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Neat
  Typical %:      5-20% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     30%
  Best with:      Galaxolide, Habanolide, Ambrettolide, Ambrox, floral heart notes
  Avoid:          Nothing — universal base note
  Handle as:      Your "musk concrete foundation." Use at high %ages for base 
                  structure. Enhance with more characterful musks on top.

  5. EXALTOLIDE (10%)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: 10% solution

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          Oxacyclohexadecan-2-one (15-Pentadecanolide)
  Synonyms:       Exaltolide, Pentalide, Thibetolide, Globalide, 
                  15-Pentadecalactone, ω-Pentadecalactone
  CAS:            106-02-5
  Formula:        C₁₅H₂₈O₂
  MW:             240.38 g/mol
  BP:             ~330°C (decomposes before boiling)
  VP:             ~0.001 Pa (extremely low)
  CLP:            5.82 (very lipophilic)
  ODT:            0.01 µg/L air (potent for a macrocyclic lactone)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Macrocyclic musks — monolactone (single ester in ring)
  Olfactophore:   16-membered ring lactone. The single ester group and the ring 
                  size of 16 atoms place it in the "animalic musky" zone — more 
                  "skin" than "laundry" compared to ethylene brassylate.
  Mechanism:      Ohloff: Exaltolide was one of the first macrocyclic musks 
                  identified from natural civet (hence the synonym "Thibetolide" 
                  — named for Tibetan musk). Its 16-membered ring with a single 
                  lactone group gives it more animalic/animal warmth than the 
                  cleaner dilactones (EB) or ketones (muscone).
                  
                  Kraft: Exaltolide is the "warm, animal skin" musk vs. 
                  EB's "clean laundry" musk. The single lactone oxygen (vs. EB's 
                  two esters) leaves more hydrophobic surface area, which 
                  translates to greater animalic warmth.
  Key SAR Rule:   16-membered monolactone = warm, animalic, skin-like musk. 
                  More "natural" character than dilactones.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Sweet, warm, musky, slightly animalic, "skin" quality, 
                  powdery-warm, balsamic undertone
  Tenacity:       Deep base — extraordinary persistence. On fabric >72h.
  Behavior:       The most "natural" smelling of the affordable macrocyclic musks. 
                  Creates a warm, comforting, skin-like base. More intimate and 
                  "you" than the cleaner polycyclic musks or EB.

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       BASE — Deep Fond (animalic musk family)
  Carles Method:  The deepest musk layer — "skin musk." Pairs with EB for 
                  depth and Galaxolide for powdery lift.
  Pairing Rule:   Exaltolide (warm/skin) + EB (clean/powder) = balanced musk base.
                  Exaltolide + Ambrettolide = luxurious skin musk duo.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Senteur de peau" (scent of skin). The ingredient that makes 
                  a fragrance smell "like you, but better."
  Craft Note:     Roudnitska deeply valued musk for its ability to create 
                  "intimacy" — the fragrance seems to emanate from the wearer's 
                  skin rather than from a bottle. Exaltolide excels at this.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  NARCOTIC, warm, erogenic
  Effect:         Intimate, warm, sensual, comforting, natural.
  Map Quadrant:   Lower-right (deeply narcotic, erogenic)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      10% solution
  Typical %:      2-10% of concentrate (accounting for 10% dilution, so 
                  20-100% by weight of your solution)
  Max safe %:     10% of concentrate (neat equivalent)
  Best with:      Ethylene brassylate, Galaxolide, Ambrettolide, Ambrox, sandalwood
  Avoid:          Nothing — universal base musk
  Handle as:      Your "skin musk." Use for intimacy, warmth, and the "smells 
                  like clean skin" effect. Your 10% solution means you need higher 
                  weight-volumes to achieve the dose. In a 30mL formula, use 
                  generously.

  1. FLORALOZONE (10%)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: 10% solution

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          2-(2-Methylpropyl)-4,6-dimethyl-1,3-dioxane
  Synonyms:       Floralozone, Florozone, Ozone-Floral, Oxane
  CAS:            25279-09-8
  Formula:        C₁₀H₂₀O₂
  MW:             172.27 g/mol
  BP:             ~195°C
  VP:             ~5 Pa (25°C — fairly volatile)
  CLP:            2.10
  ODT:            0.05 µg/L air (potent)

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Cyclic acetals — 1,3-dioxane family
  Olfactophore:   The 1,3-dioxane ring (six-membered ring with two oxygens) is 
                  the key. The two ethereal oxygens in the ring create an 
                  "ozone-fresh" character, while the isobutyl substituent adds a 
                  subtle green-floral quality.
  Mechanism:      Kraft: The 1,3-dioxane/dioxolane family produces "ozonic" notes 
                  because the acetal group (R-O-CH₂-O-R) releases an aldehyde 
                  slowly by hydrolysis. This creates a "fresh air" perception — 
                  the brain interprets the slow-release aldehyde as "outdoor 
                  freshness" (similar to how ozone after rain releases aldehydes 
                  from organic matter).
                  
                  Floralozone is specifically tuned to give "ozone after rain + 
                  flower shop" character. The dimethyl substitution controls the

  ────────────────────────

  Olfactophore:   The ortho-methoxyphenol group (hydroxyl + methoxyl on adjacent

  Mechanism:      Ohloff/Kraft: Guaiacol is the pyrolysis product of lignin —

                  conformation and creates the specific "smoky" receptor fit.

                  - Vanillin (+ CHO at para) = sweet smoky

  5. HYDROXYCITRONELLAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Your stock: neat

  CHEMICAL IDENTITY
  ─────────────────
  IUPAC:          7-Hydroxy-3,7-dimethyloctanal
  Synonyms:       Hydroxycitronellal, HCA, Muguet Aldehyde, Lauriné
  CAS:            107-75-5
  Formula:        C₁₀H₂₀O₂
  MW:             172.27 g/mol
  BP:             241°C
  VP:             ~0.3 Pa (25°C)
  CLP:            2.07
  ODT:            0.01 µg/L air

  OPK — STRUCTURE-ACTIVITY
  ────────────────────────
  SAR Class:      Hydroxy-aldehydes — muguet (lily of the valley) family
  Olfactophore:   A branched-chain aldehyde (8-carbon) with a tertiary hydroxyl 
                  at C7. The COMBINATION of aldehyde + remote hydroxyl creates the 
                  "soft, sweet muguet" character. Neither an aldehyde alone nor 
                  an alcohol alone could achieve this.
  Mechanism:      Kraft: Hydroxycitronellal is one of the oldest synthetic lily 
                  of the valley materials (first used early 1900s). The key SAR 
                  feature is the DISTANCE between the CHO and the OH groups — the 
                  7-carbon separation creates a specific molecular shape that 
                  mimics natural muguet odorants.
                  
                  Ohloff: Lily of the valley is one of the "impossible to extract" 
                  flowers — no natural absolute or EO exists. All muguet in 
                  perfumery is synthetic. Hydroxycitronellal was the FIRST material 
                  to successfully recreate this note (Diorissimo, 1956).
                  
                  REGULATORY NOTE: IFRA has restricted HCA due to sensitization 
                  potential. Current limit: 1% in leave-on products (skin contact).
  Key SAR Rule:   Long-chain aldehyde + remote hydroxyl (7 carbons apart) = 
                  sweet muguet. The separation distance is critical.

  ARCTANDER — ODOR PROFILE
  ────────────────────────
  Character:      Sweet, lilac-muguet, fresh-floral, slightly citrusy, warm, 
                  powdery-soft, dewy, "lily of the valley"
  Tenacity:       Heart; moderate persistence, moderate diffusion
  Behavior:       The CLASSIC muguet. Softer and sweeter than cyclamen aldehyde, 
                  more powdery than florol. This is the "original" lily of the 
                  valley in perfumery. Still used in every muguet accord despite 
                  restrictions.
  Natural occ.:   Not found significantly in nature — synthetic material that 
                  recreates a natural impossible flower

  CARLES — SYSTEMATIC POSITION
  ────────────────────────────
  Position:       HEART — Note de coeur (muguet/lily family)
  Carles Method:  The anchor of the muguet family. Use as the "center" of any 
                  lily of the valley accord.
  Pairing Rule:   HCA + florol + hedione = classic muguet triad.
                  HCA + cyclamen aldehyde = lily with green edge.
                  HCA + heliotropin = powdery muguet.

  ROUDNITSKA — AESTHETIC ROLE
  ──────────────────────────
  Function:       "Muguet classique" (classic lily). Roudnitska's Diorissimo 
                  (1956) was built on hydroxycitronellal as the primary material, 
                  creating the most celebrated muguet soliflore in history.
  Craft Note:     Roudnitska used HCA at very high levels in Diorissimo (>15% 
                  of concentrate). His genius was realizing that overwhelming 
                  doses of HCA create a photorealistic muguet.

  JELLINEK — PSYCHOLOGICAL MAP
  ────────────────────────────
  Axis Position:  CENTER → slightly NARCOTIC, warm
  Effect:         Sweet, innocent, spring-like, soft, romantic.
  Map Quadrant:   Center-lower (balanced, slightly narcotic-sweet)

  PRACTICAL USAGE
  ───────────────
  Your form:      Neat
  Typical %:      3-15% of concentrate
  Max safe %:     IFRA restricted: check current limit (~1% in leave-on products)
  Best with:      Florol, cyclamen aldehyde, hedione, linalool, musks
  Avoid:          Check IFRA limits — sensitizer at high levels
  Handle as:      Your "classic muguet." Build lily of the valley accords 
                  around it. Pair with florol (green modern muguet) and hedione 
                  (radiant muguet). Mind IFRA restrictions for skin products.
