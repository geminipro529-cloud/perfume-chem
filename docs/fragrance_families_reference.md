## HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Families are organized by the **Michael Edwards Fragrance Wheel** (1983, updated 2010) — the industry standard used by retail and professional perfumers worldwide — supplemented by the **traditional pre-1945 classification** and modern specialty styles. Each entry includes:
- Character / description
- Core structural accord (what gives it its identity)
- Subfamilies / variations

## CLASSIFICATION OVERVIEW

### The Michael Edwards Fragrance Wheel (2010)
Five main families, each with subdivisions arranged in a circle showing scent proximity:

```
                    FLORAL
            Soft Floral | Floral
       Floral Amber ─────────── Floral Amber
            ↑                        ↓
    AMBER (ORIENTAL)             FRESH
      Soft Amber                 Fruity
      Amber                      Green
      Woody Amber                Water/Aquatic
            ↓                        ↑
           WOODY ──────── AROMATIC (FOUGÈRE)
        Mossy Woods           Citrus
        Woods               Aromatic
        Dry Woods
```

**Five main families:**
1. **Floral** — flowers
2. **Amber/Oriental** — warm, sweet, resinous
3. **Woody** — woods, mosses, resins
4. **Aromatic Fougère** — herbs, lavender (historically at the center)
5. **Fresh** — citrus, green, aquatic

**Traditional classification also includes:** Chypre, Leather, Gourmand, Aldehydic Floral, Animalic, Soliflore, Incense/Resinous.

# PART ONE: THE FLORAL GROUP

## 1. SINGLE FLORAL (SOLIFLORE)

**IPA:** [sɔlifloʁ] — French: *solo flower*
**Origin:** The oldest perfume concept — ancient Egypt and Greece used single-flower extracts.
**Edwards Wheel:** Floral subfamily
**Gender tradition:** Primarily feminine

### Character
Centered on one flower's scent, designed to feel like pressing your face into that bloom. Can be natural (rose otto, neroli CO₂) or constructed synthetically for flowers whose scents cannot be extracted (gardenia, freesia, peony). The art is achieving the full dimensionality of one flower across all three pyramid stages.

### Core Accord
Strictly dictated by the chosen flower. For reference, the four most common soliflore targets:
- **Rose:** Geraniol + Citronellol + PEA + Rose Oxide + Nerol + Geranyl Acetate
- **Jasmine:** Indole + Benzyl Acetate + Hedione + PEA + Linalool
- **Iris:** Alpha Irone + Ionones + Orris butter-like accords
- **Muguet (Lily of the Valley):** Hydroxycitronellal + Bourgeonal + Cyclamen Aldehyde + Lilyreal

### Classic Examples
Serge Lutens Sa Majesté La Rose (rose), Lutens À La Nuit (jasmine), Dior Diorissimo (lily of the valley), Penhaligon's Bluebell

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE

## 2. FLORAL BOUQUET (BRIGHT FLORAL / MULTI-FLORAL)

**Origin:** Late 19th century. The term "Bright Floral" was added post-1945 to describe the modern version combining multiple flowers with enhanced clarity.
**Edwards Wheel:** Floral (center of the floral group)
**Gender tradition:** Feminine

### Character
Multiple flowers singing together — no single bloom dominates. Often supported by a warm, soft base (musks, woods, ambers) that serves as a foundation rather than a character. The modern Bright Floral sub-type emphasizes luminosity, airiness, and clarity added by aldehydes or green notes.

### Core Accord
Rose + Jasmine + Other floral (peony, lily, orchid)  
Base: Musks + Sandalwood/Cedar  
Modern variant: add small amounts of Aldehydes C10–C12 for brightness

### Classic Examples
Houbigant Quelques Fleurs (1912), Jean Patou Joy (1930), Estée Lauder Beautiful (1985 — Bright Floral), Lancôme Trésor, Chloé EdP

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Rose (Geraniol, Citronellol, PEA) + Jasmine (Hedione, Indole, Benzyl Acetate) + Freesia HDI + Peonile + Lilyreal ND + musks + cedar/sandalwood base

## 3. SOFT FLORAL (ALDEHYDIC / POWDERY FLORAL)

**Origin:** 1921 — Chanel No. 5 by Ernest Beaux. The first true aldehydic fragrance; defined the concept of abstract, synthetic-forward perfumery.
**Edwards Wheel:** Soft Floral
**Gender tradition:** Feminine; sometimes unisex

### Character
Flowery but veiled — soft, powdery, soapy, sometimes creamy. Aldehydes (C10, C11, C12) amplify and abstract the florals into something luminous and diffuse. Iris and violet notes reinforce the powder dimension. Musks and amber provide the skin-close base. Described as "the scent of a woman's skin after a bath." The Soft Floral has an air of classic sophistication.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Aldehydes C10 + C11 + C12 MNA (very diluted — 0.5–2%) + Bergamot + Neroli  
**Heart:** Rose + Iris (ionones) + Jasmine + Ylang modifiers  
**Base:** Sandalwood + Musk + Vetiver + Amber

### Subfamilies
- **Aldehydic Floral:** Aldehyde-driven (Chanel No. 5 style)
- **Powdery Iris:** Iris/violet-driven powder (Violettes de Grasse tradition)
- **Soapy Floral:** Clean, waxy, barbershop-adjacent (often Rose + Aldehydes + Cedar)

### Classic Examples
Chanel No. 5 (1921), Lanvin Arpège (1927), Guerlain L'Heure Bleue (1912), Chanel No. 19

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Aldehydes C10 + C11 + C11 undecylenic + C12 MNA (all present, pre-diluted 1%) + rose materials + ionones + musks + woods

## 4. FLORAL AMBER (FLORAL ORIENTAL)

**Edwards Wheel:** Sits at the boundary between Floral and Amber on the wheel
**Gender tradition:** Feminine; sometimes unisex

### Character
A warm, sensuous meeting of flowers and oriental richness. The flowers are real and identifiable, but the amber/resinous base gives depth and a "dressed" quality. Often described as sophisticated evening fragrances. The balance between floral brightness and oriental warmth is the defining tension.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + light florals  
**Heart:** Rose or Jasmine (prominent) + Iris  
**Base:** Labdanum + Vanillin + Benzoin + Sandalwood + Musk

### Classic Examples
Guerlain Shalimar (edP — bridges here and Oriental), Dior Poison (1985), YSL Opium (lighter interpretation), Giorgio Beverly Hills Giorgio

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Florals + Labdanum + Vanillin + Benzoin Resinoid + Ambermax + Iso E Super + musks

## 5. SOFT AMBER (SOFT ORIENTAL)

**Edwards Wheel:** Soft Oriental
**Gender tradition:** Unisex to feminine

### Character
The lighter, more approachable face of the Oriental family. Warm but not heavy, spiced but not overwhelmingly so. Incense may be present subtly. Floral notes are present and balance the richness. Often compared to "expensive skin" — a warmth that reads as intimate rather than exotic.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + light spice  
**Heart:** Rose or Iris + light Jasmine  
**Base:** Benzoin + Labdanum (light) + Vanillin + Sandalwood + clean Musk

### Classic Examples
Guerlain Nahéma, Estée Lauder Youth Dew (originally), Calvin Klein Obsession (softer Oriental side)

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Benzoin Resinoid + Labdanum + Vanillin + Tonka Bean FO + florals + sandalwood/cedar + musks

## 6. AMBER / ORIENTAL

**IPA:** [ˈɔʁjɑ̃tal] — term now often replaced by "Amber" in modern classifications
**Origin:** Associated with the "opulent Orient" in Victorian imagination. First codified as a family with Guerlain Shalimar (1925), though Mitsouko (1919) is earlier.
**Edwards Wheel:** Oriental (center)
**Gender tradition:** Predominantly feminine; occasionally unisex

### Character
Rich, warm, sweet, and sensual. Built around resins (labdanum, benzoin, myrrh), amber accords, vanilla and tonka, often with exotic spices (incense, cardamom, pepper). Can have an animalic undertone from musks. Evocative of warm skin, incense smoke, precious woods, and spiced desserts. Long-lasting and projecting.

The term **"Oriental"** is being phased out in favor of **"Amber"** in modern industry parlance (Fragrantica, Edwards' newer editions). Both refer to the same family.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Spice (cinnamon, cardamom, pepper)  
**Heart:** Labdanum + Rose + Jasmine + Incense  
**Base:** Benzoin + Vanillin + Sandalwood + Patchouli + Musk (animalic or clean)

### Subfamilies
- **Floral Amber:** flowers dominant (see above)
- **Soft Amber:** lighter, less resinous
- **Woody Amber:** woods dominant, amber as background
- **Spicy Oriental:** heavy spice expression — cinnamon, pepper, clove, incense
- **Incense Oriental:** frankincense/olibanum dominant

### Classic Examples
Guerlain Shalimar (1925), YSL Opium (1977), Lancôme Magie Noire, Serge Lutens Ambre Sultan, Montale Powder Flowers

## 7. WOODY AMBER (WOODY ORIENTAL)

**Edwards Wheel:** Woody Oriental — bridge between Amber and Woody families
**Gender tradition:** Unisex to masculine (modern increasingly unisex)

### Character
The "dry" side of Oriental — dry woods (sandalwood, cedar, vetiver) carry the warmth instead of syrupy resins. Often a deep, smoky, incense-laced base with amber softening it. Oud (agarwood) naturally fits here. A modern powerhouse territory for niche perfumery.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Cardamom  
**Heart:** Amber accord + Rose or incense  
**Base:** Sandalwood + Cedarwood + Patchouli + Ambrox + Vetiver

### Classic Examples
YSL M7 (2002 — first mainstream oud adjacent), Tom Ford Oud Wood, Amouage Interlude, Montale Black Aoud

## 8. WOODS (TRANSPARENT / CLEAN WOODS)

**Edwards Wheel:** Woods — the "clean" section of the Woody family
**Gender tradition:** Unisex, slightly masculine-leaning

### Character
Dry, clean, transparent woods — sandalwood (creamy), cedarwood (dry pencil shavings), vetiver (earthy, smoky), and modern synthetics (Iso E Super, Ambrox, Clearwood). Contemporary "skin woods" — intimate, close-to-the-body, modern. Often accented with musk or light amber. The dominant territory of "luxury quiet" niche perfumery.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot or Pepper  
**Heart:** Guaiac wood + Sandalwood + Cedar  
**Base:** Ambrox + Iso E Super + Musk

### Subfamilies
- **Dry Sandalwood:** Sandalwood dominant (creamy, milky)
- **Cedarwood/Vetiver:** Dry, angular, earthy
- **Modern Transparent:** Ambrox + musks + light woods (Molecule 02 style)

### Classic Examples
Acqua di Parma Colonia Essenza, Comme des Garcons Hinoki, Iso E Super by Escentric Molecules, Hermès Bois d'Orange

## 9. MOSSY WOODS (CHYPRE FAMILY)

**Origins:** This subfamily houses the **Chypre** accord. The term "Mossy Woods" is Michael Edwards' neutral replacement for Chypre in the Wheel.

### THE CHYPRE

**Pronunciation:** [ʃipʁ] — French for "Cyprus"
**Origin:** Named after the island of Cyprus. François Coty's **Chypre** (1917) created the archetype, drawing on Cyprus's historical connection with aromatics trade (oakmoss, cistus labdanum, bergamot all from the Mediterranean).
**Gender tradition:** Originally feminine; now unisex and masculine variants common

### Character
Chypre is one of the most complex and beloved fragrance structures in history. It is defined by three primary pillars in dynamic tension:
1. **Bergamot** (bright, citrusy top)
2. **Oakmoss** (dark, animalic, earthy, mossy heart/base)
3. **Labdanum** (warm, animalic-amber base)

The result is a perfume that is simultaneously fresh and deep, cold and warm, clean and animalic — a paradox made beautiful. Often described as "the smell of the Mediterranean coast."

**IFRA IMPACT:** Oakmoss (Evernia prunastri) is severely restricted by IFRA due to atranol and chloroatranol allergens. Modern chypres use synthetic substitutes — most importantly **Evernyl** (methyl orsellinate, the primary odorant of oakmoss). Many classic chypres have been reformulated and are considered diminished.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Petitgrain  
**Heart:** Oakmoss (or Evernyl substitute) + Orris/Iris + Rose + Jasmine  
**Base:** Labdanum + Patchouli + Musk (often animalic) + Vetiver

### Subfamilies
- **Floral Chypre:** Flowers (rose, jasmine) dominate the heart — most classic feminine type
- **Fruity Chypre:** Peach, plum, or aldehyde-fruit in the top (Mitsouko style)
- **Green Chypre:** Galbanum + cut-grass sharpness added
- **Leather Chypre:** Leather accord in the dry-down
- **Aromatic Chypre:** Lavender or sage elements added
- **Woody Chypre:** Modern type — woods replace some mossy character

### Classic Examples
Guerlain Mitsouko (1919), Rochas Femme (1944), Miss Dior original (1947), Hermès Calèche, Piguet Bandit

## 10. DRY WOODS (LEATHER / SMOKY)

**Edwards Wheel:** Dry Woods
**Gender tradition:** Historically masculine; now widely unisex

### Character
Austere, stark, dry, sometimes smoky or rubbery. Cedar, vetiver, and dry amber give the dryness. Leather and birch tar elements can dominate, giving a dark, almost mineral or industrial quality. Often describe as "a library," "old saddle," or "smoked oakwood." Some overlap with the Leather family.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot or Pepper  
**Heart:** Cedar + Cypriol/Nagarmotha (if available)  
**Base:** Vetiver + Birch Tar + Guaiacol + Patchouli + Smoky amber

### Classic Examples
Chanel Sycomore (vetiver/dry wood), Knize Ten (leather/wood), Antaeus by Chanel, Fahrenheit by Dior (violet + birch tar)

## 11. LEATHER

**Origin:** Emerged from the traditional cuir de Russie (Russian leather) style of the early 20th century, developed from actual leather-tanning aromatics (birch tar, smoky phenolics). Chanel Cuir de Russie (1924) is the archetype.
**Edwards Wheel:** Dry Woods (Leather sits at the extreme end of this subfamily)
**Gender tradition:** Originally masculine; now strongly unisex, many feminine leathers

### Character
Evocative of tanned leather, saddle shops, suede, or industrial rubber, depending on the variant. Supported by honey, tobacco, and wood tars in the traditional style. Modern "suede" leathers are softer and more skin-intimate. A deeply animalic family that can border on harsh or erotic.

### Core Accord
**Traditional:**
- Top: Bergamot + Aldehydes  
- Heart: Rose, Iris + *castoreum* (now synthetic)  
- Base: Birch Tar + Labdanum + Oakmoss + ISO-butyl Quinoline

**Modern Suede:**
- Top: Bergamot or Citrus  
- Heart: Iris + Peach/lactone  
- Base: Suederal + Leather FO + Cedar + Musk  

### Subfamilies
- **Russian Leather (Cuir de Russie):** Birch tar dominant, smoky, harsh, austere
- **Tobacco Leather:** Warm, honeyed; tobacco + leather interplay
- **Suede:** Soft, powdery, skin-close leather (modern)
- **Floral Leather (Cuir Fleuri):** Floral heart softens the leather backbone

### Classic Examples
Chanel Cuir de Russie (1924), Robert Piguet Bandit (1944), Hermès Bel Ami, Guerlain Habit Rouge, Tom Ford Tuscan Leather (modern)

## 12. FOUGÈRE (AROMATIC FOUGÈRE)

**Pronunciation:** [fu.ʒɛʁ] — French: *fern*
**Origin:** **Fougère Royale** by Houbigant, 1882 (perfumer Paul Parquet). The first fragrance to use a *synthetic* aromatic compound — **coumarin**, synthesized by William Perkin in 1868. The scent of fern is itself elusive and abstract; the family name is poetic, not literal.
**Edwards Wheel:** At the center in the original 1983 wheel; moved to the Aromatic sector in modern versions. Considered the bridge between Fresh/Citrus and Woody/Amber.
**Gender tradition:** Predominantly masculine; slowly expanding to unisex and feminine

### Character
The backbone of men's fragrance since the 1880s. Built on three pillars in classical tension:
1. **Lavender** — aromatic, slightly sweet top/heart
2. **Oakmoss/Evernyl** — earthy, mossy anchor
3. **Coumarin** — warm, hay-like, sweet-tobacco dry-down

The result smells herbal, clean, slightly sweet, and distinctively "masculine" in the Western tradition. Modern fougères add: woods, citrus, aromatic herbs (sage, rosemary, geranium), and increasingly aquatic/fresh elements.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Lemon + Lavender + Geranium  
**Heart:** Oakmoss (or Evernyl) + Geraniol + Hedione  
**Base:** Coumarin + Tonka + Musk + Vetiver/Cedar

### Subfamilies
- **Classic Fougère:** Strict lavender-oakmoss-coumarin triangle
- **Aromatic Fougère:** Adds sage, rosemary, thyme (herbal depth)
- **Floral Fougère:** Rose or jasmine breaks the herbal character
- **Woody Fougère:** Strong wood base
- **Fresh/Aquatic Fougère:** Calone or fresh aquatic accord added (late 1990s–2000s trend)
- **Spicy Fougère:** Pepper or cardamom adding warmth
- **Oriental Fougère:** Heavy amber/musk dry-down

### Classic Examples
Fougère Royale by Houbigant (1882), Brut by Fabergé (1964), Paco Rabanne Pour Homme (1973), Drakkar Noir by Guy Laroche (1982), Azzaro pour Homme (1978), Eternity for Men by Calvin Klein, YSL Libre (feminine fougère, 2019)

## 13. CITRUS / COLOGNE

**Origin:** The oldest fragrance family — traced to **Aqua Admirabilis** (1693) by Giovanni Paolo Feminis in Cologne, Germany, hence "eau de cologne." The Mediterranean citrus tradition (bergamot from Calabria, lemon from Sicily) runs through the heart of Western perfumery.
**Edwards Wheel:** Fresh → Citrus
**Gender tradition:** Unisex — the most gender-neutral family

### Character
Bright, transparent, energetic, uplifting. Dominated by the peel oils of citrus fruits — bergamot, lemon, lime, orange, grapefruit, yuzu, mandarin. Traditionally short-lived (citrus top notes evaporate quickly), but modern techniques use synthetics (Dihydromyrcenol, citrus-like musk accords) to extend longevity. The "eau fraîche" style is the most diluted expression.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Lemon + Lime + Grapefruit  
**Heart:** Neroli + Petitgrain + Lavender (optional)  
**Base:** Musks + Cedarwood + Amber (light)  
Modern: Dihydromyrcenol anchor for longevity

### Classic Examples
4711 Original Eau de Cologne (1799), Guerlain Eau de Cologne Impériale (1853), Hermès Eau d'Orange Verte, Acqua di Parma Colonia, Jo Malone Lime Basil & Mandarin

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Bergamot EO + Bergamot FCF + Blood Orange + Grapefruit FCF + Cedrat + D-Limonene + Citral + Neroli EO + Petitgrain EO + Dihydromyrcenol + musks + cedar

## 14. GREEN

**Origin:** Post-WWII. First significant green fragrance: Sisley Eau de Campagne (1974). Considered a lighter, more modern interpretation of the Chypre structure.
**Edwards Wheel:** Fresh → Green
**Gender tradition:** Unisex to feminine; some masculine greens

### Character
The scent of freshly cut grass, crushed leaves, green herbs, cucumber, tomato vine. Sharp, chlorophyll-rich, sometimes almost violent in its freshness. The green family can be divided into the "crushed leaf" type (cis-3-hexenol dominant) and the "mossy green" type (galbanum dominant). A fundamentally nature-connected family.

### Core Accord
**Top:** cis-3-Hexenol + Bergamot + Galbanum  
**Heart:** Cyclamen + Green floral + Violet leaf  
**Base:** Oakmoss (or Evernyl) + Cedar + Musk + Vetiver

### Subfamilies
- **Galbanum Green:** Resinous, earthy green (Vent Vert style)
- **Cut Grass:** cis-3-hexenol forward, transparent
- **Green Chypre:** Merges with Mossy Woods territory
- **Cucumber/Aquatic Green:** Overlaps with Water family

### Classic Examples
Balmain Vent Vert (1947 — first great green), Estée Lauder Aliage (1972), Sisley Eau de Campagne (1974), Calvin Klein Eternity (has green facets), Hermès Un Jardin sur le Nil

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
cis-3-Hexenol + Galbanum Resinoid (10%) + Verdox + Scentenal (1%) + Bergamot + Cyclamen Aldehyde + Parmavert + Leafovert + cedar + musks + Evernyl

## 15. WATER / AQUATIC / OCEANIC / OZONIC

**Origin:** First modern aquatic: **Davidoff Cool Water** (1988, perfumer Pierre Bourdon), inspired by Dior Eau Sauvage but with calone. Christian Dior Dune (1991) brought it fully mainstream. Calone was synthesized in 1966 and largely ignored until Mure et Musc (L'Artisan, 1978) introduced limited use, then Cool Water turned it into a movement.
**Edwards Wheel:** Fresh → Water
**Gender tradition:** Strongly unisex; many became the signature "masculine freshness" of the 1990s

### Character
A clean smell reminiscent of the ocean, sea spray, salt air, hot sand, ozone, or swimming pools. Built primarily on synthetic molecules unknown in nature: **Calone** (marine, melon, metallic), **Helional** (sea mist, mineral), **Floralozone** (fresh violet-like ozonic), and **Dihydromyrcenol** (lime, metallic, clean). The effect can be literally oceanic or simply "very very clean."

### Core Accord
**Top:** Dihydromyrcenol + Calone + Bergamot  
**Heart:** Cyclamen + Hedione + Helional  
**Base:** Ambrox + Cedar + Musk + Floralozone

### Subfamilies
- **Marine/Oceanic:** Strong calone, seaweed-adjacent, raw ocean
- **Ozonic:** More metallic, electric-air quality (thunderstorm or high altitude)
- **Fresh Aquatic:** Clean, transparent, linear — overlaps with Citrus
- **Floral Aquatic:** Florals soften the marine edge

### Classic Examples
Davidoff Cool Water (1988), Christian Dior Dune (1991), Kenzo L'Eau par Kenzo (1996), Acqua di Giò by Armani (1996), Issey Miyake L'Eau d'Issey (1992)

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Calone (1%) + Floralozone (10%) + Helional + Dihydromyrcenol + Cyclamen Aldehyde + Undecavertol + Dynascone + Hedione + Bergamot + Ambrox Super + musks

## 16. FRUITY

**Origin:** Modern category, post-1990. Primarily driven by synthetic fruit components unavailable from natural extraction. Closely tied to the "gourmand" wave.
**Edwards Wheel:** Fresh → Fruity
**Gender tradition:** Predominantly feminine; occasionally unisex

### Character
Features non-citrus fruits: peach (gamma-decalactone), blackcurrant (cassis — Blackcurrant FTEC), raspberry, mango, passionfruit, plum, apple. Can range from light and transparent (fruity floral) to rich and syrupy (fruity oriental). Lactones are central to fruity-peach-creamy characters.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate (apple) + Hexyl Acetate (green apple)  
**Heart:** Gamma Decalactone (peach) + Raspberry Ketone + florals  
**Base:** Vanilla + Musks + Sandalwood

### Subfamilies
- **Fruity Floral:** Fruits support a floral heart
- **Fruity Oriental:** Rich fruit + amber base
- **Fruity Gourmand:** Caramelized fruit, candy-like

### Classic Examples
Ginestet Botrytis, Thierry Mugler Alien (some fruity facets), Juicy Couture Viva La Juicy, Viktor & Rolf Flowerbomb, Lancôme La Vie est Belle

## 17. AROMATIC (HERBAL / SPICE AROMATIC)

**Edwards Wheel:** Fresh → Aromatic (the bridge to Fougère)
**Gender tradition:** Predominantly masculine; unisex territory expanding

### Character
Dominated by aromatic herbs: lavender, rosemary, sage, thyme, basil, mint, tarragon. Strongly connected to the kitchen garden and Mediterranean scrubland. Can be clean and sporty, or complex and almost medicinal. Often accompanied by a citrus top and a woody/musky base. The lavender-herb direction is the defining modern masculine "barbershop" territory.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Lavender + Sage (or Rosemary)  
**Heart:** Geranium + Hedione + Cardamom  
**Base:** Cedarwood + Vetiver + Musk + Coumarin

### Classic Examples
Hermès Eau Sauvage (1966 — essential aromatic), Dior Fahrenheit (aromatic+leather), Guerlain Habit Rouge (aromatic oriental), Chanel Allure Homme, James Heeley Cardinal

## 18. GOURMAND

**Pronunciation:** [ɡuʁmɑ̃] — French: *one who loves good food (sometimes to excess)*
**Origin:** **Angel** by Thierry Mugler (1992, perfumers Olivier Cresp and Yves de Chirin). First fragrance to use ethyl maltol, patchouli, and vanillin in a deliberately food-like confection. Considered radical at the time; dominant commercial category by the 2010s.
**Edwards Wheel:** Modern addition — bridges Fresh/Fruity and Amber
**Gender tradition:** Originally feminine; increasingly unisex (masculine gourmands growing rapidly)

### Character
Described as "olfactory desserts" — fragrances that smell like you could eat them. Edible or dessert-like notes: vanilla, caramel, chocolate, cotton candy, honey, praline, coffee, coconut, maple, condensed milk. The base is typically non-edible (patchouli, musk, woods) to prevent the fragrance tipping into food/flavoring territory. The gourmand is the dominant commercial fragrance style of the 21st century.

### Gourmand ≠ Sweet Oriental: Gourmands are explicitly food-referential; orientals use sweetness to support resin accords. Angel smells like candy; Shalimar smells like ancient temples with sweet resin.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Ethyl Maltol (cotton candy) + Bergamot or Grapefruit  
**Heart:** Heliotropin + Raspberry or Peach + Anisaldehyde  
**Base:** Patchouli + Vanillin + Coumarin + Tonka Bean + Musk

### Subfamilies
- **Vanilla Gourmand:** Vanillin/ethyl vanillin-centric, simple and accessible
- **Chocolate Gourmand:** Cocoa/mocha element (often synthetic)
- **Caramel Gourmand:** Maple Lactone or Ethyl Maltol-driven
- **Fruity Gourmand:** Raspberry + cotton candy + patchouli
- **Coffee Gourmand:** Roasted coffee note + vanilla
- **Tobacco Gourmand:** Warm coumarinic-tobacco + sweet musk

### Classic Examples
Thierry Mugler Angel (1992), Mugler Alien, Lancôme La Vie est Belle, YSL Black Opium, Maison Margiela Replica Jazz Club (tobacco gourmand)

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Ethyl Maltol (10%) + Vanillin (10%) + Ethyl Vanillin + Coumarin (20%) + Maple Lactone (20%) + Raspberry Ketone + Heliotropin Fleuressence + Anisaldehyde + Gamma Decalactone + Patchouli EO + Tonka Bean FO + musks

## 19. IRIS / POWDERY / VIOLET

**Origin:** Iris-based perfumery dates to ancient Greece. The modern iris style was refined in Grasse in the 19th century. The category became distinct with Guerlain L'Heure Bleue (1912) and Chanel No. 19 (1971).
**Edwards Wheel:** Bridges Soft Floral and Floral/Amber
**Gender tradition:** Traditionally feminine; now strongly unisex

### Character
Iris (from Orris root — the rhizome of *Iris pallida* or *germanica*) has an almost paradoxical character: simultaneously powdery, cool, creamy, slightly metallic, and faintly floral (violet-adjacent). The key odorant is **irone** (specifically alpha-irone), along with various ionones. Iris also carries a carroty, root-like, earthy facet. The broader powdery category includes violet (ionones), heliotrope (anisaldehyde + heliotropin), and old-fashioned cosmetic powder references.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Green (often Galbanum)  
**Heart:** Alpha Irone + Irotyl + Iris Fleuressence  
**Base:** Sandalwood + Musk + Ambrette note

### Subfamilies
- **Iris Chypre:** Iris + bergamot + oakmoss — the most sophisticated
- **Violet Powdery:** Beta Ionone + Alpha Ionone + light musks
- **Heliotropic:** Heliotropin + Anisaldehyde = talcum powder sensation
- **Iris Woody:** Iris + dry woods, increasingly unisex

### Classic Examples
Guerlain Après L'Ondée, Chanel No. 19, Prada L'Homme (Iris + Ambrox + wood), Diptyque L'Ombre dans l'Eau, Frederic Malle Iris Poudre, Dior Homme Intense

## 20. INCENSE / RESINOUS / SPIRITUAL

**Origin:** The oldest human use of fragrance — incense has been burned in ritual contexts for 5,000+ years. Frankincense (olibanum) and myrrh defined ancient Egyptian, Hebrew, and Roman religious practice. The modern "incense" fragrance family emerged as niche perfumery explored non-Western traditions.
**Edwards Wheel:** Overlaps Amber (Oriental) and Woody; sometimes classified under Dry Woods
**Gender tradition:** Strongly unisex

### Character
Smoky, resinous, warm, meditative. The scent of ancient temples, burning resins, hot stone in a church. Olibanum (frankincense) is lemony-sweet-smoky; myrrh is darker, more balsamic and medicinal. Benzoin and labdanum shift toward the amber direction. Guaiacol and birch tar add a rubbery-smoky phenolic facet. When combined with cedar and musks, the result is the modern "spiritual/dark" niche aesthetic.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Pepper or Cardamom  
**Heart:** Olibanum + Myrrh EO  
**Base:** Labdanum + Benzoin + Guaiacol + Cashmeran + Cedar

### Classic Examples
Comme des Garçons Wonderoud, Serge Lutens Ambre Sultan, Guerlain Spiritueuse Double Vanille, L'Artisan Parfumeur Passage d'Enfer (incense + lily), Maison Margiela Replica Flower Market

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Olibanum Resinoid + Myrrh EO + Benzoin Resinoid + Labdanum + Guaiacol + Cashmeran (20%) + Birch Tar (minimal) + Cedarwood + Iso E Super + musks

## 21. TOBACCO / HAY / COUMARINIC

**Origin:** Tobacco in perfumery derives from a Victorian tradition using actual tobacco absolute. The coumarinic "warm hay" character emerged with Fougère Royale (1882). Now an independent modern family, particularly in the masculine niche market.
**Edwards Wheel:** Amber (Oriental) or Aromatic Fougère; also a modifier within other families
**Gender tradition:** Traditionally masculine; now firmly unisex

### Character
Warm, sweet, animalic, slightly dry, nostalgic. The "tobacco" note in perfumery is not actually cigarette smoke — it evokes the smell of cured tobacco leaf, pipe tobacco, or tonka/coumarin. Hay notes (ethyl safranate, coumarin) give a warm, slightly caramelized grassiness. This family includes the "Bois de Tabac" (tobacco wood) style dominant in contemporary niche.

### Core Accord
**Top:** Bergamot + Rum accord  
**Heart:** Rose or Geranium + Tobacco note  
**Base:** Coumarin + Tonka + Ethyl Safranate + Labdanum + Cedar + Musk

### Classic Examples
Dunhill Edition (tobacco forward), Annick Goutal Sablé, Serge Lutens Chergui (tobacco + honey + iris), Tom Ford Tobacco Vanille, Maison Margiela Replica Jazz Club

### Buildable from Inventory: ✅ FULLY BUILDABLE
Coumarin (20%) + Ethyl Safranate + Tonka Bean FO + Labdanum + Ethyl Maltol + Vanillin + Maple Lactone + Iso E Super + Cashmeran + musks

## 22. ANIMALIC

**Origin:** Pre-synthetic perfumery relied heavily on animal-derived fixatives: musk (deer musk gland), civet (civet cat secretion), castoreum (North American beaver), and ambergris (sperm whale). These gave fragrances a distinctive "warm skin" and lasting power.
**Edwards Wheel:** Amber territory; used as a modifier throughout the wheel
**Gender tradition:** Broadly unisex

### Character
Warm, bodily, skin-close, intimate. At low doses: the richness of human skin, warmth, intimacy. At high doses: feral, sweaty, pissy, animalic in the literal sense. The animalic quality is fundamentally linked to the concept of *pheromone-adjacency* — making the wearer smell more human and sensual. Indole (present in jasmine and feces at different concentrations) is a key animalic floral modifier.

**Ethical context:** Natural animal-derived materials (civet, castoreum, musk deer) are now almost universally replaced by synthetics or not used. Ambergris from floating specimens is still harvested ethically but rare. Musk Ketone is a synthetic nitro-musk with good animalic effect.

### Core Accord
**Traditional (historical):** Civet + Castoreum + Musk + Labdanum + Rose  
**Modern synthetic:** Musk Ketone + Isobutyl Quinoline + Labdanum + Indole (trace) + Lactones

### Classic Examples
Guerlain Jicky (1889 — coumarin + animalic), Guerlain Shalimar (civet base), Yves Saint Laurent Kouros (heavily animalic), Robert Piguet Bandit

### Buildable from Inventory: ⚠️ PARTIALLY BUILDABLE
- Have: Musk Ketone (10%) ✅ — some nitro-musk animalic character
- Have: Indole (10%) ✅ — animalic floral facet
- Have: Isobutyl Quinoline (10%) ✅ — leather/animalic
- Have: Labdanum ✅ — animalic warmth
- Have: Macrolide, Habanolide, Exaltolide — musky, some animalic facets
- **Missing:** Civet, Castoreum, Ambergris naturals
- **Assessment:** Can create an animalic *effect* with Musk Ketone + Indole + Labdanum + IBQ; will not match the raw, barnyard quality of true civet-forward formulas

# APPENDIX B: REGULATORY NOTES

- **Oakmoss (Evernia prunastri):** IFRA restricted (atranol/chloroatranol allergens). Evernyl available as functional substitute.
- **Nitro Musks:** Musk Xylene/Tibetene — banned. Musk Ketone — restricted, available in small amounts. Macrolide musks preferred.
- **Coumarin:** IFRA-restricted at higher concentrations in leave-on products.
- **Isobutyl Quinoline:** Not broadly restricted but use carefully in skin-contact formulations.
- **Cinnamaldehyde:** Common allergen — IFRA guidelines apply.
- **Indole:** Use at trace levels (0.05–0.2% in final formula) to avoid fecal character.
