# Lavender perfumery: architecture, evidence and construction knowledge

Date: 6 October 2026  
Scope: literature research, official-product architecture review, current-source coverage audit, and advisory knowledge integration.  
Status: design knowledge, not proprietary reconstruction, empirical model admission, physical compounding or commercial release.

## Executive decision

Perfume-Chem should not have one “lavender perfume” recipe. It should distinguish the lavender product, its intended sensory register, its structural role, and the surrounding chassis. A naturalistic lavender soliflore, a green barbershop fougere, a lavender-vanilla amber, a feminine lavender/orange-blossom floral, a coffee lavender, and a mineral lavender/Ambrox aromatic are different design problems.

The immediate integration adds source-bounded lavender facts and nineteen architecture profiles to the existing offline knowledge pack. It does not supply measured doses, verified replicas or a universal projection model. The broader expansion is specified in [the implementation plan](FRAGRANCE_KNOWLEDGE_EXPANSION_PLAN_20261006.md).

“All lavender perfumes” is treated as architecture coverage plus a representative, edition-specific reference census. There is no defensible closed list of every historical, regional, reformulated, discontinued, bespoke and future lavender perfume. Additional products can enter the same schema without changing the architecture.

## 1. Evidence method and limits

Sources were sought in the current repository first, then through academic discovery and primary/official web sources.

- Consensus provided discovery leads for lavender GC-O, extraction and cultivar-composition research. Claims below were checked against primary publisher or author-repository records, not accepted from the plugin's generated interpretation.
- SciSpace supplied additional composition and recombination-method leads. The primary PubMed record for Carrasco et al. links an erratum (DOI 10.1055/s-0035-1558195); its numerical composition results are not admitted here because the correction text was not obtained. SciSpace's record is discovery metadata, not the corrected article.
- The GitHub connector successfully read the knowledge pack at the exact baseline commit. MotherDuck returned an authentication-retry response twice and supplied no usable dataset result; no remote data were read or written and no dependency was added.
- Elicit returned an API-access denial. Scite was shown by Plugin Management as available but not installed. Neither supplied evidence in this review.
- The authorized DeepSeek bridge's read-only health check failed with `DAEMON_TRANSPORT`. No task was transmitted and no alternate model/provider was substituted.
- Some direct publisher/full-text opens returned 403, 429 or access challenges. These sources are explicitly described as abstract/selected-section review, not a completed full-text/data review.
- Official brands support marketed architecture and disclosed notes. They do not disclose complete formulas, natural percentages, trade grades, liquid/gas concentrations, diffusion, sensory equivalence or consumer superiority.
- Search snippets from customer reviews, “you may also like” sections and another flanker are not accepted as the target product's official notes.
- Only metadata and original summaries are added. No standards text, full articles, proprietary recipes or empirical datasets are copied.
- No new numerical calibration is admitted. All release, safety, compounding and evidence-admission authority flags remain false.

### The critical Ambrox distinction

There are three different questions:

1. **Design exclusion:** a proposed formula explicitly excludes Ambrox-family ingredients.
2. **Positive commercial disclosure:** an official product record explicitly mentions Ambrox/Ambroxan.
3. **Unknown commercial composition:** the product description does not mention it.

Only the first establishes a no-Ambrox design. The third is not proof that the commercial perfume is Ambroxan-free. An ingredient declaration containing “parfum/fragrance” cannot prove the absence of an undisclosed fragrance ingredient.

## 2. What lavender material actually means

### Primary findings

[Xiao et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indcrop.2017.07.040) examined five lavender oils using descriptive assessment, chemical analysis and GC-O. Their sensory vocabulary included floral, herbal, camphoraceous, sweet, fruity and woody dimensions. Composition and sensory attributes varied across samples. This supports a multifacet material model; it does not establish one universal lavender recipe or a causal geographic quality ranking.

[Guo et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules25235541) compared experimental ethanol extracts and steam-distilled oil. Extraction conditions affected composition and sensory profile. This makes extraction method relevant to a design choice. The study's ethanol extract is not automatically a commercial absolute, and its profile is not an assay of the user's stock. Publisher version metadata were checked; direct full-text access was limited.

[de Elguea-Culebras et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmap.2024.100550) analyzed 215 Spanish lavandin oils from Grosso and Super cultivars. The cultivars and samples differed in their main and minor constituents. The author-repository record is [available here](https://uvadoc.uva.es/handle/10324/74030). A generic lavandin composition cannot silently become exact lot truth.

[Bontoux's catalog](https://bontoux.com/en/our-offer/our-products-2/) distinguishes lavender, named lavandin cultivars, essential oils and absolutes. Catalog presence demonstrates product categories, not the composition or availability of a particular owned bottle.

[Hagvall et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18759894/) found sensitizing oxidation products after lavender oil was exposed to air. Natural origin is not an oxidation-protection or safety certificate. A separate [linalyl-acetate study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0536.2007.01262.x) supports tracking oxidation of this constituent. Neither paper supplies a finished-perfume clearance or universal shelf-life.

### Identity and design table

| Material/product class | What must stay distinct | Useful design question | What cannot be assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lavandula angustifolia oil | Species, cultivar/population, source and extraction | Floral-herbal recognizer, softness, freshness, minor-facet texture | Every fine lavender smells alike; altitude alone proves quality |
| Lavandin, L. x intermedia | Grosso, Super, Abrial and other cultivars | More assertive aromatic register; compare floral/camphor balance | A fixed substitution ratio for angustifolia |
| Spike lavender, L. latifolia | Separate botanical identity | A controlled sharper aromatic/camphoraceous edge | It is interchangeable with soft fine lavender |
| Lavender absolute | Supplier product and extraction method | A fuller or differently textured register to compare | It equals EO plus a generic fixative or the study's ethanol extract |
| Lavender 40/42 | Exact commercial grade and supplier declaration | A standardized design register, if documented | The label proves origin, natural composition or an assay of the current lot |
| Linalool / linalyl acetate | Exact molecular/product identities | Adjust parts of the aromatic-floral scaffold | Two molecules recreate an entire oil or absolute |
| Lavender base / Fleuressence / F-TEC / “lavender aldehyde” | Proprietary composition or exact chemical identity | Accessible sketch, comparison standard or a special modifier | It is a natural oil, an authentic absolute or quantitatively equivalent |

A PerfumersWorld result for [Lavender Absolute #4250, SKU 6HL12275](https://www.perfumersworld.com/view.php?pro_id=6HL12275) was unavailable in the accessed listing and carried commercial-product documentation. Its name or a suggested replacement is not enough to authenticate a natural absolute. Obtain exact product/SDS/composition scope before using it as a natural-material benchmark. This is not a purchase recommendation.

## 7. What literature can and cannot make “correct”

### It can improve

- Correct interpretation of an aromatic versus floral versus gourmand brief.
- Recognition of lavender/non-lavender aromatic references.
- Selection of appropriate recognizer, bridge and support roles.
- Avoidance of default Ambrox/musk/wood stacks.
- Identity/grade distinctions and source-linked uncertainty.
- Construction of meaningful control, omission and block alternatives.
- Stock-independent design separated from physical build arithmetic.

### It cannot establish by itself

- A proprietary brand formula or its exact proportions.
- That one formula smells closer, projects farther or lasts longer on skin.
- That natural lavender or a particular musk is universally more pleasant.
- A whole-product lavender response curve from molecular linalool data.
- A receptor map from chemical-family labels.
- A safe/compliant finished perfume from informal note descriptions.
- Commercial success or superiority over the reference.

The [Wakayama threshold correction](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.0c05822) belongs in the governed gas-intensity capability path, not this architecture layer. [Almeida's finite evaporation/permeation work](https://ciencia.ucp.pt/pt/publications/evaporation-and-permeation-of-fragrance-applied-to-the-skin/) motivates scenario-specific release modeling, not a deterministic “four-hour lavender” claim from formula percentages.

The [2026 mixture-character preprint](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465471/) remains a preprint in the accessed record. Its figure distinguishes 115 component odors at multiple dilutions yielding 144 component stimuli, with 432 mixtures containing two to ten components. A simple profile baseline is worth testing in that domain; it is not evidence that a 60-material perfume's drydown or liking is solved.

[Johnson, Hirson and Ebeler, 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0042693) developed GC recomposition-olfactometry to examine selected recombinations and mixture interactions. This is a useful experimental-method precedent, not an exact lavender-perfume recipe or a guarantee that the proposed role graph is perceptually correct. The expanded floral review should similarly distinguish the [Damask-rose blooming-flower trace-odorant study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31185719/) from rose-oil, rose-absolute and finished-perfume behavior. Its primary DOI is 10.1021/acs.jafc.9b03391; a supporting-information DOI is a separate artifact.

1. **Recognizer comparison:** chosen lavender type/register, with a declared dilution and otherwise fixed background.
2. **Bridge comparison:** geranium, floral, iris, fruit or resin transition appropriate to that one brief.
3. **Base comparison:** no-Ambrox versus one supported Ambrox option only where that question is relevant.

Use small weighed prototypes rather than committing every hypothesis to a large bottle. Compare initial/5-minute character, 30–60-minute heart, four-hour blotter and next-day blotter. An informal one-session comparison supplies personal clues; a stronger similarity/performance/preference claim needs a suitable repeated blinded protocol.

Explicit sensory stop conditions: lavender no longer recognizable; medicinal/camphor dominance; excessive sugar; unwanted laundry/functional freshness; geranium/rose or white-floral takeover; dry amberwood scratching; a heavy base replacing the target; or a new material with no detectable target-linked function in the comparison.
