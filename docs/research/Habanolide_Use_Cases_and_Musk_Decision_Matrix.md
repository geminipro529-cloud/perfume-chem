## Bottom Line

`Habanolide` is your:
- premium clean halo
- metallic-mineral musk
- polished amber-musk
- crystalline luxury finish

It is **not** your:
- default musk
- fruity-skin musk
- warm powder musk
- vintage cosmetic musk
- soft intimate skin musk
- mass-clean laundry musk

If the brief is:
- `crystalline`
- `mineral`
- `metallic`
- `tailored`
- `amber-musk`
- `white-shirt luxury`

then `Habanolide` is a candidate.

If the brief is:
- `plum`
- `cassis`
- `powder`
- `lipstick`
- `retro`
- `warm skin`
- `mass clean`

then `Habanolide` should usually be replaced by something else from your shelf.

## What The Local Knowledge Says About `Habanolide`

From the knowledge graph:
- character: `musky, slightly animalic, metallic, elegant, powdery-warm`
- tenacity: `deep base`
- pairing rule: `Habanolide + Galaxolide + EB = complete musk spectrum`
- pairing rule: `Habanolide + Ambrox = sophisticated amber-musk base`
- use case: `elegant modern skin scent`

From your own iris docs:
- `Habanolide` often works as the main `clean halo`
- `Romandolide` softens it into `skin polish`
- `Ethylene Brassylate` adds `volume` and `softening`
- `Galaxolide` is better used as a small familiar bridge, not always the lead
- `Zenolide` is better when the goal is `transparent crystalline clean`

The real conclusion is:
- `Habanolide` is not wrong
- `Habanolide as default` is wrong

## Best Use Cases For `Habanolide`

### 1. Crystal iris

Use it when the perfume needs:
- cool polish
- expensive cleanliness
- a hard shiny edge under iris/violet materials

Best partners:
- `Zenolide`
- `Romandolide`
- `Iso E Super`
- `Hedione`
- `Rose Oxide`
- `Cedramber`

### 2. Modern amberwood

Use it when the perfume needs:
- modern amber-musk polish
- dry expensive projection
- less sweetness than `Galaxolide` or `Tonalide`

Best partners:
- `Ambrox Super`
- `Ambrofix`
- `Cedramber`
- `Cashmeran (20%)`
- `Iso E Super`

### 3. Citrus-mineral tailoring

Use it when the fragrance needs:
- bright but expensive structure
- a dry polished shirt-collar effect
- clean luxury rather than warm comfort

Best partners:
- `Bergamot FCF`
- `Petitgrain EO`
- `Neroli EO`
- `Rose Oxide`
- `Iso E Super`
- `Cedramber`

### 4. White floral with a clean expensive edge

Use it when the floral brief is:
- expensive
- polished
- department-store luxury
- not retro

Best partners:
- `Hedione`
- `Hexyl Salicylate`
- `Nympheal`
- `Lilyreal ND`
- `DBCA`

### 5. Saffron-glass / metallic luxury

Use it when the scent wants:
- metallic smoothness
- cool clean money
- not suede, not warm leather

Best partners:
- `Ethyl Safranate`
- `Ambrox Super`
- `Cedramber`
- `Rose Oxide`

### 6. Aldehydic polish

Use it when the surface needs:
- sheen
- cold elegance
- waxy metallic cleanliness

Best partners:
- `Aldehyde C11`
- `Aldehyde C12 MNA`
- `Musk Ketone (10%)`
- `Galaxolide`

## Wrong Use Cases For `Habanolide`

### 1. Plum / cassis / soft fruit skin

Problem:
- `Habanolide` pushes the center cleaner and more metallic
- fruit briefs usually want softness, diffusion, or warmth instead

Use instead:
- `Romandolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Zenolide`
- trace `Musk Ketone (10%)` if you want powder-shadow

### 2. Warm powder / lipstick / vintage

Problem:
- `Habanolide` cleans up the very thing that should stay warm and cosmetic

Use instead:
- `Musk Ketone (10%)`
- `Tonalide (10%)`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Galaxolide`

### 3. Soft intimate skin musk

Problem:
- `Habanolide` is too polished and external
- skin briefs usually need warmth, not shine

Use instead:
- `Exaltolide (10%)`
- `Macrolide (10%)`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`

### 4. Mass-clean floral

Problem:
- `Habanolide` can feel too niche-metallic for easy mass-clean work

Use instead:
- `Galaxolide`
- `Romandolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`

### 5. Plush powdery iris

Problem:
- `Habanolide` hardens the contour
- plush iris needs softness and body

Use instead:
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Musk Ketone (10%)`
- `Zenolide`
- `Romandolide`

## Dose Rules For `Habanolide`

These are practical working rules for your `30 mL / 25%` builds.

### Trace polish

- `20-80 uL`

Use for:
- a little lift
- an edge
- minor cleanup

### Support role

- `80-180 uL`

Use for:
- supporting a musk block
- crystal or mineral polish
- keeping it present but not dominant

### Real feature role

- `180-350 uL`

Use for:
- modern amber-musk
- crystal iris
- bright luxury white floral

### Hero musk

- `350 uL+`

Use only if the brief is explicitly:
- metallic clean
- luxury-mineral
- amberwood polish
- white-shirt chic

If the brief is not one of those, do not spend it here.

## What To Use Instead

### If the formula needs fruit-friendly diffusion

Use:
- `Romandolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Zenolide`

Why:
- `Romandolide` carries pear/plum/cassis friendliness
- `EB` adds body
- `Zenolide` keeps it transparent

### If the formula needs mass-clean familiarity

Use:
- `Galaxolide`
- `Romandolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`

Why:
- this is a safer commercial-clean direction than `Habanolide`

### If the formula needs warm powder

Use:
- `Musk Ketone (10%)`
- `Tonalide (10%)`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Galaxolide`

Why:
- these read warmer, softer, and more cosmetic

### If the formula needs transparent luxury

Use:
- `Zenolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Romandolide`

Why:
- this is the cleaner substitute for when you want polish without metallicity

### If the formula needs skin intimacy

Use:
- `Exaltolide (10%)`
- `Macrolide (10%)`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`

Why:
- these smell like skin warmth, not garment polish

## How This Applies To Your Orris + Damascone Work

### Use `Habanolide` only if the direction is:
- crystal iris
- saffron glass
- mineral amberwood
- cold polished white floral

### Do not default to `Habanolide` if the direction is:
- clean plum powder
- blackcurrant veil
- powder-plum luxury
- lipstick floral
- tea-powder
- skin scent

For most of the weird `orris + damascone` directions, the safer first musk choices are:
- `Romandolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Zenolide`
- `Musk Ketone (10%)` or `Tonalide (10%)` if powder or cosmetic warmth matters

That keeps the accord:
- soft
- deep
- strange
- readable

without hardening it into a metallic-clean shell.

## Policy Going Forward

Do **not** use `Habanolide` in a new formula unless at least one of these is explicitly true:

- the brief says `crystalline`
- the brief says `mineral`
- the brief says `metallic`
- the brief says `amber-musk`
- the brief says `tailored clean luxury`
- the formula already has enough warmth and needs polish

If none of those are true, start with:
- `Romandolide`
- `Ethylene Brassylate`
- `Zenolide`
- `Galaxolide`
- `Musk Ketone`
- `Tonalide`
- `Exaltolide`
- `Macrolide`

before you spend `Habanolide`.

That is the practical rule that prevents wasting it.
