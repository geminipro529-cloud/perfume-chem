# Lilac: identity and endpoint review before executable expansion

Date: 2026-10-07. Status: research note, not an activated capability or formula.

The existing LIGHT_LILAC card correctly separates moth responses from human
perception, but currently binds only the 2006 floral-chemistry/electrophysiology
paper. This review identifies additional primary human-olfactory and structure
work. It does not replace the immutable card or claim a complete lilac accord.

## Primary evidence and its limits

### Stereoisomers and human olfactometry

[Kreck and Mosandl, 2003](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jf021140q),
DOI 10.1021/jf021140q, distinguishes eight lilac-aldehyde stereoisomers and eight
lilac-alcohol stereoisomers using synthesis, NMR and enantioselective GC. Its
public abstract reports odor-threshold measurements. That supports tracking
stereochemical identity rather than one interchangeable "lilac chemical."

The numeric thresholds and complete sensory procedure were not available in
the reviewed abstract. No threshold, response curve, relative potency, perfume
dose or isomer ranking is extracted here. An exact commercial isomer mixture
would need its own identity binding before those data could be considered.

### Plant biosynthesis is not bottle maturation

[Kreck et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12517111/),
DOI 10.1021/jf020845p, investigated labeled precursors in Syringa vulgaris
flowers using in-vivo feeding and enantioselective multidimensional GC/MS.
This is evidence about living-flower biosynthesis. It does not establish that
adding linalool to an alcoholic perfume will generate a lilac accord during
maturation, or that a bottle reproduces those metabolic conditions.

### Floral occurrence and moth detection

[Dotterl et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16542668/),
DOI 10.1016/j.chroma.2006.02.011, examined floral volatiles from 15 plant
species and found species-dependent isomer patterns. Isolated lilac-aldehyde
stereoisomers were tested electrophysiologically on Hadena bicruris antennae.
These endpoints must stay separate: occurrence in flowers, isomer identity,
and insect response are not a human dose-response calibration or a preference
study. This is the source already bound by LIGHT_LILAC.

### Structural analogues can change the odor family

[Dacho and Szolcsanyi, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8658798/),
DOI 10.3390/molecules26237086, studied synthetic racemic ring-opened analogues.
Their qualitative assessments found shifts away from the parent floral
character toward spicy, green, fruity or herbal descriptions. The methods
describe author assessments on absorbent-paper strips at 22 degrees Celsius.
This is a small qualitative structure-odor investigation, not a consumer trial,
mixture optimization study or calibrated finished-perfume persistence test.

The practical inference is limited: structural resemblance alone is not enough
to call an analogue a faithful lilac replacement. It does not mean any of these
research compounds should be purchased, synthesized, or selected for compounding.

## Consequences for Perfume-Chem

1. Keep LIGHT_LILAC advisory until a role can bind the exact supported product
   or stereochemical mixture. A stock label containing "lilac" is insufficient.
2. Preserve aldehyde versus alcohol, stereoisomer versus mixture, supplier
   product versus pure molecule, and flower observation versus reconstruction.
3. Keep manufacturer-described substitutes as explicit reconstruction
   hypotheses. Do not silently equate a generic muguet, terpineol or floral
   base with the studied lilac molecules.
4. An eventual comparison should retain the same light-floral control and
   change only the identified lilac facet. Keep vegetal/seed-like, spicy,
   sweet-generic-floral and lost-flower-recognition outcomes as stop questions.
5. Use local reviewed allocation templates only for an explicitly heuristic
   formula. Paper prose, abundance and unsupported threshold ratios supply no
   numeric allocation authority.
6. Verify actual stock/role eligibility and physical allocation independently.
   A new label or a distinct role-plan hash alone does not establish a distinct
   material intervention, much less improved lilac recognition.

## Review and admission boundary

Reviewed material: indexed primary ACS/PubMed abstracts; indexed PMC methods
and conclusion excerpts for the 2021 study. Direct page requests also encountered
ACS 403, MDPI 429 and PubMed/PMC browser checks. No inaccessible paid full text
was claimed as read. The 2021 article is identified as open-access CC BY; this
note nevertheless contains original summaries and links only, not its dataset
or copied standards/publisher text.

DOI-specific correction/retraction searches and the retrieved primary metadata
did not surface a notice for these records. That bounded negative search is not
a guarantee that no notice exists. Before runtime admission, recheck publisher
status, scope and license and issue source-bound review receipts.

No local source record, subtype manifest, empirical calibration, threshold,
inventory row or formula was changed by this note. It must not count as four
new executable capabilities or as exhaustive lilac research.

Next admissible work: a versioned source-review/addendum successor, followed by
exact-identity role support and a separately frozen positive/negative diagnostic.
Until then, LIGHT_LILAC remains unmapped rather than falsely complete.

All release, safety, compounding and evidence-admission action flags remain false.
