## Lessons Learned

### 1. Structural Fixes > Enhancements
**F2 (structural failure, balance=26):** Base reduction + radiance → **+11.8 points**  
**F7 (already optimized, balance=88):** Aldehydes → **-4.6 points**

**Rule:** Fix broken structures before adding enhancements. Well-balanced formulas resist optimization and risk destabilization.

### 2. Geometric Mean = Weakness Intolerance
The scoring system's geometric mean composite means:
- **Fixing a critical weakness** (F2 balance 26→49) produces explosive gains
- **Creating a new weakness** (F7 balance 88→62) produces catastrophic losses
- **Enhancements in already-strong areas** (F7 radiance 60→67) don't compensate

### 3. Aldehyde Addition Threshold
Adding 1100µL of aldehydes (500µL C11 + 500µL C12 + 100µL DHM) works for:
- ✅ **Structurally broken formulas** (F2: balance failure fixed by base reduction)
- 🟡 **Moderate performers with headroom** (F1: gains offset by tradeoffs)
- ❌ **Already-optimized formulas** (F7: architectural disruption)

**Safe aldehyde dosing for well-balanced formulas:** 300-500µL total (not 1100µL)

### 4. Balance Score as Veto
Balance score distribution:
- F2 original: 26 → **critical failure**, any fix produces major gains
- F1 original: 75 → **acceptable**, enhancements have tradeoffs
- F7 original: 88 → **excellent**, ANY disruption tanks performance

**Optimization priority:** Target formulas with balance <50 first, avoid touching formulas with balance >80.

### 5. Character Radar ≠ Quality Predictor
F2 character balance **dropped** (88.8→78.7) but overall quality **improved** (56.5→68.3) because:
- Original: Mono-dimensional (warm 4.9 + sweet 3.2 = 80% of character)
- Optimized: Multi-dimensional (radiance 3.1 + freshness 1.9 + warmth 3.6 + floral 1.4)

**Implication:** High character balance can indicate either excellence (F7) or mono-dimensionality (F2 original). Context matters.
