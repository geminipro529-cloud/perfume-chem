## Strongest Fruit Families

### 1. Apricot / Stone Fruit

This is your best fruit family.

Why it is strong:

- `Apritone (10%)` already acts like a flexible fruit core.
- Your notes show it surviving multiple architectures: aldehydic, tea, white floral, berry, chypre, amber, jasmine, violet, spice, incense.
- It does not need much help to feel like a real perfume.

Best partners:

- `Cedrat FCF` for dry structure
- `Methyl Pamplemousse` for tart tea-green lift
- `Gamma Decalactone` for peach-skin body
- `Aurantiol` for blossom connection
- `Blackcurrant FTEC + Dewberry FTEC + Raspberry Ketone + Paradisamide` for orchard fruit
- `Anisaldehyde` for daring niche direction

Best proven patterns:

1. `Apritone + Cedrat FCF + Evernyl + Patchouli + Labdanum/Benzoin`
2. `Apritone + Methyl Pamplemousse + Helional + Hexyl Acetate + Verdox`
3. `Apritone + Blackcurrant FTEC + Dewberry FTEC + Raspberry Ketone + Paradisamide`
4. `Apritone + Gamma Decalactone + Delta Decalactone + Gamma Undecalactone + Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate`
5. `Apritone + Aurantiol + Neroli + Petitgrain`

## Mismatch Rules

These are the shelf mistakes worth remembering.

1. `Peonile` and cassis are not natural friends. Your own notes explicitly dropped `Peonile` because it clashed with cassis sulfur.
2. `Pear` and `apple` are accent lanes on this shelf, not headline lanes.
3. Too much `Verdox`, `Undecavertol`, `Calone`, or `Floralozone` can flatten dark fruit into shower-gel aquatic.
4. Too much `Raspberry Ketone` makes berry read cosmetic or candy-clean.
5. Too much `Paradisamide` turns fruit tropical-guava instead of keeping it faithful.
6. Too much lactone stack makes fruit read body lotion unless something dry or architectural frames it.
7. `Allyl Amyl Glycolate` is tropical-green sparkle, not real pear correction.
8. `Methyl Pamplemousse` is structure and dryness, not fruit pulp.

## Bridge Rules That Matter

1. Use `Aurantiol` when fruit needs to turn into blossom or clean floral skin.
2. Use `Paradisamide` when fruit disappears too fast.
3. Use `Gamma Decalactone` when fruit needs body, skin, or cream.
4. Use `Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate` when fruit needs a bright, immediate fresh snap.
5. Use `Cyclamen Aldehyde` when fruit needs watery polish instead of more sweetness.
6. Use `Helional` when fruit needs air, tea-leaf transparency, or sea-mist diffusion.
7. Use `Methyl Pamplemousse` when fruit needs dry tartness and structure.
8. Use `Beta Ionone` when berry needs perfume depth rather than flavor sweetness.
9. Use `Leafovert` when berry needs cassis leaf, not more fruit.
10. Use `Cedrat FCF` when soft fruit needs chypre or mineral dryness.

## Bottom Line

If you want to exploit the fruit side of your shelf properly:

1. Build around `Apritone`, `Blackcurrant FTEC`, `Blood Orange`, and the `lactone trio`.
2. Treat `Paradisamide`, `Aurantiol`, `Gamma Decalactone`, `Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate`, and `Helional` as your main fruit engineering tools.
3. Use `pear` and `apple` as accents, not as your main fruit identity.
4. Use `Leafovert`, `Cedrat`, `Grapefruit`, `Beta Ionone`, and `Evernyl` when you want fruit to feel expensive instead of edible.
5. Use `Verdox`, `Calone`, `Floralozone`, and `Undecavertol` carefully. They are useful, but they can sterilize fruit fast.
