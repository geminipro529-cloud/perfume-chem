## Next falsifiable hypothesis

After the two direct stock gates close, the highest-value low-cost experiment is a bounded concentration-aware binary-mixture benchmark. Quality, intensity, pleasantness, and component identifiability must be separate outcomes. The preregistered baselines are:

- linear component-average prediction for quality;
- strongest-component prediction for intensity;
- a nonlinear residual model only if a held-out comparison is still justified.

Support requires repeatable held-out gain without stock, carrier, session, or assessor leakage. The complexity hypothesis is refuted if the nonlinear model has no decision-relevant held-out gain, loses the gain across sessions, or only learns leakage. Physical execution stops if stock identity, carrier, matrix, concentration basis, or repeat reliability is unresolved.

This is deliberately narrower than claiming a universal mixture law. Recent preprints motivate both linear-quality and nonlinear-intensity baselines, while peer-reviewed studies show source-, pair-, matrix-, solvent-, and outcome-specific behavior: [PMID 42465471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465471/), [PMID 40832259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40832259/), [PMID 38876058](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38876058/), [PMID 28231131](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28231131/), [PMID 40005243](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40005243/), and [PMID 18534998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18534998/).
