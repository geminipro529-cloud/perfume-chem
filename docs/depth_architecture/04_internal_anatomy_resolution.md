# Volume 4 — Internal Anatomy and Resolution

**Status:** normative computational design theory  
**Sensory anatomy and realism:** `NOT TESTED`

## Construct

Internal anatomy is the organized set of facets that makes a named perfume
object feel materially and perceptually inhabited rather than merely labeled.
The term is deliberately broader than botanical anatomy. A flower may require
petal, flesh, pollen, nectar, stem, humidity, wax, spice, fruit, and shadow. A
wood may require grain, fibre, sapwood, heartwood, bark, root, resin, polish,
mineral seam, moisture, and char. Leather may require grain, nap, tannin, oil,
smoke, animal warmth, wear, and lining air. Citrus may require peel oil, zest,
pith, juice, pulp, leaf, blossom, acid, and oxidation edge. The exact anatomy
is determined by the target, not by a universal completeness checklist.

**Resolution** is the degree to which required facets and their boundaries are
purposefully represented, connected, and testable. It does not require that
every facet be separately nameable in one sniff. A coherent object can carry
internal distinctions as changes in texture, temperature, moisture, color
association, movement, or temporal emphasis. Resolution fails when the design
uses a family label as a substitute for anatomy, or when many rows all thicken
the same undifferentiated body.

The method distinguishes **macro-anatomy**, **meso-anatomy**, and
**microtexture**. Macro-anatomy defines the major object regions: flower and
stem, wood surface and interior, fruit skin and pulp, smoke source and plume.
Meso-anatomy defines subfacets that materially shape those regions: pollen,
wax, sap, tannin, salt, pith, ash. Microtexture consists of subtle polish,
grain, breath, mineral fleck, or tonal nuance whose contribution may be hard to
resolve without trained comparison. High-resolution targets can legitimately
use many rows, but microtexture cannot be allowed to inflate the formula while
macro-anatomy or interfaces remain missing.

An anatomy facet is admissible when five questions have answers: What exact
part of the named object does it represent? What material or module carries
it? Which other facets does it touch? What is lost when it is removed? Which
controlled comparison can detect that loss? A poetic adjective without these
bindings is a design intention, not an implemented facet.

Precise simplicity remains valid. A transparent lily abstraction may need
only a few exact facets. A DHP-scale floral-wood architecture may require many
independent petal, flesh, shadow, grain, enclosure, recurrence, and interface
functions. Anti-padding must not be used as a premature stopping rule. The
question is whether the declared target anatomy has closed, not whether an
arbitrary row count has been reached or avoided.

## Mechanisms

Anatomy is generated through **differentiation**, **continuity**, and
**hierarchy**. Differentiation gives adjacent regions distinct character:
petal versus flesh, skin versus pulp, grain versus cream, smoke versus ash.
Continuity lets those regions belong to one object through shared recognizers,
bridges, or gradual ratios. Hierarchy controls salience so a secondary facet
enriches without becoming a competing subject.

**Anatomical polarity** uses meaningful oppositions: cool shell and warm
interior, dry grain and oily polish, crisp skin and yielding pulp, clean suede
and animal shadow. The polarity creates depth only if both poles remain tied
to the named object. Random contrast produces collage.

**Anatomical recurrence** allows a facet to appear in different material
classes or time bands. A floral wax quality can echo in sandalwood cream; a
cool petal edge can echo in mineral wood; citrus pith can recur as dry tea or
root bitterness. An echo is valuable when it maintains identity across change,
not when it imposes the same generic chassis on every perfume.

**Selective suppression** and **release** can alter apparent anatomy. A strong
facet may obscure a weaker one; reducing it can reveal anatomy without adding
materials. Adaptation can also expose previously suppressed components. The
correct repair for missing detail is therefore not always another ingredient.
Ratio, volatility, and temporal salience must be examined first.

## Material translation

For a floral subject, naturals may provide an integrated botanical body while
known chemicals reinforce specific recognizers, modify texture, extend a
temporal band, or construct anatomy absent from the extract. The method does
not assume that a natural is complete or that a synthetic reconstruction is
inferior. It asks what the exact lot and formula actually need.

For woods, a large sandalwood or woody-amber block is not “thirty steps of
wood” merely because it contains many related materials. Rows must distribute
across grain, cream, dryness, root, polish, resin, smoke, mineral, warmth,
coolness, diffusion, persistence, and interfaces as required. Materials can
share a region while performing different functions, but each primary role
must remain unique.

For gourmands, anatomy prevents sweetness from becoming the whole perfume.
Crust, crumb, syrup, fat, roast, acid, salt-like contrast, steam, spice, bitter
residue, and non-edible bridge make an object. For aquatics, water body, air
boundary, salinity, mineral, vegetation, foam, temperature, and wet surface
replace the weak habit of treating one marine effect as complete anatomy.

Technical materials do not count as anatomy unless a physical comparison
shows that their delivery effect creates an intended observable facet. A
fixative may preserve a facet without being that facet. This distinction is
recorded in the row ledger.

## Interactions

Anatomy is relational. Petal without flesh may feel thin; flesh without edge
may feel cosmetic or amorphous. Grain without yielding body can be skeletal;
cream without grain can be bland. The contribution of one region therefore
depends on its neighbor and interface. Pairwise tests identify local
dependencies, while complete-object anti-collapse tests ensure that many
successful local accords still form one target.

Anatomical modules interact with temporal and spatial architecture. A facet
can be present chemically yet unavailable perceptually until another recedes.
A high-volatility cue can define the surface while a persistent cue carries
interior memory. Diffusion can export only one region, causing trail identity
to differ from close identity. These interactions must be documented rather
than assumed from note pyramids.

Hedonic effects are also anatomy-specific. Cream may increase comfort but
reduce grain; indolic shadow may increase floral flesh but lower liking for
some assessors; bitterness may clarify fruit skin while making the whole less
immediately pleasant. Endpoint separation is essential.

## Failure modes

**Checklist anatomy** adds every possible facet regardless of the target.
**Label anatomy** assigns petal, flesh, and pollen names without distinct
materials or omission losses. **Single-material anatomy** assumes one natural
or base automatically supplies every required region. **Decorative anatomy**
adds details that produce no repeatable difference. **Chassis anatomy** uses
the same sweet-musky-woody body under every subject.

**Overresolved collage** occurs when facets become separate mini-perfumes.
**Underresolved shorthand** occurs when a complex named target is represented
by one recognizer plus generic volume. **Microtexture inversion** occurs when
subtle rows consume attention and formula space while major anatomy is absent.
**Externalized depth** occurs when wood, amber, musk, darkness, or persistence
is declared to supply all depth for a flower or fruit whose internal anatomy
remains flat.

## Observables

Create an anatomy ledger for every lead and co-lead. Record required region,
target description, owner, material bindings, temporal windows, texture axis,
interfaces, omission loss, failure mode, probe, and evidence state. Record
optional facets separately from required ones. A missing required facet causes
a hold; an omitted optional facet does not become a deficiency later unless
the target changes and is versioned.

Physical evaluation records facet presence, clarity, integration, relative
salience, realism subtype, and adverse drift at defined time windows. For each
facet, record whether assessors can perceive the intended effect, whether they
name the same target region, and whether the complete object remains coherent.
Do not force assessors to identify formula materials.

## Controlled tests

Begin with region-level omissions. Compare complete subject anatomy against a
petal omission, flesh omission, grain omission, skin omission, or other
target-critical region while matching carrier and total raw volume. If several
materials form one inseparable region, remove the module rather than pretending
each row can be independently judged on the first pass.

Next perform within-region refinement: material ablations, substitutions, and
ratio sweeps. Test interfaces by comparing both regions separately, the full
combination without bridge, and the bridged combination. For high-resolution
systems, use staged screening: macro-anatomy first, meso-anatomy second,
microtexture last. Freeze accepted earlier stages while testing later ones.

The anti-collapse arm compares the complete architecture with strategically
reduced variants. Record identity, hierarchy, texture, temporal continuity,
genericization, and liking separately. Reject details that do not survive
repeat testing or that damage a more important target invariant.

## Claim boundary

An anatomy contract demonstrates that the design has represented and made its
required functions testable. It does not demonstrate that the functions are
perceived, realistic, beautiful, or sufficiently resolved. Material presence,
natural provenance, OAV, and formula count cannot close that gap. All sensory
anatomy and realism outcomes remain `NOT TESTED` without formula-bound physical
evidence.
