# Volume 15 — Experimental Validation

**Status:** controlled-test doctrine; physical sensory outcomes are `NOT TESTED`

## Construct

Experimental validation converts a target-linked depth mechanism into a
falsifiable comparison while preserving formula identity, total dose, sample
provenance, blinding, time, and claim boundaries. The purpose is not to certify
that a perfume is deep in one step. It is to determine whether specific
mechanisms—an anatomy facet, bridge, contrast, texture relation, transition,
spatial organization, or hedonic path—produce their declared effects under the
tested conditions.

The unit of inference is the exact formula-protocol-sample-assessor-endpoint
combination. A result does not automatically transfer across concentration,
carrier, substrate, maturation period, climate, batch, application method,
assessor population, or perfume target. A computationally complete experiment
design remains `NOT TESTED` until authorized physical compounding and observed
data exist. This volume authorizes neither procurement nor compounding.

Validation is a ladder rather than a single gate. Begin with deterministic
formula and stock validation. Continue through physical preparation controls,
mechanism isolation, temporal and spatial measurement, blinded sensory work,
hedonic preference, repeatability, and only then a bounded inference. Any
earlier failure stops promotion but does not require pretending the design is
bad. It may simply be untestable with the current evidence or inventory.

No universal sample size is installed here. Pilot variance, endpoint scale,
assessor heterogeneity, effect size of practical interest, repeated-measures
structure, multiplicity, and available physical resources determine a
prospective analysis. A small exploratory trial can reveal catastrophic
collapse or refine a ratio, but it cannot silently become confirmatory evidence.

## Mechanisms

### Freeze and provenance

Freeze the named target, ideal formula reference, current-inventory formula,
formula hash, stock ledger, dilution recipes, carrier, concentration, batch,
sample code, preparation date, maturation interval, application volume,
substrate, and evaluation windows before unblinding. Record every deviation.
If an arm is remade, give it a new sample lineage; do not overwrite the old
receipt. Separate the person preparing samples from the assessor when possible.

### Stock and measurement admission

Verify every material against live inventory and authoritative stock strength.
Rebase active dose when a stock changes. Depleted materials, lost tinctures,
unknown naturals, and supplier substitutions require explicit resolution. Any
raw-stock dose below ten microlitres must be converted into a practical
secondary dilution whose solvent, source stock, preparation volume, resulting
strength, mixing method, label, and used volume are specified. Rounding a
sub-measurable dose to ten microlitres changes the experiment and is not an
acceptable convenience.

### Constant-total mechanism isolation

The default causal geometry holds concentrate total, fragrance concentration,
carrier, application amount, maturation, and all non-target materials constant.
An ablation arm removes one mechanism and replaces its active volume with the
declared neutral carrier or a validated control. A substitution arm exchanges
one implementation while preserving target function and total. A ratio sweep
moves one relational axis through predeclared levels while keeping the pair or
module total fixed. Constant total does not guarantee sensory intensity
matching, so intensity must be measured separately rather than assumed.

### Recombination and interaction

When depth is attributed to a relationship rather than a single component,
use recombination. Test neither module, module A alone, module B alone, and
A+B under equal totals where physically meaningful. A factorial or fractional
factorial design may isolate multiple factors, but interpretation must respect
nonlinear and higher-order interactions. Pairwise success does not prove that a
larger mixture retains the interaction. Include the complete formula and the
appropriate carrier control.

### Temporal architecture

Use at least five target-relevant windows spanning opening, early transition,
heart, late heart, and drydown; add windows when the target contains rapid
handoffs or long recurrence. Window count alone does not validate temporal
depth. Predeclare the expected state, retained recognizer, transition event,
failure mode, and endpoint at each window. Separate chronological change from
olfactory adaptation by using balanced assessor groups, fresh blotters or
application sites, controlled breaks, sniff-duration limits, and where possible
independent-window assessment. Record when the same assessor repeatedly samples
one strip, because apparent flattening may reflect fatigue rather than formula.

Maturation is a separate temporal axis. If post-compound aging is relevant,
compare independently coded samples from predeclared maturation intervals made
from the same admitted stocks and procedure. Do not use a young reference
against an aged candidate. A maturation result concerns the tested sample and
interval; it does not establish shelf stability.

### Concentration and substrate

Dense mixtures can change configural character across concentration. A
dilution-series protocol should therefore compare at least the intended wearing
concentration with predeclared lower or higher levels when concentration
dependence is a material risk. Hold formula proportions constant and treat each
concentration as a separate condition. Do not interpret stronger as deeper.
Record whether anatomy, recognizers, texture, transition, and collapse change.

Skin is neither a universal truth substrate nor a nuisance to ignore. It can
modify evaporation, warmth, adsorption, moisture, and perceived texture. Begin
mechanism isolation on a controlled substrate when appropriate, then use skin
only with explicit authorization, safety clearance, standardized application,
site balancing, and assessor-specific coding. Report blotter and skin results
separately. Skin chemistry may participate in the percept but must not be
invented as an explanation without data.

### Hedonic evidence

Hedonic valence, hedonic complexity, desire to re-smell, comfort, fascination,
tension, relief, satiation, familiarity, novelty, and overall preference are
separate endpoints. A challenging or ambivalent perfume can possess deliberate
hedonic architecture without high immediate pleasantness. A universally liked
perfume can be hedonically simple. Collect ties rather than forcing choices,
and preserve individual responses before summarizing. Do not allow depth or
complexity questions to contaminate the primary liking task.

## Material translation

Every experiment requires a preparation worksheet that converts the ideal
mechanism into the current physical stocks. For each material record canonical
name, supplier or batch when known, natural versus known chemical status,
nominal stock concentration, solvent, density assumption if volume is converted
to mass, target active amount, raw volume, preparation precision, and formula
position. Naturals require composite treatment in computational diagnostics;
their batch variability remains a physical uncertainty.

Prepare secondary dilutions at a scale large enough to measure accurately and
to support all blinded arms plus reserve. For example, if a design needs one
microlitre of neat-equivalent material but the source stock is not directly
measurable below ten microlitres, one may prepare a one-percent secondary stock
by combining ten microlitres of the admitted neat source with nine hundred
ninety microlitres of the chosen carrier, mix and label it, then dose one
hundred microlitres of that secondary stock to deliver one microlitre neat
equivalent. This is an illustrative calculation, not authorization for a
specific formula; the actual dilution must be selected so both preparation and
dosing volumes are practical and totals remain constant.

Stock rebasing precedes OAV interpretation. If a formula row described as ten
percent is actually neat, a thirty-microlitre raw dose changes tenfold in active
amount. The correct response is to reconcile source authority, regenerate the
active-dose ledger, rerun the duplicate-ODT and data-spine checks, and invalidate
downstream comparisons tied to the wrong state. Do not tune another ingredient
to compensate for an accounting error.

For missing chemicals, state whether the ideal mechanism is fully represented,
approximated, or absent in the current build. Test the approximation against a
control; do not assume the same note label means the same texture or topology.
Procurement recommendations remain separate from approval and should be ranked
by target-specific causal impact, not prestige or novelty.

## Interactions

Experiment design couples all depth dimensions. An anatomy ablation can also
change intensity, family classification, texture, temporal shape, and liking.
The score sheet must therefore identify a primary endpoint and anticipated
secondary effects. A bridge trial can expose nonlinear masking; a texture
change can alter diffusion; a shadow ratio can change hedonic valence and
identity simultaneously. These interactions are reasons to measure dimensions
separately, not reasons to compress them into one score.

Physical chemistry constrains interpretation. ppm, ODT, OAV, volatility,
activity coefficients, molecular fraction, composite-natural constituents,
and modeled headspace can identify implausibility, dominance risk, or likely
sampling windows. They cannot prove what assessors experience. Analytical
headspace, when actually measured, is stronger physical evidence but still does
not establish pleasure, depth, realism, or projection by itself.

Saturation and negative space require target-aware testing. A high-density
perfume should not be forced to become transparent to pass. Instead test whether
small ratio or omission changes reveal differentiated regions while the full
arm preserves desired enclosure. If every change produces the same generic
cloud, density may be masking architecture. If distinctions are recoverable and
the target calls for saturation, low negative space is not a defect.

Family migration interacts with temporal testing. For an authorized migration,
measure departure identity, bridge, arrival, retained invariants, and hierarchy
at each window. For a stable-family target, the same observations diagnose
drift. The data are identical in form; the locked target changes their meaning.

## Failure modes

Uncontrolled total is the most common causal failure: an addition arm simply
contains more perfume. Other preparation failures include wrong stock strength,
sub-measurable raw dosing, silent rounding, unequal carrier, different aging,
unrecorded supplier substitution, mislabeled samples, natural batch mismatch,
and comparing different application amounts. Any one can mimic or erase an
effect.

Sensory failures include visible coding, fixed order, reference always first,
insufficient washout, adaptation, repeated sniffing without limits, assessor
discussion, forced choice without ties, and revealing formula length or
prestige. Endpoint contamination occurs when assessors are asked to like the
deeper or more complex sample rather than simply choose preference. A single
observed sequence is not proof of order balance.

Analytical failures include treating modeled headspace as measured, using
monomolecular OAV for a natural mixture, accepting duplicate or conflicting
ODT entries, ignoring activity coefficient assumptions, promoting OAV above
its claim ceiling, and interpreting persistence as temporal architecture.
Statistical failures include choosing endpoints after unblinding, treating
assessors as independent when observations are repeated, deleting ties,
averaging discordant duplicate cells, and turning an exploratory result into a
confirmatory claim.

Architectural failures include count-only justification, testing multiple
mechanisms at once, using a generic base as the control for every target,
mistaking family correctness for depth, equating pleasantness with hedonic
complexity, and penalizing saturated designs for low negative space. A single
sample cannot prove nonlinear interaction or spatial organization because the
required counterfactual is absent. In that case the required result is
abstention, not success or failure.

## Observables

Deterministic observables include hashes, formula rows, active and raw dose,
stock strength, dilution lineage, sample totals, preparation timestamps,
randomization schedule, code mapping, and protocol version. Physical
observables include sample mass, concentration, temperature, humidity,
substrate, maturation interval, application amount, measured headspace when a
method exists, and analytical uncertainty. Computational observables include
model version, material properties, activity coefficients, ODT source, OAV,
and simulated temporal values with explicit assumptions.

Sensory observables include named-object identification, recognizer retention,
anatomy discrimination, bridge legibility, texture descriptors, state and
transition labels, spatial judgments, intensity, collapse flags, and confidence
at each window. Hedonic observables include preference with ties, pleasantness,
desire to re-smell, comfort, fascination, tension, relief, fatigue, and
satiation. Record assessor-level data and relevant anosmia or non-detection;
do not infer missing values.

Primary endpoints must be chosen before evaluation. Secondary and exploratory
endpoints remain labeled. A mechanism passes only its predeclared acceptance
rule and only under the tested conditions. A failure may support removal,
ratio revision, or redesign, but it does not prove the entire perfume shallow.
An ambiguous or underpowered result remains unresolved.

## Controlled tests

### Stage 0 — computational and provenance admission

Confirm target lock, formula hashes, ideal/current separation, live inventory,
depleted-stock exclusions, no lost tinctures, physical data coverage, duplicate
ODT resolution, composite-natural handling, stock rebasing, OAV diagnostics,
sub-ten-microlitre dilution recipes, and claim ceiling. Reject any mismatch
before samples are coded.

### Stage 1 — carrier and preparation control

Prepare equal-total carrier controls and replicate samples from the same stock
lineage. Verify coding, mass or volume tolerance, maturation interval, and
application procedure. A preparer should retain the key while assessors and the
analyst remain blinded where practical.

### Stage 2 — single-mechanism ablation

Compare full versus mechanism-removed arms at constant total. The acceptance
rule must name the expected target-linked loss and the forbidden collateral
change. Example structure: removing the pollen bridge should reduce perceived
pollen-to-petal continuity at the declared heart window without simply making
the sample weaker or changing the named flower. The wording is a protocol
pattern, not a sensory prediction.

### Stage 3 — ratio sweep

Test a small predeclared sequence around the design ratio while holding module
total constant. Include the current ratio as an anonymously coded arm. Evaluate
identity, anatomy, texture, collision, intensity, and liking separately. A
monotonic increase is not required; the target may have a bounded optimum or a
phase transition.

### Stage 4 — recombination and higher-order interaction

Use neither/A/B/A+B geometry or an appropriate factorial design. Ask whether
the complete relation produces an effect not attributable to either module
alone, whether masking appears, and whether the named object survives. When a
third module is theoretically essential, test the higher-order combination or
leave the claim unresolved; pairwise evidence is insufficient.

### Stage 5 — anti-collapse perturbation

Hold the generic chassis while removing target-specific anatomy, or hold the
anatomy while weakening a suspected chassis. Introduce bounded concentration
and ratio perturbations. A robust design should not depend on exact numerical
fragility unless fragility is part of the target. This stage distinguishes
real resolution from padding and genericization without imposing a minimum row
count.

### Stage 6 — temporal and adaptation protocol

Collect fixed-window observations with counterbalanced order and separate
application sites or fresh media where possible. Use Temporal Check-All-That-
Apply, Temporal Dominance of Sensations, or a predeclared fixed-window method
according to the endpoint. Limit sniff duration, schedule breaks, and compare a
continuous-observation panel with independent-window groups when adaptation is
a serious alternative explanation. Test concentration and maturation series
separately rather than adding them as uncontrolled factors.

### Stage 7 — texture and materiality

Use target-specific bipolar or categorical texture terms defined before the
trial, such as dry/yielding, waxy/watery, polished/fibrous, powdered/humid, or
cool/warm. Include intensity as a covariate or separate rating, because stronger
samples can be judged rougher or denser without a true materiality change. Ask
for object-linked texture, not free association alone.

### Stage 8 — spatial and performance protocol

Separate proximity character, halo, trail, room presence, and persistence.
Use standardized application, distance, air movement, room, temperature, and
observer position. Multiple observers or instrumented physical measures may be
needed. Formula models cannot substitute. If compounding or spatial measurement
is not authorized, the correct state is abstention and `NOT TESTED`.

### Stage 9 — hedonic preference

Run blinded constant-total paired or multi-arm work with balanced order and
ties. Keep the primary liking question isolated from explanatory depth,
complexity, realism, and performance questions. Collect reasons after the
choice, not before. Analyze assessor heterogeneity rather than deleting people
who disagree. Hedonic complexity and valence receive separate endpoints.

### Stage 10 — confirmation and repeatability

Freeze the selected arm and rules before a new session. Use a new randomization
and, when feasible, new sample preparation or batch. Confirm only the mechanism
and conditions supported by the pilot. A failure to repeat lowers the evidence
state; it is not repaired by pooling incompatible sessions without a declared
model.

Stop rules include stock or sample identity conflict, unequal total beyond the
declared tolerance, broken blinding, duplicated canonical cells, incomplete
pair totals, uncontrolled substitution, safety hold, severe assessor fatigue,
or protocol deviation that can affect the endpoint. Preserve all receipts and
report the stop rather than improvising.

## Claim boundary

Passing Stages 0 and 1 establishes only that the experiment is admissible to
run. Passing a controlled mechanism test supports that mechanism for the exact
formula, comparison, sample, protocol, population, and conditions. It does not
prove universal depth, overall superiority, DHP or Opus V similarity, market
success, safety, stability, projection, longevity, or release readiness.

OAV and temporal models remain constraints and hypothesis generators. Measured
headspace remains physical evidence. Sensory ratings remain protocol-bound
observations. Hedonic results remain population- and context-bound. Any domain
without admitted evidence remains `NOT TESTED`, including when the design is
computationally complete. Procurement, compounding, skin application, safety
clearance, and release require separate explicit authority.
