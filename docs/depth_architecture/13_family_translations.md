# Volume 13 — Family Translations

**Status:** universal design doctrine; family-specific sensory depth is `NOT TESTED`

## Construct

A perfume family is not a depth score and not a prewritten formula. It is a
target-dependent vocabulary for deciding which object, internal anatomy,
relations, textures, transitions, spaces, and pleasures matter. Translation is
therefore the act of carrying the universal depth constructs into the logic of
a named family without installing ingredient defaults. The same universal
questions apply everywhere—what is the object, what is inside it, how do its
parts relate, what changes, what remains invariant, what creates relief, and
what evidence could disprove the account—but the admissible answers differ.

Family membership may be stable, hierarchical, fused, juxtaposed, or
temporally migratory. A citrus-to-incense-to-amber trajectory is not defective
because its dominant family changes. It is defective only if the target did
not authorize that migration, if the handoffs are unintelligible, or if one
generic chassis replaces the named states. Conversely, a perfume announced as
a magnolia soliflore cannot excuse an unrelated woody-amber drydown by calling
it a hybrid after the fact. The target lock decides whether migration is
architecture or drift.

Translation also prevents floral bias. Petal, pollen, stem, and nectar are not
universal slots. Wood may require grain, cut, sap, polish, root, and mineral
seam; leather may require grain, nap, tannin, oil, wear, and smoke; mineral work
may require temperature, porosity, dust, wetness, metal, and air. Each anatomy
must belong to its named object. Ingredient count, darkness, sweetness,
persistence, and family prestige remain inadmissible substitutes for this
object-specific resolution.

## Mechanisms

The following eighteen translations are complete design lenses, not recipes.
For each family, the mechanism statement identifies a depth-bearing structure
and the corresponding collapse risk.

### Floral

Lock the named flower or bouquet hierarchy, its realism or abstraction target,
and the anatomy that makes that organism specific. Petal surface, fleshy
interior, pollen, nectar, wax, stem, leaf, humidity, diffusion, and shadow are
available questions only when they serve the flower. Depth can come from the
coherence between recognizers and anatomy, the handoff from volatile lift to
living flesh, recurring green relief, and a base that echoes the flower rather
than replacing it. Collapse occurs when sweetness, salicylate, musk, or wood
becomes the sole depth signal and the subject turns into a generic floral mass.

### Woody

Name the object: living tree, freshly cut timber, polished furniture, root,
forest enclosure, charred beam, or abstract wooden volume. Resolve grain,
fibre, sapwood, heartwood, bark, root, resin seam, moisture, temperature,
mineral contact, and polish selectively. Depth arises through surface/interior
contrast, grain-to-body continuity, root-to-air enclosure, and temporal change
from cut surface to warmed body. Collapse is the uniform woody-amber wall or a
creamy sandal effect that erases grain, species-like character, and air.

### Amber and resinous

Specify glowing resin, balsamic skin, incense amber, mineral amber, or abstract
heat. Resin body, balsamic softness, dryness, radiance, char, spice, leather,
mineral glow, and vanilla-like warmth may form strata. Depth depends on how dry
and soft regions interlock, how brightness opens the object before closure,
and how smoke or bitterness regulates satiation. Persistent sweetness alone is
not amber depth. A monolithic syrup or generic amberwood projection is a
collapse even when it is powerful.

### Leather and suede

Distinguish raw hide, polished leather, suede nap, glove, saddle, worn jacket,
or abstract skin. Grain, fibre, oil, tannin, smoke, animal warmth, polish, and
worn air must form a material object. Tannic edge against yielding body, clean
surface against controlled animalic shadow, and polish-to-warm-skin transition
can create depth. Smoke alone, quinoline caricature, medicinal dirt, or cosmetic
powder standing in for suede fails the translation.

### Chypre

Define the particular tension among bright lift, floral or fruity body,
labdanum-like warmth, dry mossy shadow, patchouli-like earth, wood, bitterness,
and skin. The mechanism is not a fixed list but a controlled rise and fall of
light, body, and dry shadow while the named era or style remains legible.
Negative space around the central body is often useful but is not mandatory.
A saturated modern chypre may be valid if its internal boundaries survive.
Collapse occurs when citrus vanishes into a generic woody floral or when a
mossy-dark base is mistaken for the entire architecture.

### Fougere

Name barbershop, fern fantasy, aromatic field, soft fougere, dark fougere, or
another precise object. Aromatic lift, cool green tissue, floral bridge, hay or
coumarinic warmth, mossy floor, wood, and clean or animalic skin can establish
vertical structure. The key mechanism is legible cool-to-warm and air-to-floor
movement with recurrent aromatic identity. Laundry freshness over sweet wood,
or sweetness that buries the fern illusion, is collapse.

### Gourmand

Separate edible reference from perfume abstraction: pastry crust, chocolate,
roasted nut, milk, fruit preserve, spice confection, burnt sugar, or imagined
food. Resolve crust, crumb, cream, glaze, roast, bitterness, salt, steam, char,
and utensil or room context as target permits. Hedonic depth often depends on
appetite tension—sweetness answered by roast, salt, dryness, acidity, or char—
and on a temporal transformation rather than one sweet block. Sweet amber plus
vanilla naming itself as food is not sufficient.

### Citrus and hesperidic

Name peel, expressed oil, pulp, pith, leaf, blossom, cologne abstraction, or a
specific citrus object. Sparkling peel, bitter pith, juicy interior, waxy oil,
green leaf, floral trace, and cool air can create internal resolution. Because
volatility and adaptation can make citrus appear simpler than its construction,
temporal identity retention requires special controls. A persistent limonene
pool, brightness, or longevity is not depth by itself. Collapse occurs when
the opening is vivid but leaves no target-linked echo, or when fixatives distort
the fruit into detergent or generic wood.

### Aromatic and herbal

Specify crushed leaf, garden herb, medicinal tincture, dry aromatic bundle,
camphoraceous air, culinary stem, or abstract freshness. Leaf surface, bruised
sap, stem, flower, seed, soil, bitterness, cool vapour, and warm dried tissue
can be related. Depth may arise from the contrast between volatile coolness and
the warmer plant body, and from transformations from fresh crush to dry herb.
Collapse is a top-note cloud that disappears into an unrelated base or a
medicinal harshness misread as complexity.

### Green

Name cut stem, leaf, sap, bud, galbanum-like resin, crushed grass, humid garden,
or abstract green geometry. Resolve watery fracture, fibrous stem, bitter sap,
waxy leaf, soil, shade, sunlit air, and fruit or flower adjacency selectively.
The principal relations are wet/dry, bright/shaded, tender/fibrous, and
volatile/persistent. A single sharp green spike or cosmetic violet-leaf effect
cannot represent the whole object. Excess bitterness, detergent freshness, or
a total loss of living moisture marks collapse.

### Fruity

Identify skin, flesh, juice, seed, stone, stem, bloom, ripeness, fermentation,
or cooked transformation for a named fruit. Fruit depth depends on internal
anatomy and controlled ripeness, not generic ester density. Tartness and
sweetness, skin and flesh, juicy release and dry seed, or fresh and fermented
states can support one coherent fruit object. Collapse occurs when candy,
shampoo, tropical ester soup, or woody sweetness replaces the named fruit.

### Aquatic and ozonic

Define sea air, mineral water, wet skin, rain, river, cold mist, humid room,
ozonic abstraction, or another exact environment. Water itself is not an odor
material; the object is built through temperature, salt, mineral, vegetation,
air, skin, wet surface, transparency, and motion cues. Spatial and temporal
relations are central. One powerful marine or ozonic signal over clean musk is
an icon, not necessarily depth. Metallic harshness, melon detergent, or a
uniform blue cloud are common collapses.

### Mineral and earth

Name wet stone, dry dust, clay, soil, chalk, metal, concrete, cave, rain on
ground, or an abstract mineral body. Porosity, temperature, moisture, salt,
dust, root, metal edge, moss, air pocket, and pressure can form anatomy.
Depth may arise from contact interfaces—water entering porous stone, root
crossing soil, warm skin against cold mineral—and from slow state change.
Smoke, patchouli, or metallic aroma alone cannot stand for geology. Muddy
undifferentiation and harsh metal are collapse modes.

### Musk and skin

Specify clean fabric, warm skin, salty body, powder, animal trace, humid skin,
or abstract softness. Treat musks as perceptually heterogeneous and potentially
anosmia-prone rather than as interchangeable fixatives. Skin depth may involve
temperature, salt, sebum-like warmth, powder, fabric, hair, animal trace,
proximity, and changing substrate interaction. A redundant laundry cloud is
not depth, and apparent absence in one assessor is not proof of structural
absence. One exact musk may outperform a chord; multiple musks require distinct
target-linked roles and assessor controls.

### Incense and smoke

Name resin source, cold incense, burning wood, sacred chamber, ash, industrial
smoke, or a precise ritual space. Source, ember, plume, ash, char, cool air,
wood, mineral chamber, spice, and cloth residue can create a causal sequence.
Depth depends on distinguishing source, combustion, space, and residue while
retaining the intended meaning. A uniform smoky amber, tar, phenol, or burnt
rubber effect that erases the source is collapse.

### Tobacco and hay

Distinguish green leaf, cured tobacco, cigar box, pipe chamber, hay field,
fermentation, smoke, and ash. Leaf lamina, vein, dry fibre, cured sugar,
tannin, hay, spice, wood box, leather, fermentation, smoke, and ash can form a
process object. Depth comes from curing and use transformations, and from the
tension between dry fibre and fermented sweetness. Vanilla-amber called
tobacco, or stale ash without leaf anatomy, fails.

### Aldehydic and abstract

Name the abstract object or perceptual event: light, fabric, powder, cosmetic
body, motion, metallic air, or another construct. Resolve high-energy flash,
air gap, waxy or floral body, soap, powder, fabric, skin, and soft base where
relevant. The discontinuity must reveal a coherent body rather than merely sit
above it. Harsh flash over an unrelated base, or soap and powder erasing the
named abstraction, is collapse.

### Hybrid and multifamily

Declare the lead family, supporting families, hierarchy, and mode: fusion,
juxtaposition, transformation, or sequential migration. Interfaces and handoffs
must explain why the modules belong together. A temporal migration should be
evaluated as a trajectory with named departure, bridge, arrival, retained
invariants, and forbidden inversions. It must not be penalized for changing
family if change is the target. Collapse occurs when a shared diffusive chassis
homogenizes every family, when hierarchy accidentally inverts, or when the
wearer encounters unrelated perfumes in sequence.

## Material translation

Translation proceeds from function to candidate, never from category to
default. For each target-specific anatomical or relational need, enumerate the
available naturals and known chemicals, record stock strength and physical
availability, then test the smallest set able to express the distinction.
Naturals are composite systems and must use constituent-aware evidence where
available. A material can serve multiple relations, but its roles must not be
double-counted as independent evidence. A family name does not waive ppm, ODT,
OAV, stock-rebase, sub-10 microlitre preparation, safety, or inventory gates.

The translation ledger should contain the target function, ideal candidate,
current-inventory binding, missing-material impact, expected omission loss,
collision risk, temporal windows, and controlled probe. If the current
inventory cannot express a defining anatomy, the result is a bounded gap, not
permission to relabel the target. Conversely, an unavailable traditional
material is not automatically essential when available materials can satisfy
the exact mechanism through a different chemistry.

## Interactions

Family logic interacts with every other volume. Identity determines which
family cues are invariant. Anatomy identifies the parts. Topology determines
bridges and hierarchy. Contrast defines whether saturation, openness, shadow,
or omission serves the target. Texture prevents note names from standing in
for materiality. Temporal architecture decides whether family migration is
valid. Spatial architecture determines planes and enclosure. Hedonic
architecture distinguishes pleasure from complexity and permits ambivalent or
challenging targets. Nonlinear mixture behavior tests whether modules merge,
mask, or genericize. Physical chemistry constrains what can plausibly become
available without making a sensory conclusion.

Low negative space is not a universal defect. Some targets demand saturated,
enclosed, high-contact architecture. Such a design must still demonstrate
internal boundaries, controlled density, identity retention, and recoverability
under ablation or ratio change. Likewise, transparency is not automatically
more refined. The question is whether saturation or transparency performs the
named object's work.

## Failure modes

The first failure is family essentialism: treating every floral as petals,
every wood as Iso-E-like diffusion, every amber as sweetness, or every leather
as smoke. The second is recipe translation, where a conventional ingredient
list replaces causal architecture. The third is taxonomic policing, where an
intended family migration is penalized merely for moving. The fourth is
after-the-fact relabeling, where accidental drift is called a hybrid to avoid a
failed identity gate. The fifth is scalar prestige, where dark, dense,
persistent, expensive, natural, or ingredient-rich families are presumed
deeper. The sixth is chassis capture: different families become variations on
one diffusive woody, musky, sweet, or amber structure.

Other failures include confusing classification confidence with depth,
mistaking one recognizer for complete anatomy, forcing negative space into a
saturated target, ignoring assessor-specific musk or sulfur perception,
allowing the support family to invert hierarchy, and treating family-specific
performance stereotypes as measured facts.

## Observables

Admissible observables include target identity choices, declared invariants,
family hierarchy, anatomy labels, topology edges, texture contrasts, temporal
state descriptions, migration trajectories, spatial hypotheses, and explicit
collapse endpoints. Physical observables include exact stock strengths,
concentration, composite-natural decomposition, modeled or measured headspace
with its method, and formula hashes. Sensory observables require blinded
responses bound to formula and protocol: identity retention, anatomy
discrimination, transition recognition, texture ratings, family hierarchy,
preference, and rejection modes.

No observable has universal directionality. More saturation, more darkness,
more projection, more recognizers, more families, or longer persistence can be
right or wrong. Results are interpreted against the locked target and kept
dimension-wise. Missing evidence produces abstention or `NOT TESTED`, not an
imputed zero and not a favorable assumption.

## Controlled tests

For a stable single-family target, compare full formula against isolated
omission or constant-total substitution arms for the defining anatomy and
interfaces. For a hybrid, test lead/support hierarchy separately from the
bridge, then test the full recombination. For an intended migration, collect
responses at fixed windows and ask for departure identity, bridge legibility,
arrival identity, retained invariants, and accidental inversion. Include an
anti-collapse arm in which the shared chassis is held while target-specific
interfaces are removed.

For a saturated low-negative-space target, do not compare it against an open
version as though openness were inherently superior. Compare whether each arm
retains the named object, differentiable anatomy, controlled texture, and
desired enclosure. Use an intensity-matched or constant-total design when
possible. Separate hedonic valence, desire to re-smell, coherence, perceived
complexity, and named-object fidelity. Balance order, control adaptation and
carryover, record assessor-specific non-detection, and predeclare abstention
when no valid physical comparison exists.

## Claim boundary

Family translation establishes a complete map of questions and falsifiable
mechanisms. It does not establish that a formula belongs to the family, smells
deep, resembles a reference, is realistic, is liked, projects, lasts, is safe,
or is stable. Those claims remain `NOT TESTED` until exact physical evidence is
admitted. Passing a template means only that the design did not omit the
family-specific questions required by its own target. It does not award a
family, a rank, or a universal score.
