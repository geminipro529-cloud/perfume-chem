# Volume 5 — Relational Topology

**Status:** normative computational design theory  
**Sensory interaction and depth:** `NOT TESTED`

## Construct

Relational topology describes how perfume functions depend on one another. It
is the difference between a list of notes and an architecture. The term
*topology* is used carefully: the program records nodes and relations, but no
graph statistic is treated as perfume depth. A formula with many edges can be
incoherent; a sparse architecture can be exact. The purpose of the graph is to
make causal claims visible and falsifiable.

Nodes may represent materials, naturals, anatomy facets, accords, modules,
subjects, temporal states, or spatial functions. An edge must state direction,
sign, mechanism, target link, time scope, and failure. “Hedione supports
jasmine” is too vague. “The diffusive floral bridge increases continuity
between jasmine flesh and the late woody enclosure; omission should increase
split-plane ratings without reducing total volume” is a usable edge.

The core relation types are:

- **Anchor:** stabilizes a named identity or required region.
- **Bridge:** makes two regions or modules behave as one object.
- **Echo:** repeats a quality in another material class, location, or time.
- **Contrast:** increases relief through a controlled opposition.
- **Counterweight:** limits sweetness, density, harshness, cleanliness, or
  another directional excess.
- **Suppression control:** reduces or spaces a participant that masks useful
  information.
- **Configural blend:** creates a new odor object at a critical relationship.
- **Temporal handoff:** transfers identity or emphasis from one state to the
  next.
- **Recurrence:** returns a prior cue after intervening change.
- **Metamorphosis:** transforms the object while preserving an interpretable
  invariant.
- **Diffusion carrier:** helps an intended identity or facet appear in the
  surrounding field.
- **Persistence support:** retains a target-linked cue without merely adding
  duration.
- **Negative-space relation:** deliberate restraint allows another feature to
  remain bounded.
- **Anti-collapse relation:** a dependency whose loss causes genericization or
  hierarchy failure.

Pairwise edges are insufficient for some effects. A **hyperedge** binds three
or more participants whose complete combination is hypothesized to produce a
distinct result. Floral flesh, bitter shadow, and creamy wood may create a
rewarding depth effect that no pair reproduces. Such a claim requires an n-ary
test; pairwise compatibility does not promote automatically.

## Mechanisms

**Bridging by shared recognizers** uses overlapping odor qualities to create
continuity. **Bridging by graded texture** moves from one surface to another
through intermediate materiality. **Bridging by temporal overlap** lets an
outgoing cue coexist briefly with an incoming one. **Bridging by emotional
analogy** can link apparently different materials—such as cool petals and
mineral wood—when both serve the same target tone, but this remains a sensory
hypothesis until tested.

**Echo** preserves identity without literal repetition. A petal's cool edge
may recur as mineral dryness in wood; fruit skin bitterness may recur in tea or
root; suede softness may recur in a macrocyclic skin effect. The echo must be
specific enough that its removal changes continuity. Generic Iso E, amber, or
musk use is not an echo merely because it occurs in many perfumes.

**Contrast** needs proportion and a shared object. Warm/cool, wet/dry,
smooth/rough, clean/animalic, bright/dark, sweet/bitter, dense/transparent, and
natural/abstract oppositions can increase relief. If the poles lack a bridge,
contrast becomes juxtaposition. If one pole dominates, the relation collapses
into takeover.

**Temporal relations** add direction. A handoff is not simultaneous blending;
it specifies outgoing and incoming participants and an overlap window.
Recurrence requires an observable cue to leave or recede and later return.
Metamorphosis requires an identity invariant so that change does not become an
unrelated perfume.

**Suppression-control relations** acknowledge that reducing a participant can
increase effective complexity. Receptor antagonism, mixture suppression,
overshadowing, and attention can make addition subtract information. A
well-designed negative relation may therefore be as important as a bridge.

## Material translation

Material-to-material edges are the finest level but can become unmanageable in
large formulas. Use them for uncertain or identity-critical interactions.
Facet-to-facet edges describe anatomy. Module-to-module edges describe
architecture. Subject-to-base and phase-to-phase edges describe large-scale
continuity. Every lower-level edge should roll up to an explicit target
function rather than forming an isolated knowledge graph.

Naturals may be nodes as whole materials. Their constituent interactions can
be used for analytical hypotheses, but the program must not fabricate exact
internal edges from average literature composition. Known chemicals can be
participants when stock identity and dose are bound. Carriers and fixatives
participate in physical-delivery edges, not automatically in perceptual edges.

Musk, wood, amber, sweetness, and radiance materials are common sources of
false universal edges. Their broad compatibility makes them useful supports
but weak evidence of target specificity. A relation must explain why this
exact support belongs to this named perfume and what distinct loss its omission
causes.

## Interactions

Relations interact with one another. A bridge can weaken contrast by smoothing
too much. A counterweight can also become a recognizer. A temporal handoff can
fail because physical evaporation is too fast or because the incoming cue is
suppressed. A musk can extend continuity for one assessor and disappear for
another due to specific anosmia. The program therefore records edge conflicts
and prerequisites.

Networks also have hierarchy. Identity anchors and macro-interfaces are tested
before optional microtexture. A successful local interaction does not excuse a
global failure. The complete perfume needs anti-collapse evaluation to ensure
that all modules still form the named object.

Graph density, degree, centrality, entropy, and motif count may describe the
declared network but have no direct sensory authority. A formula can be
designed around a hub material, a chain of handoffs, parallel anatomy, or a
recurrent motif. These are structural descriptions, not rankings.

## Failure modes

**Rhetorical edges** use words such as synergy, bridge, or harmony without a
changed-factor test. **Edge proliferation** assigns every pair a relationship,
making the graph dense but meaningless. **Universal-hub capture** routes every
module through one generic diffuser or wood and calls the result integrated.
**Pairwise promotion** assumes successful pairs guarantee a successful n-ary
mixture. **Static topology** ignores that relations change with concentration
and time.

**Orphan nodes** are rows with no target relation. **Detached modules** smell
appropriate separately but split in the full perfume. **Circular
justification** claims a material is necessary because it is in the formula
and the formula is complex because the material is necessary. **Graph-score
theater** substitutes network metrics for controlled sensory evidence.

## Observables

For every relation record source node, target node, relation type, direction,
participants, ratio or dose context, temporal windows, expected target change,
failure mode, collision risks, prerequisites, probe, and evidence state. For
hyperedges record all participants and prohibit promotion from pairwise
evidence alone.

Physical observations should target relation effects: continuity, separation,
contrast, masking, hierarchy, recurrence, transition quality, identity
retention, and adverse drift. Measure the endpoints that define the relation,
not merely overall strength. Keep assessor-level data and report ties.

## Controlled tests

Use **bridge ablation**: full architecture versus carrier-matched removal of
the bridge while endpoint modules remain fixed. Use **recombination**: neither
module, each alone, both without bridge, and full bridge where feasible. Use
**ratio perturbation** around the target to map whether the relation has a
window, a monotonic slope, or a cliff. Use **temporal overlap tests** for
handoffs and recurrence.

For a three-part hyperedge, a full factorial has eight presence/absence arms.
When that is impractical, select a fractional design that still estimates the
interaction of interest and retain full, complete-omission, and critical
partial controls. Freeze total volume, carrier, application mass, and all
nonparticipating modules. Blind codes and balance order.

An edge is retained when the complete relation produces a repeatable
target-linked effect beyond participants alone and does not trigger its
predeclared failure. If it produces no difference, only generic strength, or a
new rationale invented after evaluation, it remains unsupported.

## Claim boundary

The relational graph is an experiment map. It does not prove that the
relations are smelled, synergistic, hedonic, or superior. Pairing literature,
isolated descriptors, OAV, and network statistics cannot promote a relation to
sensory truth. Every formula-specific relation remains `NOT TESTED` until its
controlled physical comparison is observed and scoped.
