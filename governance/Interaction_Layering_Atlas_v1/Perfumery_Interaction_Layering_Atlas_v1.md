## Purpose

This atlas is the first full cross-group chemistry and sensory map for the high-complexity perfume program. It records how one perfumery system is expected to alter another through enhancement, suppression, masking, rounding, sharpening, configuration, temporal extension, texture change, or collision.

It does not claim that every pair has direct empirical perfume-mixture data. Direct studies are sparse. Mechanistic evidence, domain-specific evidence, patents, and structured perfumery hypotheses are separated through explicit confidence and evidence-class fields.

## Core findings used as governing rules

1. Odor mixtures are frequently hypoadditive and can suppress one another.
2. Odorants can antagonize or modulate one another at receptor level.
3. Some blends create a new configural odor object.
4. Dominant components can conceal weaker components until adaptation unmasks them.
5. Matrix, surface, time, and skin condition change headspace release.
6. Individual sensitivity varies substantially for Hedione, musks, salicylates, and other odorants.
7. Complex formulas therefore require distributed recognizers, multiple structural classes, temporal testing, and repeated-day physical evaluation.

## Recommended physical test

For every important pair:

- prepare independent A and B modules;
- match total perceived intensity as closely as practical;
- compare A, B, 3:1, 1:1, and 1:3;
- repeat at low and high total concentration;
- score at 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours, 8 hours, and 24 hours;
- repeat on another day;
- record enhancement, masking, suppression, new-object formation, and defect onset.

## OpenCode use

The package contains twelve bounded DeepSeek research packets. Workers should enrich the atlas with primary literature, contradictions, exact material examples, and narrower dose windows. The integrator must not silently upgrade a structured hypothesis to direct evidence.
