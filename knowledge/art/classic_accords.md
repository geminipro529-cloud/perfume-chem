# Classic Accords in Perfumery

The foundational scent families and their emotional signatures.

## Fougère (Fern)
**Scent Profile:** Herbaceous, green, slightly powdery, with a touch of lavender and oakmoss.

**Core Molecules:**
- Lavender (Linalool, Linalyl Acetate)
- Oakmoss (Atranol, Chloroatranol) — *now restricted; synthetic alternatives: Iso E Super, Hedione*
- Coumarin (sweet, hay-like)
- Geranium (Geraniol)

**Emotional Signature:** Masculine, fresh, clean, slightly retro. Evokes barbershop tradition and outdoor freshness.

**Famous Examples:** Brut, Acqua di Gio (modern interpretation), Paco Rabanne 1 Million (spiced fougère).

**Chemistry Notes:** Fougère is built on the **lavender-coumarin-oakmoss triangle**. The herbaceous top (lavender) fades to reveal coumarin's sweet warmth and oakmoss's earthy depth. Modern versions replace oakmoss with synthetic woody notes to avoid allergen restrictions.

## Chypre (Cyprus)
**Scent Profile:** Citrus top, floral heart, woody-mossy base. Sophisticated, slightly animalic.

**Core Molecules:**
- Bergamot (Limonene, Linalool) — top
- Rose or Jasmine (Geraniol, Indole) — heart
- Oakmoss or Patchouli (Patchoulol) — base
- Labdanum (Labdanolic acids) — warm, resinous

**Emotional Signature:** Elegant, timeless, slightly mysterious. Evokes Mediterranean landscapes and vintage luxury.

**Famous Examples:** Chanel No. 5 (the archetype), Mitsouko, Ysatis, Estée Lauder Youth Dew.

**Chemistry Notes:** Chypré is a **three-act play**: bright citrus (0–15 min), floral heart (15 min–3 hrs), then a woody-mossy drydown (3+ hrs). The transition is smooth because the floral heart bridges the volatile citrus and the heavy base. Indole (from jasmine) adds a subtle animalic, almost fecal undertone that deepens the sophistication.

## Amber (Orientals)
**Scent Profile:** Warm, sweet, sensual, sometimes powdery or creamy. Rich and enveloping.

**Core Molecules:**
- Vanilla (Vanillin, Ethyl Vanillin)
- Amber (Ambroxan, Ambrette seed oil)
- Labdanum (warm, resinous)
- Sandalwood (Santalol)
- Musk (Galaxolide, Iso E Super)
- Sometimes: Tonka bean (Coumarin), Benzoin (Benzoic acid esters)

**Emotional Signature:** Sensual, comforting, intimate, slightly decadent. Evokes warmth, skin, and luxury.

**Famous Examples:** Opium, Shalimar, Hypnotic Poison, Prada L'Homme Intense.

**Chemistry Notes:** Amber accords are built on **vanillin + ambroxan**, a pairing that creates a soft, skin-like warmth. Ambroxan is a synthetic musk with extremely low volatility (odor life: 12+ hours), making it the perfect fixative. Vanillin's sweetness rounds the composition, while labdanum adds resinous depth. The result is a "second skin" effect—the fragrance feels like it's emanating from the wearer's body.

## Floral (Rose, Jasmine, Tuberose)
**Scent Profile:** Varies by flower, but generally sweet, romantic, sometimes green or indolic.

**Rose Accord:**
- Geraniol (rosy, green)
- Rose oxide (rosy, slightly fruity)
- Phenethyl alcohol (rosy, honey-like)
- Citronellol (rosy, slightly citrus)

**Jasmine Accord:**
- Indole (animalic, fecal in high concentrations; floral in traces)
- Methyl anthranilate (fruity, grape-like)
- Benzyl acetate (fruity, floral)
- Skatole (fecal, animalic; used sparingly)

**Tuberose Accord:**
- Indole (animalic)
- Methyl anthranilate (fruity)
- Benzyl benzoate (floral, slightly sweet)
- Heliotropin (powdery, almond-like)

**Emotional Signature:** Romantic, feminine (traditionally), elegant, sometimes intoxicating. Evokes gardens, femininity, and nostalgia.

**Famous Examples:** Chanel No. 5 (rose-jasmine), Lancôme La Vie Est Belle (tuberose-vanilla), Guerlain La Petite Robe Noire (cherry-tuberose).

**Chemistry Notes:** Floral accords are complex because many flowers contain **indole**, a molecule that smells fecal in isolation but floral in blends. The key is **balance**: indole at 0.1% adds depth; at 1%+, it becomes unpleasant. Perfumers use indole strategically to add sensuality and animalic warmth to florals.

## Cologne (Citrus)
**Scent Profile:** Fresh, zesty, clean, slightly herbal. Light and ephemeral.

**Core Molecules:**
- Bergamot (Limonene, Linalool)
- Lemon (Limonene, Citral)
- Neroli (Linalool, Linalyl acetate, Indole)
- Petitgrain (Limonene, Linalool)
- Lavender (Linalool, Linalyl acetate)

**Emotional Signature:** Fresh, clean, masculine (traditionally), energetic. Evokes morning, cleanliness, and simplicity.

**Famous Examples:** Eau de Cologne (the original), 4711, Hermès Eau de Gentilhomme, Acqua di Parma.

**Chemistry Notes:** Cologne is built on **limonene-rich citrus oils** (bergamot, lemon, petitgrain). These are highly volatile (top notes only), so cologne fades quickly (1–2 hours). To extend longevity, perfumers add lavender and neroli (middle notes) and sometimes a touch of oakmoss or sandalwood (base). The result is a **linear fragrance**—it evolves subtly but maintains its fresh character throughout.

## Leather
**Scent Profile:** Animalic, slightly smoky, warm, sometimes fruity or floral. Evokes saddle leather, tobacco, and earth.

**Core Molecules:**
- Birch tar (Cresol, Guaiacol) — smoky, animalic
- Leather accord (Iso E Super, Hedione, Ambroxan) — warm, skin-like
- Tobacco absolute (Pyrazines, aldehydes) — smoky, warm
- Opoponax (Incense-like, slightly sweet)
- Castoreum (Animalic, musky) — *now synthetic*

**Emotional Signature:** Masculine, sophisticated, slightly dangerous. Evokes craftsmanship, tradition, and raw sensuality.

**Famous Examples:** Hermès Leather, Givenchy Gentleman Reserve Privée, Tom Ford Ombré Leather.

**Chemistry Notes:** Leather accords are built on **animalic molecules** (birch tar, castoreum, indole) combined with warm bases (Iso E Super, ambroxan). The smokiness comes from pyrazines and guaiacol. The result is a complex, slightly unsettling scent that feels authentic and masculine.

## Gourmand
**Scent Profile:** Sweet, edible, dessert-like. Vanilla, caramel, chocolate, candy.

**Core Molecules:**
- Vanillin (sweet, warm)
- Ethyl maltol (caramel, cotton candy)
- Heliotropin (powdery, almond)
- Benzoin (warm, vanilla-like)
- Tonka bean (Coumarin, sweet)
- Chocolate accord (Cocoa absolute, Pyrazines)

**Emotional Signature:** Playful, indulgent, comforting, slightly childish. Evokes desserts, warmth, and nostalgia.

**Famous Examples:** Prada Candy, Lancôme La Vie Est Belle, Yves Saint Laurent Mon Paris.

**Chemistry Notes:** Gourmand is a **modern accord** (popularized in the 1990s–2000s). It relies heavily on **vanillin + ethyl maltol**, a pairing that creates a sweet, almost edible warmth. The addition of heliotropin (powdery) and tonka (creamy) rounds the composition. Gourmand fragrances are typically high in fragrance concentration (15–20%) to maximize sweetness and longevity.

## Marine (Aquatic)
**Scent Profile:** Fresh, ozonic, slightly salty, clean. Evokes ocean spray and sea air.

**Core Molecules:**
- Calone (ozonic, watermelon-like) — *synthetic, created 1990s*
- Ambroxan (warm, slightly salty)
- Seaweed accord (Iodine-like, slightly briny)
- Citrus (Limonene, Linalool)
- Aldehydes (C-10 to C-12, soapy, metallic)

**Emotional Signature:** Fresh, modern, unisex, slightly artificial. Evokes beaches, cleanliness, and escape.

**Famous Examples:** Acqua di Gio, Issey Miyake L'Eau d'Issey, Heeley Sel Marin.

**Chemistry Notes:** Marine is a **synthetic accord** built on **Calone**, a molecule that smells ozonic and slightly fruity (watermelon-like). Calone was created in the 1990s and revolutionized perfumery, enabling the "aquatic" fragrance family. The addition of ambroxan and aldehydes creates a salty, slightly soapy character. Marine fragrances are typically light and fresh, with a short to medium longevity (3–5 hours).
