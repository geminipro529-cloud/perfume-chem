# Composition Techniques & Structural Design

Architectural models for building a fragrance.

## 1. The Classic Pyramid (Triangular Structure)
**Concept:** A temporal evolution from volatile top notes to tenacious base notes.
- **Top (Head):** 15-25% of formula. Life: 5-30 mins.
  - *Role:* The "hook." Sells the perfume in the first minute.
  - *Materials:* Citrus, light herbs, aldehydes, fruity esters.
- **Heart (Middle):** 30-40% of formula. Life: 20 mins - 4 hours.
  - *Role:* The "theme." The true character of the fragrance.
  - *Materials:* Florals, spices, heavier fruits.
- **Base (Drydown):** 40-55% of formula. Life: 4 - 24+ hours.
  - *Role:* The "memory." What remains on skin/clothes.
  - *Materials:* Woods, musks, resins, vanilla.

## 2. The Linear Structure (The "American" Style)
**Concept:** The scent smells the same from spray to drydown. No evolution.
- **Origin:** Popularized in the 80s/90s for functional perfumery (shampoo, detergent) and adopted by brands like Estée Lauder (e.g., *White Linen*).
- **Technique:**
  - Use materials with similar vapor pressures.
  - Overdose on substantive heart/base notes.
  - Avoid fleeting citrus tops; use long-lasting freshness (e.g., Dihydromyrcenol).
- **Pros:** Consistent signature; the customer gets exactly what they bought.
- **Cons:** Can be monotonous or "flat."

## 3. The "Star" or "Hug" Structure (Grojsman Style)
**Concept:** A massive central accord (the "Star") that dominates, with small decorations around it.
- **Technique:**
  - Build a huge, diffusive core (e.g., 80% of formula) using transparent, long-lasting molecules (Iso E Super, Hedione, Galaxolide, Methyl Ionone).
  - Add "accessory" notes (Top/Heart) to color the star.
- **Visual:** An inverted pyramid or a diamond.
- **Example:** *Trésor* (Lancôme). The core rose-peach-amber accord is present from start to finish.

## 4. The Jean-Claude Ellena "Minimalist" Style
**Concept:** "Perfume is not a mixture, it is a composition." Few ingredients, maximum expression.
- **Technique:**
  - Short formulas (< 30 ingredients).
  - Focus on unique accords rather than complex bouquets.
  - High use of Hedione and Iso E Super for "lift" and "space."
  - "Watercolor" aesthetic: transparent, light, legible.
- **Example:** *Terre d'Hermès*, *Jardin sur le Nil*.

## 5. The "Kaleidoscope" or "Prism" Structure
**Concept:** Facets of the fragrance appear and disappear randomly.
- **Technique:**
  - Use materials with oscillating detection thresholds.
  - Avoid heavy fixatives that "glue" everything down.
- **Example:** *Mure et Musc* (L'Artisan).

## Dilution & Maceration
- **Concentration:**
  - *Extrait:* 20-30% oil.
  - *Eau de Parfum (EdP):* 15-20% oil.
  - *Eau de Toilette (EdT):* 5-15% oil.
  - *Eau de Cologne (EdC):* 2-4% oil.
- **Maceration (Aging):**
  - Freshly mixed perfume smells disjointed ("jagged").
  - **Process:** Let the concentrate sit for 1-4 weeks, then dilute with alcohol, then let sit for another 4-8 weeks.
  - **Chemistry:** Schiff base formation (Aldehyde + Amine), trans-esterification, acetal formation. The blend "rounds out."
