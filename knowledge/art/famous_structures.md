# Famous Fragrance Structures

Deconstructing the masterpieces that defined their eras.

## Chanel No. 5 (1921) - The Abstract Floral
**Perfumer:** Ernest Beaux
**Structure:**
- **Top:** Aldehydes (C-10, C-11, C-12 MNA) - *Overdose*
- **Heart:** Jasmine (Grasse), Rose de Mai, Ylang-Ylang
- **Base:** Vetiver, Sandalwood, Musk, Civet (Natural)

**Key Innovation:** The "Overdose" of aliphatic aldehydes.
- **Chemistry:** Aldehydes are fatty and soapy on their own. When combined with rich florals (Jasmine/Rose), they "lift" the heavy oils, creating a shimmering, abstract effect that smells like "a woman, not a flower."
- **Legacy:** Created the "Aldehydic Floral" family.

## Shalimar (1925) - The Reference Oriental
**Perfumer:** Jacques Guerlain
**Structure:**
- **Top:** Bergamot (30% of formula!)
- **Heart:** Rose, Jasmine, Iris
- **Base:** Vanillin (Ethyl Vanillin), Coumarin, Opoponax, Civet

**Key Innovation:** The contrast between fresh citrus and heavy amber.
- **Chemistry:** The massive dose of Bergamot (Limonene/Linalyl Acetate) provides a sharp, fresh opening that immediately crashes into the warm, sweet base of Ethyl Vanillin and Coumarin.
- **Legacy:** The template for all orientals. "Fresh top, warm bottom."

## Angel (1992) - The First Gourmand
**Perfumer:** Olivier Cresp
**Structure:**
- **Top:** Bergamot, Helional (Ozone/Melon)
- **Heart:** Dewberry, Honey, Red Berries
- **Base:** Patchouli (Overdose), Ethyl Maltol (Cotton Candy), Vanillin, Chocolate

**Key Innovation:** The Patchouli-Sugar accord.
- **Chemistry:** Ethyl Maltol smells like burnt sugar/caramel. It is usually cloying. Cresp balanced it with a massive dose of Patchouli (earthy, camphoraceous, dark).
- **Effect:** The bitterness of the patchouli cuts the sweetness of the sugar, creating a "poisonous nectar" effect.
- **Legacy:** Launched the Gourmand category.

## CK One (1994) - The Unisex Clean
**Perfumer:** Alberto Morillas & Harry Fremont
**Structure:**
- **Top:** Bergamot, Lemon, Pineapple, Papaya
- **Heart:** Hedione (High dose), Green Tea accord, Violet
- **Base:** Musk (Galaxolide/Habanolide), Amber

**Key Innovation:** The "Non-Perfume" Perfume.
- **Chemistry:** Built on a skeleton of **Hedione** (radiance) and **Musks** (clean laundry). It lacks the heavy woods or florals of the 80s.
- **Legacy:** Defined the 90s "Clean" aesthetic.

## Baccarat Rouge 540 (2015) - The Molecular Monolith
**Perfumer:** Francis Kurkdjian
**Structure:**
- **Core:**
  - **Ethyl Maltol:** (Strawberry jam/Burnt sugar)
  - **Evernyl (Veramoss):** (Synthetic Oakmoss - dry, iodine)
  - **Ambroxan:** (Ambergris - salty, skin)
  - **Hedione:** (Radiance)
  - **Saffron:** (Safranal - leathery, metallic)

**Key Innovation:** The "Transparent Gourmand."
- **Chemistry:** It contains no "natural" smelling flowers or woods. It is a precise balance of synthetic powerhouses.
- **Effect:** The sweetness of Ethyl Maltol is dried out by the Evernyl and Ambroxan. It is sweet but airy, burnt but clean. It causes extreme olfactory fatigue (wearers can't smell it, others smell it across the room).

## Dior Sauvage (2015) - The Modern Fougère
**Perfumer:** François Demachy
**Structure:**
- **Top:** Bergamot (Reggio), Pepper
- **Heart:** Geranium, Lavender, Elemi
- **Base:** Ambroxan (Overdose), Norlimbanol (Dry wood)

**Key Innovation:** The Ambroxan Overdose.
- **Chemistry:** A massive amount of Ambroxan provides a salty, musky, skin-like power that lasts for days. Combined with the "dryer sheet" freshness of modern lavender and the scratchy dryness of Norlimbanol.
- **Legacy:** The "Blue" fragrance archetype—universally pleasing, chemically powerful, and relentless.
