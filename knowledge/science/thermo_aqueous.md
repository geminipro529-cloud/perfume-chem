# Thermodynamics in Aqueous & Alcoholic Solutions

Understanding how fragrance molecules behave in their carrier (Ethanol/Water) and on the skin.

## Vapor-Liquid Equilibrium (VLE)
The core principle of perfume performance.
- **Raoult's Law Deviation:** Perfume is a non-ideal solution.
  - *Positive Deviation:* Hydrophobic molecules (Terpenes) "hate" the polar ethanol/water mix and want to escape (evaporate) faster than their pure VP suggests.
  - *Negative Deviation:* Hydrophilic molecules (Alcohols, Phenols) form hydrogen bonds with the solvent and are "held back," evaporating slower.

## The UNIFAC Model
A method to predict activity coefficients based on functional groups.
- **Application:** We can predict how a change in solvent (e.g., changing from 95% ethanol to an oil base) will alter the scent profile.
- **Oil vs. Alcohol:**
  - *In Alcohol:* Citrus pops (high activity coefficient).
  - *In Oil:* Citrus is suppressed (lower activity coefficient, high solubility).

## Henry's Law & Skin Chemistry
- **Partitioning:** Molecules partition between the air, the perfume film, and the skin lipids (sebum).
- **Sebum Effect:** Oily skin holds hydrophobic base notes (Musks, Woods) longer but suppresses their initial projection. Dry skin allows rapid evaporation.

## The "Fixative" Myth vs. Reality
- **Myth:** Fixatives "anchor" molecules chemically.
- **Reality:** Fixatives act as a high-boiling solvent that lowers the vapor pressure of the whole mixture (Raoult's Law).
- **The "Chromatographic Effect":** As the perfume evaporates, the composition of the liquid residue changes, becoming more viscous and hydrophobic, further slowing evaporation.
