# Photo and Thermal Stability: Light and Heat Will Destroy Your Work

**Which functional groups degrade, and how to protect them.**

---

## Core Principle
Light (especially UV) and heat provide energy for unwanted reactions. Some molecules are stable, others degrade fast. Know which is which.

---

## PART 1: PHOTODEGRADATION (UV Light)

### The Mechanism
**UV light (λ < 400 nm) breaks bonds.**

**Energy:**
```
E = hc/λ

UV-A (315-400 nm): 3.1-3.9 eV
UV-B (280-315 nm): 3.9-4.4 eV
UV-C (100-280 nm): 4.4-12.4 eV (mostly filtered by atmosphere)
```

**Most organic bonds:** 3-4 eV to break.

**Result:** UV light = bond cleavage = degradation.

---

### Which Functional Groups Are Photosensitive?

| Functional Group | Photosensitivity | Degradation Product | Examples |
|------------------|------------------|---------------------|----------|
| **Aldehydes (R-CHO)** | High | Acids, radicals | Citral, Cinnamaldehyde |
| **Ketones (R-CO-R')** | Moderate | Cleavage products | Carvone, Iso E Super (mild) |
| **Terpenes (C=C)** | High | Epoxides, polymers | Limonene, Pinene |
| **Phenols (Ar-OH)** | High | Quinones (colored) | Vanillin, Eugenol |
| **Nitro groups (Ar-NO₂)** | Moderate | Amines, nitroso | Musk ketone |
| **Esters (R-COO-R')** | Low | Hydrolysis (needs water) | Linalyl acetate (stable) |
| **Lactones** | Low | Stable | Coumarin, Galaxolide |
| **Ethers** | Low | Stable | Ambroxan, Iso E Super |

**Rule:** Aldehydes, terpenes, and phenols are UV-vulnerable.

---

### Specific Examples

#### Vanillin (Phenol)
**Reaction:**
```
UV
Vanillin → Vanillic acid + quinones (brown products)
```

**Result:** Darkening, loss of sweetness.

**Prevention:** Dark bottle, add antioxidants.

---

#### Limonene (Terpene)
**Reaction:**
```
UV + O₂
Limonene → Limonene oxide, Carvone, Carveol (off-notes)
```

**Result:** Fresh citrus → turpentine smell.

**Prevention:** Dark bottle, BHT (0.05%), use fresh.

---

#### Citral (Aldehyde)
**Reaction:**
```
UV
Citral → Geranic acid, polymerization products
```

**Result:** Lemon → sour/stale.

**Prevention:** Minimize UV exposure.

---

### The "Color Test" for Photodegradation
**If it turns yellow/brown/dark over time in light → photodegradation is happening.**

---

## PART 2: THERMAL DEGRADATION (Heat)

### The Mechanism
**Heat = kinetic energy → faster reactions.**

**Arrhenius Equation:**
```
k = A·e^(-Ea/RT)

Rate doubles every ~10°C increase (rough rule)
```

**Translation:** 40°C = 2x faster degradation than 30°C.

---

### Which Functional Groups Are Thermally Sensitive?

| Functional Group | Thermal Sensitivity | Degradation Product | Safe Temp |
|------------------|---------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Aldehydes** | High | Polymerization, oxidation | < 30°C |
| **Terpenes** | High | Oxidation, rearrangement | < 25°C |
| **Esters** | Moderate | Hydrolysis (if water present) | < 40°C |
| **Phenols** | Moderate | Oxidation, darkening | < 35°C |
| **Lactones** | Low | Stable | < 60°C |
| **Ethers** | Low | Very stable | < 80°C |

**Rule:** Aldehydes and terpenes hate heat.

---

### Specific Examples

#### Citrus Oils (Terpene-Rich)
**Problem:** Auto-oxidation accelerates with heat.

**Data:**
- At 20°C: Shelf life ~6 months
- At 30°C: Shelf life ~2 months
- At 40°C: Shelf life ~2 weeks

**Prevention:** Store cool (<20°C), add antioxidants.

---

#### Cinnamaldehyde
**Problem:** Polymerizes at high temp.

**Reaction:**
```
Heat + Acid
2 Cinnamaldehyde → Polymer (viscous gunk)
```

**Prevention:** Keep pH neutral, store cool.

---

#### Vanillin
**Problem:** Oxidizes to vanillic acid (less sweet).

**Data:**
- At 20°C: Stable for years
- At 50°C: Degrades in weeks

**Prevention:** Store cool, dark.

---

## PART 3: ANTIOXIDANT STRATEGIES

### Why Antioxidants Work
**Antioxidants are radical scavengers.** They react with oxygen radicals INSTEAD of your perfume molecules.

**Mechanism:**
```
R· (radical) + AH (antioxidant) → RH + A·
A· is stable (doesn't propagate chain reaction)
```

---

### Best Antioxidants for Perfumery

| Antioxidant | Typical % | Pros | Cons |
|-------------|-----------|------|------|
| **BHT (Butylated Hydroxytoluene)** | 0.01-0.1% | Very effective, odorless | Synthetic, some dislike |
| **Vitamin E (Tocopherol)** | 0.05-0.2% | Natural, GRAS | Slightly oily, mild smell |
| **Ascorbyl Palmitate** | 0.05-0.1% | Natural, stable | Slightly waxy |
| **Rosemary Extract** | 0.1-0.5% | Natural, pleasant | Herbal smell (can interfere) |

**Rule:** Use BHT for odorless protection. Use Vitamin E for "natural" formulas.

---

### How to Use Antioxidants

**Method:**
1. Dissolve antioxidant in ethanol FIRST (before adding perfume oil)
2. Add perfume oil
3. Seal immediately (minimize air exposure)

**Formula with BHT:**
```
BHT: 0.05%
Ethanol: 79.95%
Perfume oil (with terpenes, aldehydes): 20%

Mix BHT into ethanol first, then add perfume oil.
```

## PART 4: PACKAGING AND STORAGE

### Light Protection

#### Best Practices
- **Amber glass bottles** (blocks UV-A/B)
- **Dark storage** (cabinet, drawer)
- **Avoid sunlight** (even indirect)

#### Bottle Color and UV Transmission
| Bottle Color | UV Transmission | Best For |
|--------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Clear glass** | 90% (BAD) | Display only, short-term |
| **Green glass** | 50% | Moderate protection |
| **Amber glass** | <5% | Best for terpenes, aldehydes, phenols |
| **Cobalt blue** | ~10% | Good, aesthetically pleasing |

**Rule:** Amber or cobalt for anything with citrus, aldehydes, or phenols.

---

### Temperature Control

#### Storage Temps
- **Ideal:** 15-20°C (59-68°F)
- **Acceptable:** 20-25°C (68-77°F)
- **Marginal:** 25-30°C (never for terpenes/aldehydes)
- **Bad:** >30°C (degradation accelerates)

#### Special Cases
- **Citrus blends:** Store at 10-15°C (fridge OK)
- **Resinous blends:** Room temp OK (stable)

---

### Air Exposure

#### Minimizing Oxidation
1. **Full bottles:** Minimal headspace (less O₂)
2. **Nitrogen blanket:** (Advanced) Replace air with N₂
3. **Small bottles:** Decant large batch into small bottles (less air contact per use)

---

## PART 5: FORMULATION STRATEGIES FOR STABILITY

### Strategy 1: Choose Stable Molecules
**Replace photosensitive ingredients with stable analogs.**

| Unstable | Stable Alternative | Trade-Off |
|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Limonene | Dihydromyrcenol | Less citrus, more metallic |
| Citral | Geraniol + Linalool | More floral, less lemon |
| Vanillin | Ethyl Vanillin | More stable, slightly different |
| Natural Rose | Phenylethyl Alcohol + Rose Oxide | Less complex, more stable |

**Rule:** Synthetics > naturals for stability.

---

### Strategy 2: Add Antioxidants
**0.05% BHT = 5-10x longer shelf life for terpene-heavy blends.**

---

### Strategy 3: Avoid Problem Combos
**Aldehydes + Heat + UV = disaster.**

**Bad Combo Example:**
```
High citral (aldehyde)
Clear glass bottle
Stored in sunlight

Result: Brown, stale in 2 weeks
```

**Good Combo:**
```
Moderate citral
+ 0.05% BHT
Amber bottle
Dark storage

Result: Fresh for 6+ months
```

---

### Strategy 4: Macerate in Dark/Cool
**Maceration = time. Time + light/heat = degradation.**

**Rule:** If macerating >1 week, do it in amber bottle, dark, cool.

## PART 7: TESTING FOR DEGRADATION

### The Protocol
1. **Visual:** Color change (yellow → brown = oxidation/photodegradation)
2. **Olfactory:** Smell fresh vs aged sample. Off-notes?
3. **pH:** Check if pH dropped (acids forming from oxidation)
4. **Viscosity:** Increased viscosity = polymerization

### Accelerated Aging Test
**Want to predict shelf life fast?**

**Method:**
1. Make 3 samples
2. Sample A: Dark, cool (15°C) - control
3. Sample B: Dark, warm (40°C) - thermal stress
4. Sample C: Light, room temp (25°C) - photo stress
5. Check after 1 week

**Interpretation:**
- Sample B darker/off-smell → thermal instability
- Sample C darker/off-smell → photoinst ability
- Both fine → probably stable

**Rule:** 1 week at 40°C ≈ 2-3 months at 20°C (rough estimate).

## PART 8: TROUBLESHOOTING DEGRADATION

### Problem 1: Blend Turned Yellow/Brown
**Cause:** Phenol/aldehyde oxidation, photodegradation

**Fix (preventive):** Amber bottle, antioxidants, dark storage

---

### Problem 2: Citrus Smell Went Stale/Turpentine
**Cause:** Terpene oxidation (limonene → carvone/limonene oxide)

**Fix (preventive):** BHT, store cool (<20°C), use within 6 months

---

### Problem 3: Sweet Vanilla Became Sour
**Cause:** Vanillin oxidation → vanillic acid

**Fix (preventive):** Amber bottle, store cool

---

### Problem 4: Viscous Gunk Formed
**Cause:** Aldehyde polymerization

**Fix (preventive):** Don't macerate aldehyde-heavy blends >2 weeks, keep pH neutral

---

## PART 9: ADVANCED STABILITY TRICKS

### Trick 1: The "Chelator Addition"
**Metal ions (Fe³⁺, Cu²⁺) catalyze oxidation.**

**Fix:** Add EDTA (0.01-0.05%) to chelate metals.

**Use:** If your water source has metals, or using natural extracts (which may contain trace metals).

---

### Trick 2: The " Nitrogen Flush"
**For maximum stability:**
1. Fill bottle with perfume
2. Briefly flush with nitrogen gas (from canister)
3. Seal immediately

**Effect:** Removes O₂ from headspace → minimal oxidation.

---

### Trick 3: The "Stabilizer Blend"
**Combine antioxidants for synergy:**
```
BHT: 0.03%
Vitamin E: 0.05%
Ascorbyl Palmitate: 0.02%

Total antioxidants: 0.1%
```

**Effect:** Multi-mechanism protection, longer shelf life.

---

## PART 10: REGULATORY AND SAFETY

### IFRA Restrictions Related to Stability
**Some ingredients are restricted partly because degradation products are allergenic.**

**Example:** Limonene oxidation products (limonene oxide, carvone) are more allergenic than fresh limonene.

**Rule:** Stability isn't just cosmetic—degradation can create safety issues.

## THE GOLDEN RULES

1. **Amber glass blocks UV** → use for any citrus/aldehyde/phenol blend.
2. **BHT (0.05%) extends shelf life** 5-10x for terpenes.
3. **Cool storage (<20°C)** = slower degradation (Arrhenius).
4. **Citrus oils are perishable** — use within 6 months.
5. **Synthetics (musks, ethers) are stable** — no special care needed.
6. **Degradation products can be allergenic** — stability = safety.
7. **Test at 40°C for 1 week** to predict shelf life.

---

**Use this:** Light and heat are your enemies. Amber bottles, BHT, and cool storage aren't optional for terpene/aldehyde blends—they're mandatory.
