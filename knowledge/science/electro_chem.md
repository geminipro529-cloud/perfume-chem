# Electrochemistry & Stability

Why perfumes go bad, change color, or smell metallic.

## Oxidation Pathways
The primary enemy of freshness.
- **Terpenes (Limonene/Pinene):** React with atmospheric oxygen to form peroxides (skin sensitizers) and off-odors (turpentine-like).
  - *Prevention:* BHT/Tocopherol (Antioxidants).
- **Aldehydes:** Oxidize to carboxylic acids (smell sour/fatty).
  - *Prevention:* Keep cold, minimize headspace.
- **Vanillin:** Oxidizes to form dark brown polymers (discolors the juice).

## Schiff Base Formation
A condensation reaction between an Aldehyde and a Primary Amine (e.g., Methyl Anthranilate).
- **Visual:** Turns the perfume dark yellow/orange/brown.
- **Olfactory:** Creates a new, deep, tenacious molecule (e.g., Aurantiol).
- **Usage:** Often intentional (Neroli bases), but can be a disaster if unplanned (e.g., Vanillin + Anthranilate = Black sludge).

## pH Effects
- **Esters:** Hydrolyze in acidic or alkaline conditions back into acid + alcohol (smell changes completely).
- **Skin pH:** Varies (4.5 - 5.5). Can catalyze hydrolysis of sensitive materials over hours.

## Metal Catalysis
- **Iron/Copper:** Trace metals from processing equipment or storage drums can catalyze oxidation.
- **Chelators:** EDTA or Citric Acid are added to bind these metals and protect the fragrance.

## UV Degradation
- **Light:** Breaks double bonds, creates free radicals.
- **UV Absorbers:** Benzophenone-4 or Ethylhexyl Methoxycinnamate are added to protect the color and scent.
