# Functional Group Reactivity in Perfumery

**The chemist's advantage: Predict interactions by functional group, not by individual molecules.**

---

## Core Principle
Functional groups dictate reactivity. Learn the pattern once, apply it to every molecule with that group.

---

## 1. ALDEHYDES (R-CHO)

### Reactivity Profile
- **Electrophilic carbonyl carbon** - reacts with nucleophiles
- **Oxidize to carboxylic acids** - R-CHO → R-COOH
- **Form Schiff bases with amines** - R-CHO + R-NH₂ → R-CH=N-R + H₂O

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Aldehyde + Amine | Yellow/brown discoloration, smell changes | Never mix. Separate formulas. |
| Aldehyde + O₂ | Oxidation to acid, sharp smell becomes sour | Use antioxidants (BHT), store in dark |
| Aldehyde + Alcohol | Hemiacetal formation, mild interactions | Usually fine in ethanol |

### Examples
- **Citral** (lemon aldehyde) + any amine = brown mess
- **Cinnamaldehyde** + O₂ = cinnamic acid (loses sweetness)
- **Benzaldehyde** + traces of iron = dark discoloration

### Rule
**If it has -CHO, keep it away from -NH₂. Period.**

---

## 2. PHENOLS (Ar-OH)

### Reactivity Profile
- **Acidic** (pKa ~10) - deprotonates in base
- **Oxidize easily** - form quinones (colored)
- **React with Fe³⁺** - purple/brown complexes

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Phenol + Base | Deprotonation, discoloration | Keep pH neutral (5-7) |
| Phenol + O₂ | Quinone formation, brown color | Antioxidants, inert atmosphere |
| Phenol + Metal ions | Colored complexes | Use glass, avoid metal containers |

### Examples
- **Eugenol** (clove) + alkaline water = darkens
- **Vanillin** + Fe³⁺ = purple stain
- **Guaiacol** + O₂ = brown oxidation products

### Rule
**Phenols hate base and oxygen. Keep them in acid/neutral, dark conditions.**

---

## 3. ESTERS (R-COO-R')

### Reactivity Profile
- **Hydrolyze in acid/base** - R-COO-R' + H₂O → R-COOH + R'-OH
- **Transesterification** - can exchange R' groups
- **Stable in neutral pH**

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Ester + Acid + H₂O | Hydrolysis to acid + alcohol | Keep water out, pH neutral |
| Ester + Base + H₂O | Saponification (soap formation) | Avoid alkaline conditions |
| Ester + Heat | Slow hydrolysis | Store cool |

### Examples
- **Linalyl acetate** + moisture = linalool + acetic acid
- **Benzyl benzoate** + NaOH = benzyl alcohol + sodium benzoate
- **Hedione** (ester) is stable in ethanol but not in water/acid

### Rule
**Esters are happy in dry, neutral ethanol. Water + pH extremes = death.**

---

## 4. TERPENES (C=C Double Bonds)

### Reactivity Profile
- **Oxidize via autoxidation** - C=C + O₂ → epoxides, aldehydes
- **Polymerize under acid catalysis**
- **React with radicals**

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Terpene + O₂ + Light | Oxidation to off-notes | Dark storage, antioxidants (0.05% BHT) |
| Terpene + Acid | Polymerization, gummy residue | Neutral pH |
| Terpene + Heat | Accelerated oxidation | Store cool |

### Examples
- **Limonene** + O₂ = carvone, limonene oxide (smell changes from fresh to musty)
- **Pinene** + O₂ = verbenone (turpentine smell)
- **Geraniol** + O₂ = geranial, neral (metallic aldehydes)

### Rule
**All terpenes will eventually oxidize. It's not "if", it's "when". Use fresh or add antioxidants.**

---

## 5. ALCOHOLS (R-OH)

### Reactivity Profile
- **Form esters with acids** - R-OH + R'-COOH → R-OCO-R' + H₂O
- **Oxidize to aldehydes (1°) or ketones (2°)**
- **Form ethers (slow)**

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Alcohol + Acid | Ester formation (usually desired) | Control conditions |
| Alcohol + Strong oxidizer | Aldehyde/ketone formation | Avoid peroxides, strong acids |
| Alcohol + H-bond acceptor | Slowed evaporation (desired) | Use strategically |

### Examples
- **Linalool** + citric acid → linalyl esters (fruity)
- **Geraniol** + O₂ (slow) → geranial (aldehyde)
- **Phenylethyl alcohol** + benzoic acid → phenylethyl benzoate

### Rule
**Alcohols are mostly stable. Watch for oxidation under harsh conditions.**

---

## 6. LACTONES (Cyclic Esters)

### Reactivity Profile
- **More stable than linear esters** (ring strain reduces hydrolysis)
- **Can open under strong base**
- **Stable in neutral conditions**

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Lactone + Strong base | Ring opening to hydroxy acid | Keep pH < 8 |
| Lactone + Heat | Usually stable | Safe to heat |

### Examples
- **Coumarin** (lactone) = stable in ethanol for years
- **Galaxolide** (macrocyclic lactone) = extremely stable
- **γ-Decalactone** (peach lactone) = stable

### Rule
**Lactones are your friends. Stable, reliable, no drama.**

---

## 7. NITRO GROUPS (Ar-NO₂)

### Reactivity Profile
- **Reduce to amines** under strong reducing conditions
- **Stable in neutral/oxidizing conditions**
- **Can photobleach (blue light absorption)**

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Nitro + Reducing agent | Amine formation | Avoid strong reductants |
| Nitro + UV light | Slow degradation | Dark storage |

### Examples
- **Musk ketone** (aromatic nitro musk) = stable but light-sensitive
- **Nitro musks** generally stable

### Rule
**Nitro musks are stable but keep them in the dark.**

---

## 8. AMINES (R-NH₂, R₂-NH)

### Reactivity Profile
- **Basic** (pKa ~10) - protonate in acid
- **Nucleophilic** - react with electrophiles (aldehydes, epoxides)
- **Oxidize to colored products**

### In Perfumery
| Reaction | Result | Prevention |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Amine + Aldehyde | Schiff base (yellow/brown) | Never mix |
| Amine + Acid | Salt formation (reduced volatility) | Avoid strong acids |
| Amine + O₂ | Oxidation, discoloration | Inert atmosphere |

### Examples
- **Any amine** + citral = disaster
- **Indole** (weakly basic amine) + aldehydes = color changes

### Rule
**Amines are reactive. Keep them away from aldehydes, strong acids, and oxygen.**

---

## QUICK REFERENCE TABLE

| Functional Group | Avoid | Compatible With | Storage |
|------------------|-------|-----------------|---------|
| Aldehyde (-CHO) | Amines, Strong base | Alcohols, Esters | Dark, antioxidant |
| Phenol (Ar-OH) | Base, O₂, Metals | Neutral pH, Alcohols | Dark, neutral pH |
| Ester (R-COO-R') | Water + Acid/Base | Dry ethanol | Cool, dry |
| Terpene (C=C) | O₂, Light, Heat | Stable in dark | Dark, cool, antioxidant |
| Alcohol (R-OH) | Strong oxidizers | Most things | Room temp OK |
| Lactone (cyclic ester) | Strong base | Most things | Very stable |
| Nitro (Ar-NO₂) | UV light | Most things | Dark |
| Amine (R-NH₂) | Aldehydes, Acids, O₂ | Neutral conditions | Inert atmosphere |

---

## THE GOLDEN RULES

1. **Aldehyde + Amine = Never**
2. **Phenols + Base = Discoloration**
3. **Esters + Water = Hydrolysis**
4. **Terpenes + O₂ = Eventually oxidize**
5. **When in doubt, keep pH 5-7, store dark and cool**

---

**Use this:** When you see a molecule, identify its functional groups. Apply these rules. You'll predict 90% of stability issues before they happen.
