## Why This Is the Hedonic Recut

1. **Preserve what the nose already accepted.** The complete lemon/mandarin/ginger dose, the 200-uL DHM current, both lavenders, and the literal cedar/vetiver spine remain fixed.
2. **Place pleasure in the transition.** The supplier calls the Pink Pepper Base a heart note; at 70 uL it is positioned between ginger and Hedione/linalools, where it can make the perfume feel alive rather than simply smell peppery.
3. **Give standard Hedione room instead of pretending it is HC.** Hedione is raised modestly to 660 uL while Iso E, Sandalore, Cashmeran, Timberol, Benzyl Benzoate, and Romandolide are reduced. The theory is greater luminosity and smoothness with less masking; equivalence is not claimed.
4. **Trade generic density for readable grain.** Cedar and vetiver remain exact. Ebanol gains only 10 uL and Vetival gains 20 uL, so the base becomes more articulated without introducing another named wood.
5. **Keep tension.** Ambrox, Timberol, Helional, Cyclamen, lemon, and Ginger remain the cool/hard pressure; Mandarin, premium lavender, Pink Pepper Base, Hedione, Mayol, Sandalore, Azarbre, and Romandolide provide release.
6. **Avoid easy identity-killers.** No vanilla, ethyl maltol, heavy resin, extra musk, blue-marine dose escalation, or new citrus is used. Those could improve immediate friendliness while making it a different perfume.

This is a strong theory for higher pleasure, clarity, and re-smell desire, not proof. The user's positive observations are the only current sensory evidence, and they concern the predecessor and prior Pink Pepper Base additions—not this exact formula.
