## Blinding, Randomization, and Stopping Rules

1. A preparer who does not evaluate holds the code key. Evaluators and data analysts remain blind until exclusions and primary outcomes are frozen.
2. Each formula/module arm requires two independent preparations; repeat pours from one concentrate are not independent preparations.
3. Order is balanced within evaluator and time window. Use clean substrates and prevent cross-contamination.
4. Record anosmia/fatigue and enforce rest; no evaluator smells the complete causal matrix in one sitting.
5. First-block observations are exploratory. Replicated same-direction results across two blocks may support a bounded physical claim only within the tested conditions.
6. A tie favors the fewer-material, lower-dose, or zero-musk option.
7. Stop an arm immediately for safety uncertainty, phase instability, unacceptable irritation, gross identity failure, or a takeover exclusion. No hedonic result overrides safety.
8. Scores diagnose; the Lavender Current identity contract adjudicates.
