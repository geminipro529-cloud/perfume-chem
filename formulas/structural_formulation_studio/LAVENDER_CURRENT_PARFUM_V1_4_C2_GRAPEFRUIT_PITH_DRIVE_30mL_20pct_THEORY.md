## Candidate Theory

**Name:** Grapefruit Pith Drive.  
**One sentence:** the accepted lavender current acquires a bitter, airy grapefruit pith that starts at the front but points vertically through vetiver and Iso E into the dry base.  
**Why this is not merely “more citrus”:** Grapefruit is assigned a front-to-rear arm. Red Mandarin supplies the warm pulp reflection; grapefruit supplies peel, pith, and trace sulfurous tension; Vetiver and dry cedar receive that tension in the base.  
**Hedonic hypothesis:** bitter contrast should reduce the impression of sweetness and make the unchanged DHM/ambrox chassis feel adult, brisk, and more compulsively dry. This remains NOT TESTED.  
**Failure veto:** catty/sweaty sulfur, body odor, harsh pith, cheap grapefruit cleaner, rind bitterness without pleasure, or a base so dry that the lavender fabric disappears.

Primary aroma-dilution work on grapefruit identifies 1-p-menthene-8-thiol among several high-impact odorants and shows that typical grapefruit aroma requires a system of odorants, not one marker. The repository provides a constituent-weighted Grapefruit FCF composite model, but its assumed constituent fractions and thresholds are not bottle/lot measurements. Its unusually large modeled OAV is therefore a sensitivity flag, not proof that C2 leads or outperforms.

**Pressure:** Grapefruit FCF + DHM + Scentenal + Vetiver + Iso E + Ambrofix + Timberol.  
**Release:** Red Mandarin + BONTAUX lavender + Hedione HC + Mayol + AIMI + Sandalore/Ebanol/Javanol + Azarbre + Romandolide.  
**Opposed textures:** bitter/airy/dry/vertical versus warm/soft/luminous/yielding.  
**Wood decision:** unchanged. Grapefruit already has explicit vertical receivers in Vetiver, Virginia cedar, Iso E, Timberol, and the mineral sandal seam. Adding or rebasing wood now would duplicate those roles and confound the citrus screen.
