## Why Zenolide, and why this amount

IFF describes Zenolide as a delicate musk with a red-fruit facet and comparatively low base impact. My design inference is that it is the better starting choice for quiet support without adding another pronounced woody subject. The fruit facet could soften the bitter gin impression too much; it is a specific uncertainty to evaluate, not a promise that the perfume will be fruit-free. [IFF Zenolide](https://www.iff.com/scent/ingredients-compendium/zenolide/)

The independent Sol review preferred Habanolide for its earthy/woody relationship to vetiver. Its manufacturer also describes powderiness, warmth and considerable strength. Parent selection instead prioritizes restraint and leaves Habanolide out of this version. Neither material is intrinsically superior. [dsm-firmenich Habanolide](https://studio.dsm-firmenich.com/product/habanolider-pe-947303)

The **250 µL Zenolide allocation is a new trial dose**, not an equivalence conversion from Romandolide. It keeps the six other ingredient transfers, carrier amount, and total supplied-stock volume constant. Equal raw volume does not establish equal mass, potency, fixation, safety or sensory effect. No stock-strength rebase or hidden potency multiplier is used.

Other owned musk options were considered, not treated as interchangeable:

- **Ethylene Brassylate:** the supplier's herbaceous-musky description could suit botanicals, but it provides less direct support for the low-impact finish being selected here. It is deferred, not declared incompatible. [AAC technical sheet](https://aac-europe.com/media/products/ETHYLENE_BRASSYLATE_AAC068_AACE_mxelLbs.pdf)
- **Exaltolide:** the manufacturer's rounded, powdery/woody musk direction is not the main target; the owned 10% DPG stock also has an unspecified fraction basis. [dsm-firmenich Exaltolide](https://studio.dsm-firmenich.com/product/exaltolider-pe-941962)
- **Ambrettolide:** its documented richness, floral character and fruit sweetness are a greater design risk for this dry brief; the owned stock is 10% w/w in DPG, not neat. [IFF Ambrettolide](https://www.iff.com/scent/ingredients-compendium/ambrettolide/)
- **Galaxolide:** a powerful, persistent clean musk is a different starting emphasis; the owned 50% DEP stock would also alter the carrier ledger. The manufacturer reference describes the undiluted ingredient, not an assay of this working stock. [IFF Galaxolide](https://www.iff.com/scent/ingredients-compendium/galaxolide-undiluted/)

Manufacturer descriptions support selection hypotheses, not the identity, condition or performance of an untested owned lot. No manufacturer use-level figure is treated as a regulatory maximum or a prescribed formula ratio.

## Trial handling and evaluation

This is a new-bottle development composition, not instructions to add materials to a previous bottle. For the first physical trial, I recommend **10 mL of this same complete formula** before committing the full 30 mL: scale every ingredient and the ethanol by one third. That is one composition at a smaller size, not another variant. The unrounded smallest transfer is then 10 µL; do not scale below that direct-transfer floor without a prepared, labeled working stock and recalculated carrier accounting.

For any bench trial, follow each material's handling documents, use suitable eye/skin protection and ventilation, and keep ethanol away from ignition sources. Record actual additions. Close and mix the labeled bottle; check for homogeneity, clouding or separation before evaluating a small amount on a scent blotter. Do not apply to skin or distribute for wear on the strength of this card. A rest period does not establish safety or correct a poor formula.

The useful observations are whether juniper reads as gin, vetiver remains identifiable through the drydown, cypress stays secondary, and the musk does not add unwanted fruitiness or blur the botanicals. No projection, skin-life, liking, richness or superiority result is claimed.
