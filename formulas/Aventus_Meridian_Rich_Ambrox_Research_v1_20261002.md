# Aventus Meridian — Rich Ambrox development lead v1

Date: 2 October 2026. Mode: reconstruction-informed creative target, not an exact commercial reconstruction.
Status: FORMULATION_HYPOTHESIS_NOT_SENSORILY_VALIDATED. Root design; no current bottle, formula predecessor or inventory is modified.

The lead is an Aventus-style citrus/pineapple/cassis/jasmine/smoky-wood structure deliberately enlarged around crystal Ambrox. The published information inspected does not establish Creed's quantitative formula, batch-specific smell, similarity percentage, consumer preference, sillage or longevity. The printed development build uses a controlled guaiacol smoke hypothesis; a qualified-birch reference alternative remains separate.

Canonical arithmetic record: [JSON design](Aventus_Meridian_Rich_Ambrox_Research_v1_20261002.json).

## 1. Locked brief, reference and negative space

Recognizers: citrus peel; a green, juicy pineapple/cassis axis rather than candy; a transparent jasmine seam; smoky suede, patchouli, cedar and moss; a conspicuous ambergris/Ambrox finish.

Protected anchors: pineapple/cassis identity, woody dryness and the smoke-to-wood transition. Mobile sockets: ester balance, floral texturing, musk pair and the size of the Ambrox block.

Negative space: vanilla gourmand, ethyl-maltol fruit syrup, blue shower gel, obvious iris, a rose/jasmine bouquet, campfire tar, or an indiscriminate superamber stack. Extra strength is not the definition of richness. All time-window and interaction assignments below are perfumer hypotheses, not measured receptor maps or calibrated gas predictions.

Use a real, identified Aventus sample for any eventual similarity assessment. Without it, the comparison is to the documented architecture only. Reformulation, reference-bottle and batch uncertainty remain unresolved.

## 2. Research and evidence matrix

- [Creed's current Aventus description](https://creedboutique.com/products/aventus) supports citrus/cassis, pineapple/pepper/jasmine and birch/patchouli/musk architecture. [Creed's Aventus explanation](https://creedboutique.com/blogs/creed-journal/aventus-your-questions-answered) additionally discusses cedar, moss and Ambroxan. These are marketed notes, not exact ingredient identities or percentages.
- [Characteristic Aroma Compounds from Different Pineapple Parts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6264279/) is primary fruit-aroma research. Its ethyl-ester findings motivate a small realistic ester accent. A food extract's analytical concentration, OAV or threshold is not transplanted into this perfume.
- [PW Allyl Caproate](https://www.perfumersworld.com/view.php?pro_id=4FC02995), [Allyl Cyclohexyl Propionate](https://www.perfumersworld.com/view.php?pro_id=4FC00030), [Allyl Amyl Glycolate](https://www.perfumersworld.com/view.php?pro_id=4FG00029), [Ethyl Butyrate](https://www.perfumersworld.com/view.php?pro_id=3FD00174) and [Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate](https://www.perfumersworld.com/view.php?pro_id=4FD04118) provide supplier identity and olfactive-role evidence. The proposed doses are design judgments, not supplier-certified optima.
- [Firmenich Ambrox Super](https://www.firmenich.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/ingredients/marketing-sheet/perfumery/AMBROX_SUPER_909158.pdf) supports using the actual crystalline product for an ambergris/diffusion role. The 18% target here is deliberately aggressive; it is not a claim that this concentration is necessary, superior or approved.
- [Firmenich Habanolide](https://www.firmenich.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/ingredients/marketing-sheet/perfumery/HABANOLIDE_947303.pdf) supports a warm, substantive musk role. Romandolide is included as a softer alternative texture, not because multiple musks automatically improve a perfume.
- [PW birch documentation](https://www.perfumersworld.com/document-list.php?pro_id=7TP00822) contains a Category 4 table of 0.0100% and contradictory prohibition wording. [IFRA's birch standard](https://ifrafragrance.org/standards/IFRA_STD48_0011.pdf) distinguishes crude pyrolysate from rectified material meeting a PAH specification. Neither the table nor the word rectified clears the user's bottle or the finished formula.

No authenticated complete Creed GC-MS/formula with reproducible quantitative support was identified. Internet reconstruction percentages are not treated as ground truth. No gas curve, threshold or chemistry-family receptor affinity is manufactured for this design.

## 6. Stock preparation, concentrate and 30 mL evaluation bottle

Use a balance readable to 0.001 g; uncertainty is still determined by the actual balance, not merely its display. Weighing avoids an unverified density conversion. Labels need only material, w/w strength, carrier and date for these personal working stocks; photographs or purchase receipts are not required.

- New 10% stocks (Allyl Caproate, Ethyl Butyrate or Geranium): **0.200 g neat + 1.800 g perfumery ethanol = 2.000 g**.
- 1% stocks from a known 10% w/w parent (Blackcurrant, Rose de Mai, Beta Damascone, Suederal, Guaiacol): **0.200 g parent stock + 1.800 g perfumery ethanol = 2.000 g**. A DPG/DEP parent retains its carrier. The resulting stock is not pure ethanol plus fragrance.
- E2MB from neat: first make 10% with 0.200 g + 1.800 g ethanol; then make 1% with 0.200 g of that 10% stock + 1.800 g ethanol. This avoids trying to weigh 2 mg directly.
- Evernyl 10% w/w DPG is already recorded. Guaiacwood is exactly **one-third by mass**, not a rounded 33% stock.
- Do not prepare new stocks from depleted, unidentified or merely recommended historical preparations.

The independent perfume concentrate is the 100% mass target above. The stock-containing compound is **8.6724 g**, not a neat 6 mL concentrate. Carrier-containing preparations cannot be magically stripped of DPG/DEP.

Proposed single 30 mL evaluation build, after choosing/confirming this design:

1. Weigh the listed rows. Prefer a vessel with sufficient spare volume; record the separate **1,080 mg crystal Ambrox** operation.
2. Allocate **6.000 g of perfumery ethanol** initially to help dissolution. Add Ambrox gradually to the alcohol/liquid base, mix capped as appropriate, and confirm the compound is homogeneous. This is a starting solvent allocation, not a demonstrated solubility certificate.
3. Add dry-wood and musk materials, then moss/leather/smoke stocks, then the transparent heart and natural traces, then fruit esters/citrus/pepper. Keep volatile materials capped. Avoid exposing concentrated irritants or warming flammable alcohol near ignition sources.
4. After complete dissolution and temperature equilibration, add the same perfumery alcohol **q.s. to measured 30.00 mL**. Do **not** simply add 24 mL. Existing carriers and dissolved-solid volume already contribute.
5. Record finished mass only if reporting w/w. This bottle contains 6 g product equivalents per 30 mL, i.e. **20% w/v**, not a claimed 20% w/w or v/v.
6. Withhold if crystals persist, phase separation occurs or cold storage reprecipitates material. Filtering out Ambrox is a composition change, not a successful dissolution fix.

For identical strength to a 20% w/w trial in exactly 30 mL, measure the selected finished blend density and scale the entire composition proportionately. Do not use a 1 g/mL default.

An entirely mass-based option is simpler: **8.6724 g of the printed stock charge + 21.3276 g additional perfumery ethanol = 30.0000 g finished perfume**, containing **20.0000% w/w** product equivalents. The 21.3276 g includes the initial 6 g solvent allocation; do not add both as separate full allowances. Its finished volume is not assumed to be 30 mL.

Using the owned 25% w/w Ambrox solution would require **4.3200 g stock** for the same 1.0800 g Ambrox, bringing **3.2400 g** additional carriers. It is not the same matrix as the crystal build and invalidates the printed total. Crystal Ambrox is never assigned a fictitious µL amount.

- Minimalist standalone control in JSON tests a different architecture; do not attribute any difference solely to ingredient count.
