## Design thesis

R6 obtains depth by distributing small contributions across 63 rows. Parallel A
asks whether the same brief is clearer when it is expressed with 23 liquid
transfers plus the same single Ambrox crystal charge:

1. a larger, simpler lavender channel;
2. a warm amber-wood scaffold without the driest high-impact amber boosters;
3. two musks with deliberately different jobs;
4. a compact resin/hay shadow rather than a full balsamic stack; and
5. the same 300 mg neat-crystal Ambrox basis as R6, so the central amber dose
   is directly comparable rather than estimated from the 25% stock density.

The design is not claimed to be better before a blinded physical A/B comparison.

## ppm, ODT, and OAV contract

Every dose is convertible to finished-product active concentration once the
transferred stock density and final batch mass are recorded:

```text
active_mass_g = transferred_volume_mL x stock_density_g_per_mL x active_mass_fraction
active_ppm_w_w = active_mass_g / final_batch_mass_g x 1,000,000
```

For the neat Ambrox crystal row specifically:

```text
active_Ambrox_mass_g = 0.300 g
active_Ambrox_ppm_w_w = 0.300 g / measured_final_batch_mass_g x 1,000,000
```

The source form and nominal active charge now match R6 directly: 300 mg of neat
Ambrox Super crystals. Final-product ppm w/w still requires the measured final
batch mass; no volume-equivalent assumption is made.

Numerical OAV is intentionally withheld. A supported OAV requires a compatible
gas concentration and gas-phase threshold for the exact identity/product and
scenario. Liquid dose or liquid-phase ppm is not inserted directly into a gas
threshold equation. The natural oils require the repository's composite-natural
handling rather than a single molecular ODT. Therefore:

```text
ppm_w_w_state = HOLD_DENSITY_AND_FINAL_BATCH_MASS
gas_concentration_state = NOT_MEASURED_OR_APPLICABLY_MODELED
ODT_compatibility_state = PARTIAL_AND_IDENTITY_SCOPED
OAV_state = WITHHELD_PHASE_AND_APPLICABILITY_MISMATCH
```

## Why these materials, and why not the nearby alternatives

- **Amber woods:** Azarbre and Sandalore were chosen with Iso E Super and
  Vetiveryl Acetate for warm, rounded, transparent support. Ambermax, Amber
  Xtreme, Norlimbanol, Timberol, and Cedramber were omitted to avoid adding a
  separate dry, high-impact woody-amber spike. Clearwood, Bacdanol, Ebanol,
  cedarwood, guaiacwood, patchouli, and vetiver were omitted because their jobs
  would partly duplicate the selected four-material scaffold.
- **Musks:** Habanolide supplies the airy veil; Romandolide supplies the softer
  woody-fruity body. Ethylene Brassylate, Ambrettolide, and Exaltolide were
  omitted because a third and fourth musk would need controlled evidence of a
  nonredundant benefit.
- **Resin/hay:** Labdanum, olibanum, coumarin, and hay retain deep-amber warmth
  and late aromatic continuity. Benzoin, opoponax, tonka, tobacco, heliotropal,
  and vanilla materials were omitted to keep the result drier, less powdery,
  and less gourmand.
- **Lavender:** the two owned lavender products remain the natural center.
  Linalyl acetate and Ethyl Linalool extend them; clary sage and spike lavender
  keep the center aromatic. Free linalool, linalool oxide, geranyl acetate, and
  terpinyl acetate were omitted because the selected block already covers their
  main bridging functions.
- **Floral bridge:** regular Hedione, Hexyl Salicylate, and AIMI provide air,
  persistence, and polish. Hedione HC and the additional floral alcohols,
  acetates, ionones, mimosa, and muguet materials were omitted to prevent the
  lavender from becoming a generic floral cloud.
- **Opening:** bergamot, petitgrain, cedrat, and juniper form one dry aromatic
  opening. The pepper, cardamom, coriander, nutmeg, cypress, elemi, rosemary,
  and DHM stack was omitted so the opening reads as lavender-citrus rather than
  a separate spice accord.

## Expected sensory difference — hypotheses, not observations

- **Opening:** Parallel A should open as a single dry bergamot-petitgrain-juniper
  gesture around a larger lavender block. R6 should feel more intricate and
  spicy because it contains a much broader citrus, spice, conifer, and resin
  transition.
- **Heart:** Parallel A should show the two lavender products more directly,
  with Hedione used as space rather than a major floral volume. R6 should be
  more blended, floral, and texturally complex, with a greater risk of obscuring
  the exact lavender outline.
- **Drydown:** Parallel A should be smoother and more linear—Ambrox, Azarbre,
  Sandalore, polished vetiver, and two clean musks. R6 should have a darker,
  more resinous, woody, and musky drydown with more internal movement.
- **Sweetness:** Parallel A should be noticeably drier because it removes
  benzoin, tonka, opoponax, tobacco, heliotropal, and the broader floral stack.
- **Potential advantage:** clearer concept, fewer measuring errors, faster
  compounding, and easier omission testing.
- **Potential disadvantage:** less plushness, fewer micro-transitions, and a
  greater chance of feeling skeletal if the simplified woods do not adequately
  cushion the high lavender load.

## Decision

Neither formula is the winner on paper. Parallel A is the stronger **clarity and
speed challenger**; R6 is the stronger **complexity and faceting challenger**.
