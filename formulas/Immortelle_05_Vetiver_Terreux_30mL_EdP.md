# Immortelle Vétiver Terreux — 30mL EdP

## Character
A dark, earthy composition where Immortelle Absolute's golden curry-honey warmth provides an unexpected luminous counterpoint to the deep, smoky-earthy vetiver base. The vetiver axis (Indian ruh khus + Vetival + Nagarmotha) creates a multi-faceted earth register — rooty, smoky, suede-dry — while immortelle floats above like a golden haze. Patchouli and Cedarwood reinforce the woody-earth structure; Labdanum bridges the immortelle's honeyed warmth to the vetiver's smoky dryness. The result is less "green vetiver" (Guerlain Vetiver) and more "dark earth with golden light" — vetiver as soil, immortelle as the sun on that soil. Think *Lalique Encre Noire* reimagined with a Corsican heart — darker, earthier, but with unexpected warmth radiating from within.

**Family**: Woody Vetiver — vetiver-patchouli-cedar earth base, immortelle-labdanum golden heart, bergamot-grapefruit clean top

## Material Justification

- **Immortelle Absolute (10%)**: The golden halo — 180 µL of 10% = 18 µL active (0.3% of concentrate). At modifier level, it creates a luminous contrast against the dark vetiver earth. The curry-honey-maple character floats above the rooty-smoky base like late afternoon sun on dark soil — the composition's central creative idea. Without immortelle, this is just another vetiver; with it, the vetiver-earth becomes a landscape with lighting.
- **Vetiver EO India (250 µL)**: The primary earth character — Indian ruh khus vetiver is deeper, more complex, and smokier than standard Vetiver EO. At 250 µL neat, it dominates the base register. Its natural smoky-rooty-woody profile is the "dark soil" foundation. Chosen as the primary vetiver for its richness — standard Vetiver EO would be too thin for this composition.
- **Vetival (120 µL) + Vetikon (80 µL)**: Vetiver facet expansion. Vetival adds suede-vetiver dryness — the textural, leathery dimension of vetiver that Vetiver EO India's smoky character doesn't fully capture. Vetikon provides the cleaner, woody-vetiver ketone facet. Together with the natural EO, they create a three-dimensional vetiver: smoky (India), suede-dry (Vetival), clean-woody (Vetikon).
- **Nagarmortha Oil (40 µL)**: Cypriol-profile earthy-smoky bridge — extends the vetiver's smoky-earthy character deeper and darker. Functions as an "oud substitute" at a fraction of the cost, adding the dark, almost medicinal-earthy depth that makes the vetiver read as "ancient soil." At 40 µL, it's a powerful accent — too much would shift toward Middle Eastern oud profiles.
- **Patchouli EO (150 µL) + Clearwood (100 µL)**: Dual patchouli for earth reinforcement. Patchouli EO provides the classic camphoraceous-earthy darkness that deepens the vetiver's soil character. Clearwood adds a cleaner, more modern earth — the bright side of patchouli that prevents the base from becoming monochromatically dark. Together they create an earth register that feels natural, not muddy.
- **Labdanum Resinoid (10%)**: 150 µL of 10% = 15 µL active. The golden-resinous bridge between immortelle and vetiver. Labdanum's warm amber-leather complexity connects immortelle's honey to vetiver's smoke — without it, the two would read as separate layers rather than an integrated accord. At 15 µL active, it's present as a binder, not a character note.
- **Coumarin (20%)**: 80 µL of 20% = 16 µL active. Hay-sweet tonka that pairs with immortelle (proven synergy) and adds a sun-dried-grass sweetness to the earthy base. Prevents the vetiver-earth from becoming purely dark and oppressive — the coumarin is the olfactory equivalent of dried grasses in earth, keeping it natural and breathing.
- **Oakmoss Absolute (10%) + Evernyl (50%)**: Mossy-lichen layer at restrained doses. Oakmoss at 10 µL active provides a bitter-forest-floor nuance that enriches the vetiver earth. Evernyl at 50 µL active adds synthetic mossy body. Both at moderate doses — this is a vetiver, not a chypre, but the moss adds essential complexity to the earth register.
- **Cedarwood EO (200 µL) + Timberol (80 µL)**: Cedar axis. Cedarwood EO provides warm, natural cedar identity — the wood that grows alongside vetiver grass. Timberol adds dry-architectural cedar precision — the "structure" that gives the woods definition. Together they create a cedar frame around the vetiver heart.
- **Iso E Super (550 µL)**: Molecular cocoon — volume and skin-adhesion without identifiable wood character. Creates the "space" that the vetiver occupies. Standard dose for a modern woody composition — enough to create the anosmic halo effect without dominating.
- **Vertofix (160 µL) + Azarbre (80 µL) + Cedramber (80 µL)**: Woody-amber bridge. Vertofix glues the wood layer to the musk layer — essential in vetiver compositions where the transition from rooty-earth to clean-musk can feel abrupt. Azarbre adds cedar-amber warmth (warmer than Ambrox, complementing the dark-earth character). Cedramber bridges cedar and amber registers.
- **Kephalis (30 µL)**: Powerful woody-amber structural accent — a few µL adds backbone and tenacity to the vetiver base. Its tobacco-iris undertone aligns with the dark-complexity of the composition. Kept at trace — Kephalis easily dominates if overdosed.
- **Ambrofix (30% w/v)**: 60 µL of 30% = 18 µL active. Clean amber sparkle at restrained dose — adds a touch of mineral brightness that prevents the dark-earth base from becoming flat. The crystalline effect creates a sense of "air above the soil."
- **Bergamot FCF (300 µL) + Grapefruit FCF (120 µL) + Cedrat FCF Sicilian (80 µL)**: Triple-citrus clean opening. Bergamot provides the classic bright top. Grapefruit adds bitter-clean pink-pith dryness — the most vetiver-compatible citrus, pairing naturally with woody-earthy accords. Cedrat adds bitter citron edge for sharpness. Three citrus materials justified because this is a vetiver — the genre demands a significant citrus contrast against the dark base. Chosen over Blood Orange (too sweet-juicy for vetiver-earth) and Red Mandarin (too warm-gourmand).
- **Black Pepper EO (30 µL)**: Dry-spice sparkle — the pepper cuts through the vetiver density at the top, adding a piquant brightness that bridges the citrus to the earthy heart. The dryness aligns with vetiver's own dry character.
- **Hedione HC (450 µL) + Hedione (250 µL)**: Radiance amplifier pair. Hedione expands the immortelle's golden warmth outward while lifting the vetiver's heaviness. Without Hedione, the dark-earth base would feel oppressive and closed; with it, the composition breathes. 700 µL total is appropriate for a composition that needs projection against dense earth notes.
- **Romandolide (160 µL)**: Projection musk — diffusive woody-musk that extends the vetiver's character into sillage. Chosen over Habanolide (too warm-intimate for a vetiver that should project) and over Zenolide (too clean-fresh — would fight vetiver's earthiness). Romandolide's slightly woody character aligns with the vetiver-cedar axis.
- **Ethylene Brassylate (200 µL)**: Depth musk — creamy-lactonic skin-sit that anchors the volatile vetiver top facets. The lactonic softness prevents the vetiver's harsh-smoky edge from becoming scratchy on skin. At 200 µL, it's substantial enough to hold the large vetiver dose.
- **Ambrettolide (10% in DPG)**: Character-echo musk — 80 µL of 10% = 8 µL active. Naturalistic fruity-musky warmth that bridges immortelle's honeyed character to the vetiver at the skin. The "golden soil" drydown — where vetiver's earth meets immortelle's sun, Ambrettolide whispers the memory of that meeting.
- **Hexyl Salicylate (150 µL) + Benzyl Benzoate (130 µL)**: Dual fixative. Hexyl Salicylate provides a light, green-floral transparent fixative that creates adhesion for the citrus-aromatic top. Benzyl Benzoate provides invisible molecular-weight fixation for the vetiver base. Chosen over Benzyl Salicylate (too cosmetic-warm — would contradict vetiver's raw earthiness).
- **Geranium EO (30 µL)**: Trace rose-geranium freshness — adds a subtle minty-rosy lift at the heart that prevents the immortelle-vetiver transition from feeling heavy. Just enough to add complexity without introducing a floral register.
