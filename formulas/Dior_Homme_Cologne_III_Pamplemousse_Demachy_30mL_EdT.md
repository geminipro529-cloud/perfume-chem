## Why Grapefruit, Why Now

Demachy rejected grapefruit for DHC II. Too meta, he said — grapefruit citrus plus grapefruit
blossom makes a fragrance about grapefruit. He was right for DHC II. But for DHC III, the
brief is different: make something that smells good to most people on first sniff. The
single-plant narrative that was too conceptual for DHC II becomes the emotional core of DHC III.

Most people don't think about grapefruit blossom. They've never smelled it. What they know is
grapefruit — the breakfast fruit, the pink half with sugar on top, the sharp-bitter-clean scent
that means morning, energy, freshness. When that familiar opening gives way to something softer
and greener — but recognizably from the same plant — the reaction isn't intellectual ("clever
concept") but visceral ("that's beautiful, what is it?").

The grapefruit rind-to-blossom transition is the fragrance's secret weapon. At first spray: that
zing of pink grapefruit, bright and cheerful — everyone recognizes it, everyone likes it. Five
minutes later: the bitterness softens, a green-floral glow emerges. It's still grapefruit, but
transformed — the way orange blossom relates to orange, but subtler because grapefruit blossom
is almost unknown in perfumery. The wearer's friends say "you smell amazing." The wearer's
partner says "you smell like you." Neither can identify the blossom as grapefruit — they just
know it works.

## Accord Architecture

### Layer 1 — Grapefruit Rind (Top, ~29% of concentrate)
Grapefruit FCF at 22% of concentrate. Clean, bitter-pink, the smell of a grapefruit being peeled
in a bright kitchen. Linalyl Acetate at 7% bridges the citrus to the blossom — grapefruit oil
contains linalyl acetate naturally, so this reinforcement reads as an extension of the rind's
own character, not a bergamot transplant. One citrus, unadorned. No aldehydes. No lemon. No
companion citrus. The grapefruit smells exactly like grapefruit — and that's the point.

### Layer 2 — Grapefruit Blossom, Full Bloom (Heart, ~43% of concentrate)
This is where DHC III earns its name. Petitgrain EO at 8% — the green skeleton of the blossom.
Paradisamide at 0.4% active — the tropical-grapefruit blossom character, the same molecule
that gave DHC I and II their signature. Ethyl Linalool at 9% — the extended bridge that carries
the grapefruit's natural freshness into the blossom and beyond. Hedione at 20% + Hedione HC at
2.4% — the radiance backbone that makes the blossom bloom outward rather than sit on skin.

The heart is the largest layer by design (43% of concentrate vs 28% base). This is a fragrance
about the blossom — the rind is the introduction, but the blossom is the destination. For the
first time in the DHC line, the heart outranks the base in volume.

### Layer 3 — Structured Musk, Restrained (Base, ~28% of concentrate)
The same OAV-balanced base from DHC II: Romandolide at 10% (projection, arm's length),
Ambrofix at 0.78% active (crystalline pillar, present but not dominant), Norlimbanol at 0.5%
(longevity scaffold), Vetival at 0.5% (suede-vetiver micro-texture). Four musks on three axes.
The base serves the blossom — it provides longevity and projection without demanding attention.
After hour 3, when the grapefruit rind has faded and the blossom begins to thin, the structured
musk rises just enough to say "I'm still here" — clean, warm, textured, but never loud.

## Maceration

Store cool and dark. Invert gently once per week.
**Minimum 4 weeks.** Grapefruit FCF integrates faster than cedrat and slightly faster than bergamot
(less terpenic complexity). The expanded heart needs the full time — hedione at 20% and ethyl
linalool at 9% take 2-3 weeks to cohere with the petitgrain-paradisamide blossom accord.
Norlimbanol + Vetival still need 3-4 weeks for full integration.

## Why Grapefruit at 22% (Not 28% Like Bergamot)

Grapefruit FCF has ODT 1.0 ppm — 33% lower than bergamot's 1.5 ppm, meaning it's 50% more
perceptible per unit volume. At 1100 µL (22% of concentrate), grapefruit achieves OAV ~36,700
in final product — already higher than bergamot's 31,111 at 1400 µL. Pushing grapefruit to
28% would push OAV past 46,000, making the opening aggressively bitter rather than cheerfully
bright. The 22% dose hits the sweet spot: unmistakably grapefruit, immediately appealing,
never punishing.

## The Key Materials, Recontextualized

### Petitgrain EO at 8% (up from 7% in DHC I/II)
With grapefruit rind as the opening, petitgrain isn't just providing a generic green-floral
bridge — it's providing the GREEN STAGE of the grapefruit blossom accord. At 8%, it's the
loudest it's ever been in the DHC line, and it works because the context has shifted: petitgrain
is no longer a bridge between unrelated materials (bergamot → grapefruit blossom), it IS the
blossom structure itself.

### Ethyl Linalool at 9% (way up from 6-7%)
With grapefruit's more volatile, less tenacious opening (grapefruit oil is higher in limonene,
lower in linalyl acetate than bergamot), the bridge needs to work harder. Ethyl Linalool at
9% extends the grapefruit's freshness into the heart and keeps the connection alive through
hour 4-5. This is the highest dose in any DHC formula, and it's the material that makes the
rind-to-blossom transition seamless rather than abrupt.

### Hedione at 20% (up from 19%)
The expanded heart needs more radiance. At 20%, hedione amplifies the grapefruit blossom
without adding a single identifiable floral note — it just makes the blossom bloom outward,
reaching the nose of someone standing across the room who says "what smells so good?"

### Linalyl Acetate at 7% (up from 5%)
Grapefruit oil naturally contains less linalyl acetate than bergamot (~2-5% vs ~20-30%).
Adding LA at 7% brings the grapefruit opening closer to the structural bridge level that
petitgrain and ethyl linalool expect. Without this boost, the opening reads as "grapefruit
juice" (acidic, sharp) rather than "grapefruit perfume" (balanced, refined). This is the
single most important adjustment for making grapefruit work as a solitary citrus — it
civilizes the rind.

## OAV Layer Balance

| Layer | OAV sum (final product) | % of total |
|-------|------------------------|------------|
| TOP (grapefruit + LA) | 36,667 + 1,458 = 38,125 | 30.4% |
| HEART (hedione + petitgrain + ethyl linalool + paradisamide + HC) | 6,667 + 5,333 + 10,000 + 6,667 + 1,333 = 30,000 | 23.9% |
| BASE (musks + ambrofix + norlimbanol + vetival) | 16,667 + 6,667 + 4,167 + 26,000 + 333 + 8,333 + 1,667 = 57,167 | 45.6% |

The base is still the OAV leader (7 materials vs top's 2), but the heart+top combined
(54.3%) now exceeds the base (45.6%). For the first time in the DHC line, the citrus-blossom
complex carries more olfactory weight than the structured musk — and that's what makes DHC III
smell so good on first sniff. The grapefruit opens, the blossom blooms, and the musk supports
rather than competes. This is the DHC architecture, fully realized.
