## Name and Olfactive Target

**Fourfold Ember** is a dry, unisex temple-resin perfume: a narrow resinous lift above luminous frankincense, a bitter medicinal myrrh shadow, a cinnamic-smoky storax bridge, and a restrained benzoin ember held inside dry timber and root.

The intended image is heated stone, old timber, rising incense, bitter resin, and a dim balsamic glow. It must not drift into vanilla amber, gourmand sweetness, modern amberwood, campfire tar, leather, laundry musk, or a generic pile of resins.

### Identity recognizers

1. A vertical, luminous incense plane.
2. A dark medicinal-balsamic counterplane.
3. A continuous transition from bright resin to dark resin to late balsamic warmth.
4. A dry timber/root frame that preserves separation between the four tinctures.

### Excluded takeovers

- No vanillin, ethyl vanillin, ethyl maltol, maple lactone, coumarin, Tonkarome, or extra benzoin: these would convert the name into sweet amber or gourmand.
- No Olibanum Resinoid or Myrrh EO in the primary design: they would duplicate and potentially conceal the two tinctures whose behavior must be learned.
- No Ambrox Super, Ambermax, Amberwood F, Norlimbanol, large Iso E Super structure, or musk chord: these would give persistence by imposing a modern woody-amber identity.
- No birch tar, cade, IBQ, castoreum, or tobacco: the target is heated resin and stone, not campfire, leather, animalic warmth, or smoke saturation.
- No floral heart: the four tinctures must own the subject before any floral counterpoint is considered.

## Chemical-Compatibility Ceiling

The current literature supports two provisional chemistry islands, not a bottle-proven four-way accord:

- Benzoin and storax plausibly overlap in aromatic benzoate/cinnamate, benzenoid, and balsamic chemistry.
- Myrrh and frankincense plausibly overlap in terpenoid and sesquiterpenoid chemistry, conditional on the unresolved species and extraction profiles.
- Cross-island overlap is not established for the four current bottles.

Therefore the whole four-tincture combination remains `HOLD`. Shared marketing language such as “resinous” or “incense” does not prove constituent compatibility, physical stability, or olfactive coherence.

## Required Unlock Evidence

For each tincture, bind the following to the exact physical bottle:

1. Supplier, product name, lot, country/source, and botanical species where available.
2. Exact meaning of the declared percentage: w/w, w/v, v/v, or original resin charge relative to which final mass or volume.
3. Ethanol strength and any water or co-solvent content.
4. Starting resin mass, starting solvent mass/volume, extraction time and temperature, and whether agitation or heat was used.
5. Filtration method, retained/discarded solids, and final tincture mass or volume after filtration and evaporation losses.
6. Stock density at a stated temperature, or a reproducible gravimetric conversion for the actual bottle.
7. Clarity, haze, sediment, phase separation, and behavior after dilution in the intended final ethanol strength.
8. Supplier SDS/COA/IFRA certificate and allergen declaration, if they exist.
9. Bottle-bound compositional evidence sufficient for a composite natural-mixture OAV model; published essential-oil profiles are not interchangeable with ethanol-tincture composition.

An evaporative-residue result may help quantify nonvolatile extracted solids, but it does not by itself provide volatile composition, ODT, composite OAV, or dermal safety.

## Controlled Evaluation Plan After Unlock

The first physical work should be a small, carrier-matched, blinded diagnostic set rather than a 30 mL bottle:

1. Establish an all-four parent arm.
2. Prepare four single-omission arms: minus frankincense, minus myrrh, minus storax, and minus benzoin.
3. Replace each omitted tincture with an equal amount of its verified carrier so total mass/volume and ethanol strength remain constant.
4. Keep support materials, stock lots, preparation order, container, maturation, spray hardware, and evaluation windows identical.
5. Randomize labels and presentation order. Use at least two independent preparations.
6. Record visual stability separately from smell: immediate clarity, 24-hour haze/sediment, one-week stability, and phase separation.
7. Record free description before scoring. Then score identity, resin separation, bright-to-dark transition, sweetness, smoke, medicinal harshness, muddiness, diffusion, and liking as separate axes.
8. Retain a tincture only when its omission causes a repeatable, target-linked loss that is not explainable as a generic change in total odor mass.

The screen is not permission to infer similarity, safety, stability, preference, longevity, or release success.

## Primary and Official Evidence Anchors

- FAO/JECFA, *Benzoe tonkinensis*: the named resin is ethanol-soluble and is characterized by benzoic acid, coniferyl benzoate, vanillin, and benzyl benzoate, but the specification excludes Sumatra benzoin and does not identify the current bottle: <https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/jecfa_additives/docs/monograph16/additive-522-m16.pdf>
- Hovaneissian et al. (2006): styrax, storax, and benzoin names have important botanical and chemical ambiguity; analytical characterization is needed to relate trade names to composition: <https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/chimie/articles/10.1016/j.crci.2005.12.010/>
- Al-Harrasi and Al-Saidi (2008): botanically certified Omani *Boswellia sacra* hydrodistilled oil was monoterpene-rich, with E-beta-ocimene and limonene prominent; this is essential-oil evidence, not a tincture assay: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18830149/>
- Alqahtani et al. (2019): commercial *Commiphora myrrha* samples showed substantial furanosesquiterpenoid variability; a generic myrrh label cannot supply a fixed active profile: <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31997905/>
