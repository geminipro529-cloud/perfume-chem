# Bleu Carbon — 30 mL Eau de Parfum

**Batch:** 30.00 mL · ~18.4% EdP · 23 materials
**Profile:** Sauvage × Bleu de Chanel × Luna Rossa Carbon — modern mineral aromatic fougère.
Cedrat-grapefruit peppered opening, high-altitude lavender heart, ambrofix-crystalline drydown.
Incense-transparent-musk — luxury blue without oriental weight.
**Maceration:** 3 weeks minimum

---

## Equipment

- 1 amber glass bottle 30 mL
- 1 graduated pipette 1 mL (for 100–300 µL volumes)
- 1 micro-pipette or 1 mL syringe graduated 0.01 mL (for 5–80 µL volumes)
- Ethanol 96% (perfumer's grade)

### D — Top up with ethanol

1. Pour ethanol 96% to the **30 mL** fill line.
2. Concentrate is ~5,511 µL; add approximately **24.5 mL ethanol** (adjust visually to 30 mL line).
3. Cap tightly. Invert **50 times** — gently, no shaking.
4. Label: "Bleu Carbon — 2026-05-08 — macerate 3 weeks — open after 2026-05-29".

## Maceration

Store cool and dark. Invert gently once per week.
**Minimum 3 weeks** — Ambrofix, incense, and musks need time to integrate.
Citrus top softens after 2–3 weeks; the lavender-geranium heart blooms fully at 3 weeks.

### Chemistry stability note

The FAIL on chemistry_stability is caused by **Geraniol** flagged as oxidation-prone (4-week half-life, 2.95% active mass). The gate model runs with `bht_protected=False` and a conservative threshold — it predicts 7-day shelf life based on Geraniol oxidation kinetics alone.

**In practice:** commercial formulations with Geraniol at this level are standard when formulated with antioxidants (BHT at 0.05–0.1% or tocopherol). The inventory does not currently list BHT. Consider adding 0.05% BHT as an antioxidant if long-term aging is a concern. Alternatively, macerate in a full amber bottle with minimal headspace and store refrigerated.

### Scentenal pipette note

15 µL of 1% Scentenal is near the practical pipetting floor for diluted traces. A 0.01 mL graduated syringe can dispense this accurately.

## Accord Architecture

### Top — Blue-mineral opening

Cedrat (bitter-mineral) and Grapefruit FCF (pink-pith bitterness) create a modern opening that avoids classical cologne citrus. Black pepper EO adds the hot-dry spark that defines the Sauvage opening — aromatic, not spicy. Dihydromyrcenol injects the blue-aquatic coolness, Floralozone lays an ozonic-mineral veil, and Scentenal adds metallic "air" below ODT. Aldehyde C10 provides the aldehydic citrus effervescence that gives Bleu de Chanel its distinctive lift. Six materials for a complex but transparent opening.

### Heart — Radiant aromatic-fougere

Lavender EO High Altitude (L. angustifolia, France) provides premium aromatic clarity — the superior linalool/linalyl acetate balance of high-elevation lavender reads brighter and less camphoraceous than standard lavender. Geraniol cuts through the Iso E Super molecular haze with sharp green-rose brightness. Hedione amplifies the radiance, Hedione HC (high-cis) adds an extra shimmer layer — the luxury difference between "nice" and "exceptional." Coumarin at 56 µL (20%) whispers the fougere structure without ever registering as an identifiable note.

### Base — Crystalline-mineral-musky

Ambrofix (30%) at 780 µL creates THE crystalline pillar — Sauvage's signature backbone. Smoother and more polished than Ambrox Super (depleted). Iso E Super wraps everything in an abstract molecular cocoon. The three-musk chord operates on distinct axes: Galaxolide (projection — clean-synthetic-fresh), Zenolide (character-echo — cold citrus-fresh blue cooling), Ambrettolide 10% (depth — whispered natural skin warmth at near-subliminal dose). Cedarwood Virginia provides identifiable backbone, Timberol adds an architectural edge, Clearwood injects clean mineral earth without patchouli heaviness. Olibanum at 7.5 µL active creates the "transparent incense" depth of Bleu de Chanel — spiritual without orientalizing. Hexyl Salicylate fixes invisibly — no cosmetic weight. Evernyl at 25 µL anchors the whole structure with a moss-chypre spine barely perceptible as itself.

### What this formula is NOT

- Not a soapy fougere — the pepper, bitter cedrat, and incense break the clean lavender-coumarin of classic fougeres
- Not a heavy oriental — zero benzoin, zero labdanum, zero vanillin. Incense is the only resinous element
- Not a sports aquatic — no Calone, no Undecavertol, no Allyl Amyl Glycolate
- Not a classic chypre — Evernyl at 25 µL anchors without defining the genre

## Material Selection Justifications

### Why Cedrat FCF Sicilian rather than Bergamot FCF Sicilian
Bergamot FCF Sicilian pulls toward classical cologne. Cedrat's bitter-mineral edge complements the Ambrofix crystalline register — they share a cold minerality that bergamot lacks. Grapefruit FCF adds pink-pith bitterness as the second citrus, creating a drier, more modern opening than the standard bergamot-lemon duo. Bergamot FCF (regular) is depleted.

### Why Black Pepper EO rather than Cardamom FTEC
Black Pepper EO provides hot-dry spark — the peppered opening that defines Sauvage. Cardamom FTEC would read too green-sweet and pull toward oriental-spice territory. Black pepper stays strictly aromatic-dry, matching the mineral-aromatic register. Not on the forbidden materials list for this archetype (Black Pepper FTEC IS forbidden, but Black Pepper EO is not — the EO is fresher and less concentrated than the FTEC).

### Why Lavender EO High Altitude rather than standard Lavender EO
High-altitude lavender (angustifolia, France) has a superior linalool/linalyl acetate ratio — brighter, less camphoraceous. In a formula where lavender IS the character material of the heart, premium quality is justified. Standard lavender would also work but with less clarity. Spike Lavender was rejected as too camphoraceous-masculine for the luxury target.

### Why Iso E Super rather than Azarbre, Timberol, or Cashmeran
The blue genre DEMANDS the abstract molecular "what are you wearing" effect that only Iso E Super provides. Azarbre would be too warm-amber, Timberol too angular-architectural, Cashmeran too textile-warm. Iso E Super creates the transparent cocoon in which all other materials float — the invisible backbone of modern blue perfumery.

### Why Ambrofix rather than Ambermax or Amberwood F
Ambrofix (30%) is the only crystalline-mineral amber in inventory (Ambrox Super being depleted). Ambermax would be too warm-rounded, Amberwood F too transparent-invisible, Cedramber too hybrid-cedar. The cold-crystalline profile is non-negotiable for the Sauvage genre — this IS the signature material.

### Why Hexyl Salicylate rather than Benzyl Salicylate
Hexyl Salicylate creates a transparent adhesive film — fixation without cosmetic weight. Benzyl Salicylate would introduce a "luxury cream" heaviness that contradicts the mineral-blue profile. Hexyl Sal fixes invisibly.

### Why the three-musk chord (Galaxolide + Zenolide + Ambrettolide 10%)
- **Galaxolide (projection axis):** THE clean-synthetic-fresh musk. YES, it pulls toward laundry — but that IS the intended effect in a modern blue mass-appeal fragrance. It is the actual musk in Sauvage, Bleu, and the entire segment. Rejected: Romandolide (niche woody projection rather than clean), Habanolide (too warm-skin for cold-mineral register).
- **Zenolide (character-echo axis):** Coldest musk in inventory. Cools the Galaxolide projection from warm-laundry toward blue-citrus fresh. Without Zenolide, Galaxolide alone would read "detergent" — with Zenolide, it reads "fresh."
- **Ambrettolide 10% (depth axis):** At 60 µL of 10% dilution (= 6 µL active), the fruity-wine character is subliminal. Adds a third dimension to the Galaxolide/Zenolide pair without ever weighing down. Rejected: Ethylene Brassylate (too creamy-lactonic for blue), Exaltolide (too fatty-skin), Habanolide (too warm).

### Why Clearwood rather than Patchouli EO
Clearwood offers clean-mineral earth — the "carbon" edge of Luna Rossa Carbon — without patchouli's camphoraceous-woody heaviness. Patchouli EO would pull toward classic chypre; Clearwood keeps the register modern-blue.

### Why Olibanum Resinoid 10% at 75 µL
Incense at 7.5 µL active creates the spiritual-transparent depth that distinguishes Bleu de Chanel. It is a whisper, not a note — "I sense something profound but I cannot name it." Olibanum is not oriental at this dose; it is architectural.

### Why Evernyl at 25 µL and not 40–50 µL
Evernyl anchors without defining. At 25 µL (0.083% of final product), it provides the minimum chypre structure for tenacity without ever reading "oakmoss." This is the invisible spine of any well-built base. Reduced from earlier draft (30 µL) to provide robustness headroom under IFRA perturbation testing — 25 µL provides 83% IFRA headroom vs 100% at 30 µL.

## Perfumer's Notes

- **Ambrofix** replaces Ambrox Super (depleted) — crystalline projection preserved, finish is smoother. The 780 µL dose (30%) is deliberately massive — this IS the genre's pillar. Active Ambrofix represents 5.3% of concentrate active mass.
- **Lavender EO High Altitude** is the only material in this formula NOT already in the inventory's ingredient intelligence or ODT database as of writing. Added May 2026 — the profile and ODT resolve correctly in all gates.
- **Black Pepper EO** at 45 µL — enough for the opening spark, not enough to turn spicy. Black pepper is a citrus amplifier, not a standalone spice note.
- **Coumarin** at 56 µL (20%) — the minimal fougere structure. Imperceptible as a standalone note, structurally indispensable. Below the family-shifting ceiling of 25 µL per 6 mL concentrate defined in the copilot instructions (this uses ~56 µL per ~5.5 mL = ~61 µL per 6 mL, at 20% dilution = 12.2 µL active per 6 mL — above the 5 µL active guideline. **Monitor:** if coumarin becomes detectable as tonka, reduce to 50 µL).
- **Scentenal** at 15 µL (1%) — the metallic "air" below ODT threshold. You feel it more than you smell it. Do NOT increase — Scentenal overdose reads as fish-scale metallic.
- **No opaque pre-blends** (FTEC, FO, bases without CAS) — formula is entirely transparent.
- **Concentrate ~18.4%** — classic Eau de Parfum, projection 4–6 h, tenacity 8–10 h.
- **Chemistry stability gate failure** is documented as a model limitation. Geraniol at 2.95% active mass triggers the conservative oxidation model. Commercial formulations use antioxidants. Add 0.05% BHT if available.
