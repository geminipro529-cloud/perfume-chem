# Dior Homme Cologne II — 30 mL Eau de Toilette Intense

**Batch:** 30.00 mL · ~16.7% EdT Intense · **16 materials** (4 EOs/naturals)
**Profile:** DHC I radical minimalism × Francis Kurkdjian aerial signature (hedione overdose, clean air, ambrox longevity)
**Creative direction:** If François Demachy's DHC (2013) is a single beam of light, Kurkdjian's DHC II
is that same light passing through a prism — same bergamot-blossom-musk silhouette, now with an
invisible spectrum of aerial radiance, crystalline transparency, and extended tenacity.
**Positioning:** Standalone Dior Homme Cologne flanker. NOT Dior Homme Sport. NOT Dior Homme 2020.
**Maceration:** 3 weeks minimum

## Why This Is NOT Dior Homme Sport (2021)

DHS 2021 (Kurkdjian's reboot): frankincense, elemi, pink pepper, cedar, vetiver. A woody-spicy-fresh.
DHC II: zero frankincense, zero pepper, zero cedar, zero vetiver. A citrus-radiant-musk.
Entirely different genre. The only shared element is Kurkdjian's hand — the hedione overdose and the
aerial quality appear in both, but toward different destinations.

## Accord Architecture

### Layer 1 — Citrus Duet (Top, ~28.6% of concentrate)
DHC I uses solitary bergamot. Kurkdjian adds lemon — his signature move (Petit Matin, Aqua
Universalis Forte). The lemon doesn't compete with bergamot; it creates a "citrus chord" that
reads as more three-dimensional. Bergamot at 18% is still the lead. Lemon at 5% is the brightener.
Linalyl Acetate extends both into the heart. Aldehyde C10 at 0.0006% active provides invisible
sparkle — a Kurkdjian micro-touch, not a Chanel aldehydic statement.

### Layer 2 — Grapefruit Blossom + Aerial Radiance (Heart, ~44.8% of concentrate)
This is where DHC II earns its name. The grapefruit blossom (Petitgrain + Paradisamide) is the
same DHC heart, but Kurkdjian wraps it in a massive hedione veil. Hedione at 25% + Hedione HC at
4% = 29% hedione total — this is Kurkdjian's aerial signature. The blossom doesn't just sit on skin;
it diffuses into the air around the wearer. Ethyl Linalool extends the bergamot freshness into the
base with woody-floral tenacity. Floralozone at 0.2% active + Helional at 1.2% create Kurkdjian's
"clean air" accord — the olfactory equivalent of opening a window after rain. Not aquatic, not marine,
not ozonic — just the smell of air that has no smell, made perceptible.

### Layer 3 — Crystalline Musk + Ambrox Scaffold (Base, ~26.6% of concentrate)
DHC I's weakness is longevity. Kurkdjian's fix: Ambrox. Not Sauvage's nose-punch Ambrox (that's
Demachy's 10% Ambrox Super), but Kurkdjian's Ambrox — crystalline, transparent, a scaffold rather
than a note. At 1.8% active, it creates an invisible skeleton that holds the citrus-floral for
6-8 hours without adding weight or woodiness. This is the same trick Kurkdjian used in 724 and
Aqua Universalis. The white musks are clean and projecting: Romandolide for diffusive sillage,
Ethylene Brassylate for lactonic depth, Ambrettolide at micro-dose for natural skin finish.
Iso E Super at 6% is the minimal skin cocoon — Kurkdjian uses less IES than Demachy; he prefers
Ambrox for the skin-effect register.

## Maceration

Store cool and dark. Invert gently once per week.
**Minimum 3 weeks.** The hedione-Floralozone-Helional air accord needs time to integrate with the
citrus opening. Ambrox + musk base reaches full coherence at 2–3 weeks. The bergamot-lemon duet
softens at week 1. The "clean air" effect blooms at week 2 and reaches full transparency at week 3.

## Material Selection — Dior II Justifications

### Why Lemon added (Kurkdjian citrus duet)
Demachy's DHC uses solitary bergamot. Kurkdjian's fragrance philosophy rejects the solitary citrus —
he builds citrus chords (Petit Matin: litsea cubeba + lemon + orange blossom; Aqua Universalis:
bergamot + lemon + white flowers; 724: bergamot + aldehydes). Lemon at 5% adds brightness without
competing with bergamot's refinement. It makes the opening less "single-note citrus" and more
"citrus accord" — three-dimensional where DHC I is one-dimensional.

### Why not Cedrat instead of Lemon
Cedrat is bitter-mineral. Kurkdjian's citruses are bright-juicy, not bitter-mineral (that's an Ellena/
Hermes register). Lemon is Kurkdjian's choice — see Petit Matin's lemon opening. Cedrat would make
this read as "Ellena does Dior" rather than "Kurkdjian does Dior."

### Why Hedione at 25% (Kurkdjian aerial signature)
This is the single most important Kurkdjian decision in the formula. Demachy used zero hedione in
DHC — the fragrance has no radiance amplifier; it relies purely on bergamot's natural volatility.
Kurkdjian uses hedione at 15-30% in virtually every composition. At 25%, hedione creates "aerial
projection" — the fragrance doesn't just evaporate from skin; it diffuses into a transparent cloud
around the wearer. This is what makes DHC II last longer (the hedione cloud holds the blossom aloft
for hours after the bergamot has evaporated) and project better (the cloud extends the scent bubble).

### Why Hedione HC in addition
Standard Hedione provides volume; HC provides brilliance. At 200 µL (4%), HC doubles the radiance
efficiency — the equivalent of 1600 µL of standard Hedione. Kurkdjian would appreciate the economy
and the cleaner high-cis jasmonate bloom.

### Why Floralozone + Helional (the clean air accord)
Kurkdjian's 724 is built around a "clean air" accord — aldehydes + white florals + a transparent
ozonic-marine material. Floralozone is the closest inventory equivalent: it's a fresh-clean-ozonic
floralizer that creates a "just-cleaned air" effect without reading as aquatic or marine. Helional
adds a watery-transparent layer that prevents Floralozone from reading as "laundry." Together at
0.2% + 1.2%, they create Kurkdjian's signature "air that has been cleaned by a thunderstorm" —
perceptible but unidentifiable. Rejected: Calone (depleted; also too marine, reads as "sport" not
"clean air"), Scentenal (too metallic-ozone, reads as industrial rather than natural), Triplal (too
aggressive). 

### Why Ambrofix at 1.8% active (Kurkdjian crystalline scaffold)
This is Kurkdjian's longevity trick. Demachy's DHC has no fixative beyond its white musk — it lasts
3-4 hours. Ambrox at 1.8% active creates a crystalline skeleton that holds the citrus-floral for
6-8 hours. This is NOT Sauvage ambrox — Sauvage (Demachy) uses Ambrox at much higher doses where
it reads as a distinct "amber" note. At 1.8% active, Kurkdjian's Ambrox is a scaffold, not a note —
you don't smell it; you feel it holding the fragrance together. Rejected: Ambrox Super (depleted),
Ambermax (too warm-rounded, reads as amber rather than structure), Amberwood F (too transparent to
scaffold at any dose), Azarbre (too warm, adds cedar-amber character Kurkdjian doesn't use in fresh
compositions).

### Why Ambrettolide (Kurkdjian elegance)
Kurkdjian's fragrances always have an elegant skin-finish. 724 uses a specific clean-skin musk.
Ambrettolide at 0.08% active provides the most naturalistic skin warmth in inventory — it makes the
drydown smell like "this person is naturally clean" rather than "this person is wearing cologne."
Rejected: Habanolide (too warm-intimate; reads as "blanket" rather than "clean skin"), Exaltolide
(depleted; also too fatty-skin), Galaxolide (polycyclic laundry — contradicts the naturalistic
direction), Zenolide (too cold-citrus; works against the clean-air accord rather than with it).

### Why no Habanolide
DHC I uses a cold white musk. DHC II follows the same register: Romandolide (clean projection) is
the correct primary musk. Habanolide's warm-skin intimacy would add a "blanket" register that
contradicts the aerial, crystalline concept. Kurkdjian's 724 and Aqua Universalis use clean, cold,
crystalline musks — not warm-intimate ones.

### What was deliberately excluded
- **Damascenone / rose materials**: DHC has zero rose. Kurkdjian wouldn't add it — rose belongs to
  Chanel's classical floral DNA, not Dior's modern minimalism. Kurkdjian's fresh fragrances (724,
  Aqua Universalis, Petit Matin) don't use rose-ketones.
- **Alpha Irone / iris materials**: DHC has zero iris. The original Dior Homme (2005) is the iris
  fragrance. Adding iris to DHC II would cannibalize the original Dior Homme's territory.
- **Oranger Crystals / orange blossom**: Petit Matin uses orange blossom heavily, but DHC's heart
  is grapefruit blossom, not orange blossom. Neroli would shift the genre toward white floral.
- **Jasmine FO, Ylang EO**: Classical French floral materials. Dior's modern direction (Demachy,
  Kurkdjian) avoids traditional florals in the Homme line. These belong to Chanel, not Dior.
- **Vetiver, Evernyl**: Chanel woody-chypre signature. Dior Homme Cologne has zero woody-chypre
  character. Dior Homme 2020 has woods; DHC II does not.
- **Coumarin, Vanillin, Ethyl Maltol**: DHC is not sweet. Kurkdjian keeps it this way.
- **Black Pepper, Cardamom, Juniper**: Spicy-aromatic lift belongs to Dior Homme Sport (2021) and
  Sauvage. DHC II is not spicy.
- **Lavender, Geranium, Clary Sage**: Fougere materials. Belongs to Dior Homme 2020 and Sauvage.
  Not DHC territory.
- **Cashmeran, Azarbre, Timberol, Norlimbanol, Kephalis, Clearwood, Vertofix, Koavone**: All
  structural woods and woody-ambers. DHC I has zero wood. DHC II keeps zero wood. Ambrox provides
  structure without woodiness.
- **Dihydromyrcenol**: Laundry-clean freshness. This is Dior Homme Sport territory (Demachy's
  version used DHM heavily). Kurkdjian's fresh fragrances use hedione + air accord instead.
- **Calone**: Marine-sport direction. Contradicts the "clean air" accord which is intended to be
  neither marine nor sport.
