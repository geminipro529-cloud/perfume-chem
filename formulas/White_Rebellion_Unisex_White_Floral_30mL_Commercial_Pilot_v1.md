# White Rebellion — Unisex White Floral — 30 mL Commercial Pilot v1

**Status:** Commercial-intent pilot design; not a production master formula and not yet physically compounded, stability-tested, safety-assessed, or smelled  
**Concept:** A direct competitor in the narcotic orange-blossom, tuberose, and apricot-osmanthus territory of Good girl gone Bad by KILIAN, made more unisex through bitter-green aromatics, dry woods, mineral musk, vetiver, and crystal Ambrox.  
**Commercial strategy:** Keep enough natural tuberose for irregularity and realism while using the owned Tuberlia/Tuberalia trade base for repeatable, lower-cost floral body.  
**Finished volume:** 30.0 mL, measured after all materials dissolve and return to room temperature  
**Liquid stock charge:** exactly 6,000 µL  
**Separate solid charge:** exactly 240 mg Ambrox Super crystals  
**Alcohol:** Ethanol 96%, q.s. to a measured final volume of 30.0 mL

## Commercial tuberose module

The 700 µL tuberose-identity allocation is:

- **300 µL Tuberose Absolute 10% in DPG** — nominally 30 µL absolute if the stock is truly 10% by volume; the current fraction basis is unresolved, so this is not an exact active-mass claim.
- **180 µL neat Tuberlia Base** — 3.0% of the 6,000 µL liquid concentrate and 0.6% of the 30 mL finished bottle by volume.
- **220 µL DPG** — preserves the original module volume and avoids silently increasing total active loading.

This replaces 700 µL of the 10% natural stock in the creative comparator. It reduces nominal natural absolute from 70 µL to 30 µL while retaining natural texture and adding a reproducible commercial tuberose body. Tuberlia is a proprietary preblend: its supplier documentation and exact bottle identity govern safety, allergens, and maximum use, not the repository's molecular proxy values.

## Ambrettolide neat-stock rebase

The current owned Ambrettolide is user-confirmed neat. Use **10 µL neat Ambrettolide** and allocate **90 µL of the DPG row** to preserve the former 100 µL nominal-10% module. Never substitute 100 µL neat Ambrettolide.

## Aurantiol strength branch — choose exactly one

The current bottle may be neat or a 10% stock. Use one 250 µL constant-volume module:

| Bottle label | Aurantiol | DPG | Module total | Nominal Aurantiol |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Confirmed 10% stock | 250 µL | 0 µL | 250 µL | 25 µL equivalent |
| Confirmed neat | 25 µL | 225 µL | 250 µL | 25 µL |

If the 10% label omits fraction basis or carrier, the instruction remains a raw-volume pilot dose but not an exact active-mass value. Never add 250 µL neat Aurantiol.

## Pilot compounding order

1. Weigh 240 mg Ambrox Super crystals into the final vessel and dissolve in roughly 5 mL from the final ethanol allocation. Use only a gently warm water bath if required; apply no direct heat.
2. Reserve the DPG row by purpose: 90 µL for the Ambrettolide rebase and 220 µL for the commercial tuberose module.
3. Add 10 µL neat Ambrettolide followed by its 90 µL DPG allocation.
4. Add 300 µL Tuberose Absolute 10%, 180 µL Tuberlia Base, and the 220 µL tuberose-module DPG allocation.
5. Resolve and add exactly one Aurantiol branch. If its label cannot yet be read, leave the 250 µL module outstanding and do not bring the bottle to final volume.
6. Add the remaining base, heart, and top stocks in listed order, swirling gently between layers.
7. Add ethanol to approach the final mark, mix, return to room temperature, and then bring to a measured 30.0 mL final volume.
8. Check after 24 hours for haze, sediment, color drift, or Ambrox recrystallization. Evaluate at 48 hours, 14 days, and four weeks before changing the formula.
