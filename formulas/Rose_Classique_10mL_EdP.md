# Rose Classique — 10 mL EdP

**Classical rose-violet perfume in the Poucher / Wells & Billot tradition.**  
**Family:** Floral Rose-Violet (classical)  
**Concentration:** ~21% EdP · ~2,100 µL concentrate in 10 mL  
**Character:** Honeyed rose absolute, violet face powder, sandalwood talcum, aldehydic sparkle.

## Why This Architecture

This is a PEA-dominant (absolute-style) classical rose built over the three-period fixative spine: **cinnamyl alcohol** (balsamic warmth) + **sandalwood** (creamy anchor) + **Musk Ketone** (nitro-musk talcum powder). The ionones (Alpha Ionone + Methyl Ionone Pure) create the "lipstick rose" powder dimension — what Poucher, Wells & Billot, and every pre-1985 perfumer considered inseparable from "rose perfume."

Damascenone at 95 ppm adds the honey-wine halo Firmenich proved every perfumer prefers, without reading as modern or fruity. The musk chord serves three axes: Ethylene Brassylate (depth, creamy-skin), Romandolide (projection, clean-woody sillage), Musk Ketone (character-echo, talcum powder).

No benzyl salicylate (that's for jasmine chypres, not classical rose). No Galaxolide (reads as laundry-detergent, not face powder). No Iso E Super (the classical rose doesn't need a molecular cocoon — the ionones already provide abstraction).

## Dosing Notes

- **Musk Ketone 10%**: Pre-diluted from pure powder. If compounding from pure powder, weigh ~20 mg, dissolve in DPG first, then add.
- **Damascenone 1%**: 20 µL = 0.2 µL active. At 95 ppm — the Firmenich-tested range. Below 50 ppm the effect is lost; above 200 ppm it reads as modern fruity-rose. Do not skip.
- **Maturation**: 3–4 weeks sealed, cool, dark. The ionones + Musk Ketone require time to integrate. Testing before 2 weeks will read as disjointed — PEA dominates and the powder hasn't bloomed.

## Accord Architecture

| Layer | Function | Materials |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldehydic Sparkle** | Classical French opening — fizz, diffusion, petal reality | Aldehydes C10/C11/C11 undecylenic, Bergamot FCF |
| **Rose-Violet Core** | The "lipstick accord": rose alcohols + ionones at ~5:1 | PEA, Citronellol, Geraniol, Rhodinol, Nerol, Alpha Ionone, Methyl Ionone Pure |
| **Muguet Air Gap** | Space between petals — prevents the rose from suffocating | Hydroxycitronellal, Cyclamen Aldehyde |
| **Honey-Wine Halo** | The damascenone trace that makes rose read as "real" | Damascenone 1% at 95 ppm |
| **Metallic Dew** | Green-rose lift, petal freshness | Rose Oxide 1%, Geranium EO |
| **Classical Fixative Spine** | Balsamic warmth + creamy sandalwood + nitro-musk talcum | Cinnamyl alcohol, Ebanol, Musk Ketone |
| **Musk Chord (3-axis)** | Depth (skin) + projection (sillage) + character-echo (powder) | Ethylene Brassylate, Romandolide, Musk Ketone |
| **Authenticity Anchor** | Natural rose — small dose, outsized effect | Rose de Mai Absolute 10% |

## Material Rejections

- **No Benzyl Salicylate** — jasmine-carnation-chypre fixative, not classical rose. Would add cosmetic weight that shifts toward "white floral."
- **No Galaxolide** — reads as laundry-detergent in rose context. The classical rose drydown should smell like face powder and sandalwood, not fabric softener.
- **No Iso E Super** — molecular cocoon unnecessary in ionone-heavy architecture. The ionones already provide abstraction and powder.
- **No citronellyl/geranyl acetates** — not in inventory. Phenyl Ethyl Acetate covers the honeyed-rose ester register instead.
- **No Lilial** — too cosmetic-modern for pre-1985 classical. Hydroxycitronellal is the period-correct muguet material.
- **No Javanol** — too dry-mineral for classical warmth. Ebanol provides the creamy register that mirrors period Mysore sandalwood.
- **No Habanolide** — too warm-intimate; would compete with Musk Ketone's powder axis. Romandolide's clean-woody projection is the correct complement.
