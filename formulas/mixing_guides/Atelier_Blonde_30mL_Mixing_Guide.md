## Design Summary

This is the strongest all-around formula in the current wood-and-musk set because it behaves like a **finished luxury perfume**, not just a materials exercise.

The design goal:
- blonde polished woods
- cashmere texture
- white-clean musk persistence
- trace moss depth
- almost no readable top note

Nothing in this formula is meant to smell loud or decorative. Even the citrus and aldehydic touches are there only to sharpen the surface and give the woods a lacquered opening.

## Step-by-Step Mixing Instructions

### Phase 1: Prepare Workspace

1. Clean the beaker, tools, and workspace with ethanol.
2. Arrange the materials in the order shown below.
3. Label the final bottle: `LW-14 Atelier Blonde / 30mL / 30% / [Date]`.

---

### Phase 2: Build The Musk Ladder

**Target:** soft persistence, fabric diffusion, white-clean body

4. Add `Habanolide` — `0.90 mL`
5. Add `Ethylene Brassylate` — `0.90 mL`
6. Add `Zenolide` — `0.72 mL`
7. Add `Musk Ketone (10%)` — `0.36 mL`
8. Add `Exaltolide (10%)` — `0.36 mL`
9. Stir for `30 seconds`

**Check:** this should already smell soft, white-clean, powdery, and expensive, but incomplete.

---

### Phase 3: Build The Blonde Wood Core

**Target:** transparent polished woods with cashmere softness

10. Add `Iso E Super` — `1.26 mL`
11. Add `Clearwood` — `0.90 mL`
12. Add `Cedarwood EO` — `0.90 mL`
13. Add `Cedarwood oil Virginia` — `0.54 mL`
14. Add `Bacdanol` — `0.90 mL`
15. Add `Cashmeran (20%)` — `0.72 mL`
16. Add `Evernyl` — `0.27 mL`
17. Stir for `45 seconds`

**Check:** the fragrance should now smell like pale woods, clean fabric, and soft oakmoss shadow. It should not smell green, smoky, or sweet.

---

### Phase 4: Add Surface Polish

**Target:** lacquered luxury finish, not “top notes”

18. Add `Bergamot FCF oil Sicilian` — `0.09 mL`
19. Add `Aldehyde C12 MNA (1%)` — `0.09 mL`
20. Add `Rose Oxide (1%)` — `0.09 mL`
21. Stir gently for `20 seconds`

**Check:** you should get a brief impression of polished brightness and cold surface sheen, not a citrus perfume and not a rose perfume.

---

### Phase 5: Dilute To Final Volume

22. Confirm concentrate volume is `9.00 mL`
23. Add `Ethanol 96%` — `21.00 mL`
24. Stir or shake thoroughly for `60 seconds`
25. Transfer to the final bottle
26. Cap tightly and shake again for `30 seconds`

## Maturation Schedule

### Day 0
- sharp, separated
- aldehydic edge is too obvious
- musks feel detached from woods

### Day 3
- musks begin to round out
- cedar and clearwood start to read as one structure
- still a little dry and angular

### Day 7
- first serious evaluation point
- blonde wood effect becomes clear
- the opening polish is more integrated

### Day 14
- recommended minimum maturity
- cashmere texture is present
- oakmoss trace sits underneath rather than poking out

### Day 21-30
- best stage
- the formula reads seamless, expensive, and fully composed

---

## Expected Olfactive Development

### Opening
Not really citrus. More like light reflecting off polished pale wood. The bergamot, aldehyde, and rose oxide act as surface gloss.

### Heart
This is where the perfume lives: blonde wood, soft cedar, abstract woody diffusion, and fabric texture. The Bacdanol and Cashmeran make the structure feel plush instead of skeletal.

### Drydown
White-clean musk, pale wood, and faint mossed cashmere. The base should feel elegant and persistent, not heavy.

---

## Troubleshooting

### Too sharp or too dry
- let it mature longer before changing anything
- if still too sharp next batch, reduce `Aldehyde C12 MNA (1%)` from `90 µL` to `60 µL`

### Too flat or too soft
- next batch: raise `Iso E Super` by `90-180 µL`

### Too mossy / too dark
- next batch: reduce `Evernyl` from `270 µL` to `180 µL`

### Not luxurious enough
- next batch: raise `Cashmeran (20%)` by `90 µL`
- or raise `Habanolide` by `90 µL`
