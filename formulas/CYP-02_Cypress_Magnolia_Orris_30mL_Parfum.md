## 1. Target identity before materials

### Named subject

**Cypress is the sole named and continuous subject.** The user described the
owned material as a high-quality French Cypress EO. This is accepted as the
design brief and stock identity supplied by the user; it is not independently
proven by lot documentation, botanical authentication, GC-MS, or smelling in
this work.

The target is not a photorealistic cypress tree, forest, sauna, aromatherapy
oil, or conifer accord. It is a luxurious perfume that exposes the pleasurable
dry-green, aromatic, polished-wood possibilities of the Cypress EO. Magnolia
petal-light opens bodily space around the Cypress line. Orris rhizome-shadow
creates a cool, tactile lower register. A transparent wood chamber lets the
subject recur into the drydown without becoming a pencil-cedar or amberwood
wall.

Complexity means target-linked depth, richness, changing relations, and
hedonic potential; **ingredient count earns no complexity credit**. Every row
must own a distinct function, interface, temporal handoff, or failure control.

### Intended smell trajectory — hypothesis, not an observation

- **Opening:** dry, aromatic Cypress comes forward through a restrained bitter
  Cedrat edge. Petitgrain and Terpinyl Acetate bend the volatile green material
  toward perfumery rather than a literal branch or cleaning product.
- **Heart:** Cypress remains the vertical axis while Magnolia becomes cream-white
  petal illumination, not a second named subject. The heart should feel more
  bodily and inviting without becoming generic white floral or muguet.
- **Lower heart:** Orris appears beneath the petal light as cool violet-rhizome,
  faint root, and suede shadow. It must deepen Cypress, not replace it with
  lipstick, makeup powder, or a reused Dior Homme iris accord.
- **Drydown:** Cypress returns through transparent mineral wood, yielding
  sandalwood texture, a narrow moss punctuation, and one intimate macrocyclic
  musk. The intended finish is polished dry-green wood on skin, not sweet amber,
  laundry musk, or opaque woody-amber pressure.

This smell description is a design forecast. Actual odor, pleasure, target
fidelity, depth, and wear behavior remain `NOT TESTED`.

## 2. Evidence and theory boundary

Published *Cupressus sempervirens* studies show that organ, population, and
season can materially change the oil. A leaf/wood/cone study found a strongly
alpha-pinene / delta-3-carene chemotype and organ-specific differences, while
other studies report materially different proportions. This supports treating
the user's whole oil as an information-rich natural subject and forbids
inventing its constituent percentages. It does not prove the composition or
odor of the owned French lot.

Magnolia evidence is likewise species- and material-specific. Headspace work on
*Magnolia champaca* found terpenoids, volatile esters, and benzenoids, with
linalool/linalool oxides among major volatiles; other Magnolia species can be
dominated by different floral volatiles. Because the inventory records only
`Magnolia EO`, the formula uses a bounded natural dose plus a broad petal
scaffold. It makes no photorealistic Magnolia or species-equivalence claim.

Orris is split into three functions rather than treated as one generic iris
block. The owned Alpha Irone gives a precise cool character, Orris Liquid gives
tactile body, and Alpha Ionone gives violet-wood mobility. Published work on
irone isomers shows substantial odor differences and masking among samples;
evidence about beta-irone is not silently transferred to the user's
alpha-irone stock.

Finally, psychophysical work on binary odor mixtures found that mixture
pleasantness was usually intermediate and strongly influenced by relative
perceived intensities, while exceptions and substantial subject variance
remained. Therefore no ingredient property, formula score, OAV, price, prestige,
or language-model preference is accepted as observed liking. The most hedonic
position available computationally is the most target-faithful non-takeover
geometry, not a synthetic beauty number.

Primary research used for these boundaries:

- [Cypress organ/population GC-FID and GC-MS study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23776018/)
- [Magnolia champaca floral VOC metabolome/transcriptome study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12864-017-3846-8)
- [Irone-isomer synthesis and olfactory evaluation](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-08925-z)
- [Binary odor-mixture pleasantness study](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/33/7/599/330603)

## 6. Why Magnolia/Orris is the selected heart

The heart frontier was evaluated before dosing. It did not reuse a prior Dior,
iris, or white-floral model.

| Heart direction | Target contribution | Critical risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnolia only | luminous petal relief and bodily softness | beautiful but vertically shallow; Cypress can remain severe and planar | rejected as incomplete |
| Orris/iris as co-subject | powerful luxury code and tactile depth | replaces Cypress with lipstick, powder, or a recognizable iris perfume | rejected as target drift |
| Jasmine or narcotic white-floral heart | rich living floral body | creates a second named subject and can swamp the dry-green axis | rejected |
| Rose heart | warmth and hydrated petals | pulls the perfume toward rose/wood and changes the emotional identity | rejected |
| Magnolia petal-light plus Orris rhizome-shadow | upper illumination and lower tactile counterform around the same Cypress axis | requires tight dose hierarchy to avoid floral or iris takeover | **selected** |

The selected position is “hedonic” only in the defensible design sense: it
maximizes bodily relief, contrast, tactile depth, and temporal change while
preserving the user's chosen subject. It does not claim that any person has
liked the result.
