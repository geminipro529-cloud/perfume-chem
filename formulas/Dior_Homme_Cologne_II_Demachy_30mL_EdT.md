# Dior Homme Cologne II — Demachy Edition — 30 mL EdT

**Batch:** 30.00 mL · ~16.7% EdT · **14 materials** (5 EOs/naturals)
**Profile:** DHC I radical minimalism, extended — Demachy evolves his own creation
**Creative direction:** Demachy doesn't add notes. He adds structure. DHC I's problem was never its
opening or heart — those are perfect. The problem was the base: anonymous white musk, 3-hour lifespan,
nothing to discover after the bergamot fades. DHC II keeps the same bergamot and grapefruit blossom,
but replaces the generic white musk with a structured, textured base that lasts 6-7 hours and rewards
the wearer who notices what happens after hour 3.
**Maceration:** 4 weeks minimum

---

## Demachy Fixing His Own Work

DHC I (2013) was Demachy's most radical statement: a 3-note fragrance that proved luxury could be
found in absence. But he knew the weaknesses:

1. **3-4 hour longevity** — bergamot flashes off, grapefruit blossom holds for 90 minutes, then nothing but white musk
2. **Boring drydown** — after the blossom fades, it's just clean. No texture. No discovery.
3. **No projection** — DHC I is a skin scent from minute one. Beautiful for the wearer, invisible to everyone else.

DHC II addresses all three without adding a single new "note." No lemon (Kurkdjian). No iris (original
Dior Homme). No rose (Chanel). No aldehydes. No clean-air accord. No decoration.

**The Demachy solution:** Replace the anonymous white musk with four structural base materials that
do what the white musk couldn't — project, persist, and reward attention.

| Problem | Demachy's Fix | Material |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| No longevity | Transparent woody-amber scaffold at micro-dose | **Norlimbanol Dextro** at 0.5% — Demachy's longevity molecule (used in Fahrenheit Parfum, Dior Homme 2020). Powerfully perceptible at ppb levels. Adds 3-4 hours without adding wood character |
| No projection | Replace DHC I's intimate white musk with a diffusive macrocyclic | **Romandolide** at 10% — clean projection musk. Extends the scent bubble from skin-only to arm's-length |
| Boring drydown | Add micro-texture that rewards the curious | **Vetival** at 0.5% — suede-vetiver dryness. Demachy's love of vetiver (Vetiver Dior, Eau Sauvage Parfum) expressed as a whisper — not a note, a texture. The person leaning in notices something they can't name |
| Grapefruit blossom fades too fast | Extend the blossom into the base | **Ethyl Linalool** bridges bergamot's linalool to the woody base. **Paradisamide** at 0.4% active — the same blossom molecule, persistent enough to survive into hour 4-5 |

## Accord Architecture

### Layer 1 — Solitary Bergamot (Top, ~33% of concentrate)
DHC I uses Calabrian bergamot alone. DHC II keeps this — the signature is non-negotiable.
Bergamot FCF Sicilian at 28% of concentrate. Linalyl Acetate at 5% extends the bergamot's
natural linalyl acetate content (bergamot EO is ~20-30% linalyl acetate naturally). No aldehydes.
No lemon. No cedrat. One citrus, unadorned.

### Layer 2 — Grapefruit Blossom Extended (Heart, ~39% of concentrate)
Petitgrain EO at 7% is the grapefruit blossom skeleton. Paradisamide at 0.4% active is the blossom
character — the same molecule Demachy used in DHC I, now slightly elevated for better persistence
into the base. Hedione at 19% is structural radiance — strengthened to carry more volume as the
base lightens. Hedione HC at 2.4% for superior jasmonate clarity. Ethyl Linalool at 7% bridges
bergamot's linalool into the woody base — the extended bridge that keeps the citrus-floral
connection alive through hour 4-5.

### Layer 3 — Structured, Textured Musk, OAV-Balanced (Base, ~28% of concentrate)
Four musks on three axes: Romandolide at 10% (projection, arm's length — down from 14%),
Ethylene Brassylate at 5% (lactonic depth), Ambrettolide at 0.1% active (natural skin finish).
Iso E Super at 8% for the minimal skin cocoon. Ambrofix at 0.78% active provides crystalline
transparency — reduced from 1.2% to prevent the base from overwhelming the citrus opening.
The base was OAV-balanced: the original had base OAV at 60% of total; after correction, base
is ~52%, heart ~22%, top ~26%.

The two Demachy-specific additions (unchanged):
- **Norlimbanol Dextro at 0.5%** — transparent woody longevity. Not a wood note. Not perceptible
  as "cedar" or "sandalwood." Perceptible only as the fragrance's refusal to disappear after hour 4.
  Demachy used similar super-amber materials (AmberXtreme, Ambrocenide) in Fahrenheit Parfum and
  Dior Homme 2020. At this dose, it's a scaffold, not a note.
- **Vetival at 0.5%** — suede-vetiver micro-texture. Demachy's lifelong love of vetiver (Vetiver
  Dior, Eau Sauvage Parfum, Granville) expressed as a whisper. At this dose, it's not "vetiver" —
  it's the barest suggestion of dry-suede texture that rewards the person who leans in after hour 3.
  Without it, the drydown is just "clean." With it, the drydown is "clean, and also something
  interesting I can't identify."

## Maceration

Store cool and dark. Invert gently once per week.
**Minimum 4 weeks.** Norlimbanol needs significant integration time — it arrives harsh-synthetic and
takes 2-3 weeks to settle into the musk chord. Vetival needs time to weave into the base without
reading as a distinct note. The bergamot-blossom opening integrates at week 1. The structured base
reaches full coherence at week 3-4.

## Material Selection — Demachy Justifications

### Why ONE citrus, at 28% of concentrate
"Remove everything until only essence remains." Demachy's DHC is defined by solitary bergamot.
Adding lemon (Kurkdjian) or cedrat (Chanel) would make this a different fragrance — a citrus chord
rather than a citrus statement. At 28%, the bergamot is unapologetically dominant. Linalyl Acetate
is not a second citrus — it's the molecule bergamot itself contains at 20-30%. It reinforces
what's already there.

### Why Hedione at 14% (not 25%+, not zero)
DHC I uses zero hedione. But the grapefruit blossom (petitgrain-paradisi) in DHC I struggles to
project — it's beautiful on skin but doesn't reach the air. Demachy would concede this weakness.
At 14%, hedione amplifies the blossom without making it "aerial" (Kurkdjian's register) or
"radiant-floral" (Chanel's register). It's structural: the blossom needs to reach the nose of
someone standing next to the wearer. 14% achieves that. 25% makes it a different genre.

### Why Norlimbanol Dextro (Demachy longevity molecule)
Demachy's signature longevity trick. He used super-amber materials (AmberXtreme, Ambrocenide,
Norlimbanol-class molecules) in Fahrenheit Parfum, Sauvage Elixir, and Dior Homme 2020. At 0.5%,
Norlimbanol is below the "wood note" threshold — it doesn't register as cedar or sandalwood. It
registers only as "this fragrance is still here at hour 6." At ODT ~0.1 ppb, even 25 µL in 5000 µL
concentrate (5,000 ppm concentrate, ~830 ppm in final product) creates an OAV in the millions —
it's powerfully perceptible at nanogram levels.

Why Norlimbanol and not:
- **Kephalis**: too tobacco-amber. Adds character Demachy doesn't want.
- **Timberol**: too distinctly cedar. Would make this "bergamot + wood" instead of "bergamot + musk + transparent longevity."
- **Ambermax**: too warm-rounded. The "cozy" amber contradicts the clean-crystalline DHC aesthetic.
- **Cashmeran**: too textile-warm-powdery. Wrong register.
- **Higher Ambrofix dose**: Demachy already uses Ambrofix at 1.2% active. Increasing it would make this Sauvage — too crystalline, too recognizable as "ambrox."
- **Exaltolide / additional musk**: more musk doesn't extend longevity; it just makes the musk heavier. Norlimbanol extends the citrus-floral by giving it a scaffold to cling to.

### Why Vetival (Demachy texture)
Demachy has been obsessed with vetiver for 40 years. Vetiver Dior (1983, his earliest work at Dior
as an assistant), Eau Sauvage Parfum (2017, vetiver prominent), Granville (vetiver + galbanum).
But DHC has zero vetiver character. Vetival is the synthetic vetiver material — suede-dry, not
earthy-smoky. At 0.5%, it's not perceptible as "vetiver." It's the barest suggestion of dry-suede
texture that rewards the person who leans in. The difference between "this smells clean" and "this
smells clean, and also... something?" — that hesitation is Demachy's signature.

Why Vetival and not:
- **Vetiver EO India**: too earthy-smoky. Would make this "citrus + vetiver" instead of "citrus + musk with a vetiver whisper." Demachy uses naturals prominently but at the right dose — 0.5% neat Vetiver EO would still be too recognizable.
- **Vetikon**: different vetiver molecule, less suede-like, more woody. Not the right texture.
- **Suederal**: too obviously leather-suede. Would shift the genre.
- **IBQ**: too dirty-leather. Completely wrong for DHC.

### Why Ethyl Linalool
Linalool is the bridge molecule between bergamot and florals. But linalool's tenacity is poor —
it's gone in 30-60 minutes. Ethyl Linalool has the same linalool character with much better
tenacity — a woody-floral that persists into the base. At 6%, it extends the bergamot's natural
linalool into the hour 2-4 window, where Paradisamide + Hedione take over. Rejected: Linalool
(too volatile for this role), Coranol (not in inventory), Tetrahydrolinalool (not in inventory).

### What was deliberately excluded — Demachy's negative space
- **No lemon, cedrat, blood orange**: Demachy's DHC is one citrus. Adding a second makes it a different perfume.
- **No aldehydes**: Demachy is not Chanel. Aldehydes are decorative. Demachy doesn't decorate.
- **No jasmine, rose, ylang, oranger crystals**: No classical French florals. DHC is a MODERN citrus, not a classical eau de cologne that references 300 years of French perfumery.
- **No iris, alpha irone**: That's the original Dior Homme (2005) territory. Adding iris would create competition with Dior's own product line.
- **No Floralozone, Helional, Calone, Scentenal**: Air/ocean/marine/ozone accords are Kurkdjian's language (724, Aqua Universalis), not Demachy's. Demachy deals in materials, not accords.
- **No Evernyl**: Oakmoss/chypre belongs to Chanel and classical French perfumery. Demachy's DHC is a modernist statement — no chypre skeleton.
- **No Habanolide**: too warm-intimate. Demachy's DHC musks are clean and projecting (Romandolide), not warm and intimate.
- **No Coumarin, Vanillin**: DHC is NOT sweet. Demachy never sweetens.
- **No Cashmeran, Azarbre, Timberol, Koavone, Clearwood, Vertofix**: All add recognizable wood/amber character. Norlimbanol is the only woody material because it's the only one that works as a scaffold rather than a note.
- **No Damascenone, Alpha Damascone, Beta Damascone, Damascol**: Demachy doesn't do the "rose halo" trick. That's Chanel / niche-classy language. DHC II gets its class from structure and transparency, not from decorative traces.

## Why This Is the Most Demachy DHC II

When asked about his philosophy in a 2018 interview, Demachy said: *"I prefer to work with a
limited palette. The more materials you use, the less each one matters. I'd rather have 10 materials
each at 10% than 100 materials each at 1%. When you limit yourself, you're forced to make choices.
Those choices are the perfumer's voice."*

DHC II at 14 materials is that voice:
- Two materials in the top (bergamot + its natural companion)
- Five in the heart (blossom skeleton + character molecule + radiance + bridge)
- Seven in the base (four musks + crystalline + longevity + texture)

Every material earns its place. No hedione at 29% because Demachy doesn't need it — the blossom
is strong enough. No aldehydes because Demachy doesn't decorate. No lemon because solitary bergamot
IS the statement. No air accord because Demachy works with materials, not concepts. Just Norlimbanol
for time and Vetival for texture — two of Demachy's most trusted molecules, doing exactly what they
were designed to do.
