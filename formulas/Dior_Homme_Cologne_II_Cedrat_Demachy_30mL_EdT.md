# Dior Homme Cologne II — Cédrat — Demachy Edition — 30 mL EdT

**Batch:** 30.00 mL · ~16.7% EdT · **14 materials** (5 EOs/naturals)
**Premise:** Dior tells Demachy: same DHC II structure, but no bergamot. Use a different citrus.
**Demachy's choice:** Cedrat FCF Sicilian — bitter citron. The coldest, most mineral citrus in
existence. Where bergamot is DHC's linen suit, cedrat is its steel-and-glass tower.
**Maceration:** 4 weeks minimum

---

## Why Cedrat

Demachy was given a choice and chose the hardest one.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- **Lemon** — too classical, too obvious. Every house has a lemon cologne. Demachy doesn't do obvious.
- **Grapefruit** — too meta. The grapefruit blossom is already the heart. Grapefruit citrus + grapefruit blossom = a fragrance about grapefruit. Demachy doesn't do conceptual.
- **Blood Orange** — too warm, too juicy. DHC is cold-transparent, not warm-fruity. Blood orange pulls toward gourmand.
- **Red Mandarin** — too sweet-tangerine. Pulls toward oriental. Wrong genre entirely.
- **Lime** — too cocktail, too sharp-acidic. Reads as a summer drink, not a perfume.
- **D-Limonene** — a molecule, not a material. Demachy works with naturals, not single-molecule approximations.

**Why cedrat:**
Cedrat is the citrus for people who find bergamot too easy. It's bitter where bergamot is bright,
mineral where bergamot is floral, cold where bergamot is warm. It's the citrus that asks the
wearer to meet it halfway — and Demachy has always made fragrances that respect the wearer's
intelligence.

The cedrat + grapefruit blossom pairing creates a DOUBLE BITTER accord — the most severe,
transparent citrus opening in the Dior Homme line. Where the original DHC is "I just showered,"
the cedrat DHC is "I just showered in a glass building in Zurich." Same cleanliness, different
architecture.

## Bergamot DHC vs Cedrat DHC

| Axis | Bergamot DHC II | Cedrat DHC II |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| **Citrus character** | Refined, bright, floral-linalool. Universally loved. | Bitter, mineral, cold-citron. Challenging. |
| **Opening effect** | "Expensive bergamot" — recognizable, welcoming | "What IS that citrus?" — unfamiliar, intriguing |
| **Citrus-blossom relationship** | Bergamot's linalool smoothly bridges to petitgrain's green-floral | Cedrat's bitterness amplifies petitgrain's green-bitterness. Double bitter. No smooth transition — a stark handoff |
| **Temperature** | Cool | **Cold** — 2-3°C colder on the skin |
| **Hedione adjustment** | 16% (700 µL) | **17% (850 µL)** — slightly more radiance to compensate for cedrat's severity |
| **Paradisamide adjustment** | 0.4% active (200 µL of 10%) | **0.3% active (150 µL of 10%)** — reduced to prevent the double-bitter grapefruit-on-grapefruit amplification |
| **Wearer** | Wants to smell refined and approachable | Wants to smell refined and untouchable |
| **Season** | Spring/summer, day | Summer, day-to-evening. Works in hot weather where bergamot reads as too soft |

## Accord Architecture

### Layer 1 — Solitary Cedrat (Top, ~33% of concentrate)
Cedrat FCF Sicilian at 30% of concentrate. Solitary, massive, unapologetic — the same structural
approach as the bergamot original, but a completely different olfactory character. Cedrat is
bitter citron — the fruit that looks like a lumpy lemon but smells colder, more mineral, less
acidic. Linalyl Acetate at 3% (150 µL) — sufficient to bridge the citrus to the blossom without
adding bergamot character. The cedrat-petitgrain handoff is clean: bitter citrus fades, green
bitterness rises to meet it.

### Layer 2 — Grapefruit Blossom, Full Volume (Heart, ~39% of concentrate)
The OAV-balanced heart. Petitgrain EO at 7% — the full grapefruit blossom skeleton, no longer
pulled back for fear of double-bitter aggression. Paradisamide at 0.4% active — the full DHC
signature dose, the blossom is back to full character. Hedione at 19% + Hedione HC at 2.4% —
the radiance backbone carries the blossom through the lighter base. Ethyl Linalool at 7% —
a longer bridge between the expanded heart and the reduced base, keeping the citrus-floral
connection alive through hour 4-5.

### Layer 3 — Structured Musk, OAV-Balanced (Base, ~28% of concentrate)
The base has been lightened to correct the OAV imbalance. Romandolide at 10% (down from 14%) —
projection at arm's length, not room-filling. Ambrofix at 0.78% active (down from 1.2%) —
crystalline pillar present but no longer dominant. The base total OAV has been reduced from
64% of the formula to approximately 42% — still substantial (musks are always base-heavy),
but no longer overwhelming the citrus-blossom layers. Norlimbanol at 0.5% for longevity,
Vetival at 0.5% for micro-texture. Four musks on three axes (Romandolide projection, EB
lactonic depth, Ambrettolide natural finish). The base now supports without dominating.

## Maceration

Store cool and dark. Invert gently once per week.
**Minimum 4 weeks.** The same as the bergamot version. Cedrat integrates faster than bergamot
(less terpenic complexity to settle) — but Norlimbanol + Vetival still need the full 3-4 weeks.
The hedione-clarity adjustment benefits from the extra time.

## The Cedrat Character: What Changes, What Doesn't

**What changes:**
- Opening: from "beautiful bergamot" to "what IS that citrus?" Cedrat is unfamiliar to most people. The opening is a question, not an answer.
- Temperature: 2-3°C colder on skin. Cedrat reads as mineral-cold; bergamot reads as cool-bright.
- Bitterness: significantly more bitter. The cedrat-petitgrain double bitterness creates a very dry, very transparent effect — like the difference between still water (bergamot) and sparkling water (cedrat).
- Approachability: reduced. This is a fragrance for people who find the original DHC too ingratiating.
- Recognition: lower. No one will say "cedrat!" — they'll say "what is that?" and then "I want it."

**What doesn't change:**
- Structure: 14 materials, same base, same heart skeleton, same longevity (6-7 hours).
- Cleanliness: still an "I just showered" fragrance. Different soap, same cleanliness.
- Drydown: after hour 3, the cedrat and bergamot versions converge. The citrus is gone; what remains is the structured musk base (Norlimbanol + Vetival + musks). A person testing both on different arms would find them identical after hour 4.
- Price point / positioning: same Dior Homme Cologne line, different flanker.

---

## Why This Is Demachy's Choice, Not Marketing's

Marketing would choose lemon. It's safe, recognizable, universally liked. Demachy chooses cedrat
because it says something new while staying within his aesthetic. The cedrat DHC is colder, more
severe, more transparent — it pushes the DHC concept further than bergamot allowed. Where bergamot
is DHC's commercial face, cedrat is DHC's artistic statement.

This is the difference between a perfumer and a brand manager: the brand manager says "what citrus
tests well?" The perfumer says "what citrus creates the most interesting version of this idea?"
For Demachy, the interesting version is the one that makes people ask questions.
