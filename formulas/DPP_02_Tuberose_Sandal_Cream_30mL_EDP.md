## Concept Lock

A narcotic tuberose whose apparent creaminess develops through jasmine, benzyl materials, lactones, salicylates, and a broad sandalwood residue. This is intentionally the non-resin-amber member of the set. Its depth comes from a floral-to-cream-to-sandal recursion rather than labdanum or benzoin.

Every macro-plane contains a miniature sequence of deep seed, body, contour, upper veil, and return thread. The completed loop is:

Deep benzyl-musk residue → sandal body → salicylate-lactone cream → living jasmine interior → tuberose identity → Hedione-muguet veil → neroli-benzyl-acetate glint → evaporation returns the sandal residue through a renewed cream-petal aura.

## Material Selection Rationale

- **Tuberose identity:** Tuberose Base provides mass, Tuberlia supplies a controlled tuberose contour, and the 10% absolute adds living natural irregularity. Methyl Benzoate makes the identity recognizable and reconnects it to the volatile benzyl-acetate surface.
- **Living interior:** Jasmine Absolute contributes natural depth, Jessemal supplies warm jasmine body, Indole provides a deliberately restrained biological shadow, Ylang adds creamy floral volume, and Aurantiol extends the orange-flower axis. Heliotropal connects that interior to the cream plane.
- **Cream plane:** Delta Decalactone gives milk-like body while Gamma Undecalactone adds the lighter peach-cream edge. Benzyl Alcohol and the two salicylates connect this lactonic plane to both white flower and sandalwood.
- **Sandalwood recursion:** Ebanol is the dense kernel and Bacdanol the milky mass. Iso E Super is used only as the diffusive upper surface of that base; it is not the perfume’s identity.
- **Single musk:** Ethylene Brassylate was selected for creamy persistence and compatibility with the lactone-salicylate axis. Additional clean or projecting musks were omitted because they would steer the late flower toward laundry or abstract woods.
- **No resin amber:** Labdanum, benzoin, olibanum, opoponax, myrrh, and modern amberwood pressure materials are deliberately absent so the perfume remains a tuberose-sandal cream rather than becoming the second amber formula.

## Recursive Compounding Procedure

1. Use one clean amber vial and add P7 through P1 in the formula-table order.
2. After every completed plane, cap and gently roll until homogeneous. The planes are compositional relationships, not literal unmixed liquid layers.
3. After the concentrate reaches 6,000 µL, mix until uniform.
4. Add approximately 22 mL ethanol 96%, mix, then add ethanol q.s. to the 30.00 mL final-volume mark.
5. Inspect clarity immediately and at 24 hours, 7 days, and 14 days.
6. Evaluate on blotters at 0, 5, and 30 minutes; 2, 6, 24, and 48 hours. The decisive DPP question is whether the sandal residue regenerates a cream-petal aura rather than ending as a disconnected woody musk.
