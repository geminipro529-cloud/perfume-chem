# Mass-Market Masculine Aromatic Fougère — Education Set, 3 Variations

**Date:** 2026-04-30
**Goal:** train the discipline of *subtraction* by building three clean mass-market masculine aromatic fougères from zero. Each is a 30 mL EDP at 20% concentration. Each is built around a different archetype spanning 33 years of the category.

---

## The Educational Decision

**Choice: educate on mass-market masculine aromatic fougère, not a different family.**

Reasoning:

1. **The real gap.** The `Vol d'Ambre v3c` exercise just exposed an honest weakness — the user's formulation reflex is amber-fruity-floral-gourmand maximalism. Every bottle includes vanilla + benzoin + multiple jasmines + roses + apple + cardamom + amber. This is one mode. Mass-market masculine fougère is the *opposite mode*: cold, dry, restrained, deliberately incomplete. **Learning to omit is harder than learning to add.**
2. **Direct commercial relevance.** Sauvage / Bleu de Chanel / Acqua di Giò Profondo dominate Thai mass-market masculine. The user already lives in this market. Building one clean version of each archetype teaches the category from the inside.
3. **No new materials needed.** The existing inventory already contains every material required. A different family (chypre, floral aldehydic, leather) would require new purchases and slow the loop.
4. **Best ROI per learning hour.** The user already has the perfumery vocabulary; what they lack is restraint. This is a discipline problem, not a knowledge problem. The quickest fix is forced practice.

**What this set is *not*:** dupe-cloning. These are *archetype-correct* compositions in the spirit of Sauvage / Bleu / Drakkar, not 1:1 clones. The point is the architecture, not the smell-alike.

---

## Common Architecture

All three follow the same five-tier mass-market masculine aromatic fougère skeleton:

| Tier | Function | Materials by category |
|---|---|---|
| Citrus top | volatile <5 min, first impression | bergamot, grapefruit, lime, citron, petitgrain |
| Aromatic heart | 5–60 min, family-defining axis | lavender, linalyl acetate, clary sage, geraniol, juniper |
| Spice/pepper hook | bite, modernity | cardamom, eugenol; in V3 omitted |
| Coumarin/tonka | sweetness *bridge*, not gourmand load | coumarin only; no vanilla, no benzoin |
| Woody/musky/amber base | longevity and projection | Iso E Super, cedarwood, sandalore, Ambrox, musks |

**Forbidden in this set:**

- Vanillin, Ethyl Vanillin, Benzoin Resinoid (gourmand load — disqualifies the family read).
- Apritone, Hexyl Acetate, Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate (apple esters — push toward fruity-amber).
- Hedione HC (out of stock anyway).
- Multiple jasmines or roses (kitchen-sink floral mass).
- Cashmeran above trace (creamy-warm pulls toward oriental).
- Norlimbanol Dextro above 0.3% concentrate (woody OR overload risk in clean fougère).

This list is the *learning point*. None of these are bad materials — they are simply category-disqualifying for clean masculine fougère.

### Why This Reads Like Sauvage

- **Ambrox at `1.96%` neat** — Sauvage EDP parity. This is the dominant character marker.
- **No Evernyl.** Modern Sauvage-tier does not use moss; the family read comes from lavender + coumarin + Iso E Super + dry cedar woody framework.
- **Cardamom FTEC + Eugenol = "pink pepper proxy."** No real pink pepper EO needed; the eugenol-clove + cardamom-ginger combination delivers the same OR signal at the heart-aromatic boundary.
- **Heavy Iso E Super (`2.40%` neat)** — gives the transparent-woody Sauvage signature without competing with the aromatic heart.
- **Habanolide + Galaxolide = `1.40%` musks** — clean white-musk trail for skin-projection, no animalic.
- **Beta-Pinene `0.50%`** — pine-resin sparkle in the top, a defining Sauvage facet.
- **Lavender at `1.20%` neat, no spike lavender** — Sauvage uses smooth lavender, not the camphoraceous spike profile.

### What V1 Teaches

The discipline of building a high-Ambrox modern fougère with **only the materials the family needs**. No vanilla, no benzoin, no fruit, no rose, no jasmine body. The lavender + Hedione is the *only* heart bridge.

### What V3 Teaches

The actual *original* fougère grammar — lavender + coumarin + oakmoss + patchouli/vetiver. **This is the family at its purest.** Comparing V3 against V1 and V2 side-by-side teaches what the modern category drift looks like: 1982 → 2015 is a story of moss disappearing, woods becoming abstract (Iso E), and Ambroxan replacing oakmoss as the base anchor.

### Safety Note On V3

Evernyl at `0.08%` is intentionally close to the IFRA Cat 4 ceiling (`~0.1%` finished product). Measure the `24 µL` precisely against a tared vial; do not estimate by drop count. If unsure, use `20 µL` instead — `0.067%` is still enough for a clear moss read and gives a `30%` IFRA headroom buffer.

| Era / archetype | 2015+ modern | 2010+ modern | 1982 classical |

**The structural story across the three:**

- V3 (1982): the family lives on `lavender + coumarin + oakmoss + patchouli`. Ambrox is incidental. Citrus is supporting.
- V2 (2010s): oakmoss is gone (regulatory and fashion); Iso E Super becomes the architecture; Ambrox enters as moderate base.
- V1 (2015+): Ambrox dominates; lavender quiets; eugenol+cardamom replaces classical moss as the bite material.

This is why all three score as *aromatic fougère* under any reasonable family classifier despite radically different smell profiles. The family is more flexible than people think; what changes is *which axis carries the family signal*.

## Mixing Protocol (Common To All Three)

### 1. Prepare BHT antioxidant

Weigh `~1.2 mg` BHT into a clean 1 mL vial. Add `200 µL` ethanol 96%; swirl to dissolve. The `200 µL` of ethanol carrier counts as part of the concentrate volume.

### 2. Add to concentrate vial in base → top order

Generally: lowest-VP base materials first (musks, Ambrox, Iso E, woods), then heart (Hedione, lavender, linalyl acetate), then top (citrus, petitgrain, beta-pinene). Specific orders are listed in each variation's table by tier label.

For V3 specifically: add `Evernyl` first (it is a crystalline solid that needs the longest dissolution time in ethanol).

### 3. Top up with ethanol 96%

After all materials are in, the concentrate vial should hold approximately `6,000 µL`. Verify by weight or by graduated transfer. If short, top up with ethanol 96%.

### 4. Bottle and macerate

Transfer concentrate to the 30 mL bottle. Add `24,000 µL` ethanol 96%. Cap, invert `20×` gently. **Macerate `4 weeks minimum`** before final evaluation.

The 4-week maceration is non-negotiable for fougères. Lavender + coumarin + Evernyl integration takes longer than oriental ambers because the aromatic-axis materials need to cross-condition with the woody base before the family read clarifies. Judging at 1 week is misleading.

### 5. Evaluation protocol

- **Day 7:** check direction only. Does the family read fougère?
- **Day 14:** check structure. Is the lavender-coumarin-base axis clear?
- **Day 28:** make the verdict. Is it the right archetype?
- **Day 60:** retest cold. Maceration is fully done by here.

## What To Do With This Set

1. **Pick one variation to make first.** The recommendation is **V1 (Sauvage)** — it teaches the modern category and is the most wearable in Thai climate.
2. **Make all three eventually.** The educational value is in the *comparison*. Sniffing V1, V2, V3 side-by-side on Day 28 teaches more than making any single one.
3. **Do not modify the recipes during the first build.** The point is to build the archetype clean, then learn from the result. Modifications come on V2 of each, not V1.
4. **Compare against a real bottle on Day 28.** Buy a Sauvage 5 mL travel size or a Bleu de Chanel sampler. The reference comparison is what closes the educational loop.

The discipline lesson is the same across all three: **the materials you didn't add are as important as the materials you did**.
