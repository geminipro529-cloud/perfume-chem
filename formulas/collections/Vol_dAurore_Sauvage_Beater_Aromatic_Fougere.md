# Vol d'Aurore — Aromatic Fougère Sublime (Sauvage-Beater Composition)

**Date:** 2026-04-30
**Family:** Modern Aromatic Fougère
**Format:** 30 mL EDP, 20% concentration
**Brief:** beat Sauvage EDP on its own market — Thai mass-market masculine — through *art, layering, and texture*, not through cloning. Match Sauvage's strengths (Ambroxan skin warmth, cold-pepper opening, clean diffusion); exceed it on the axes Sauvage neglects (real heart, textural depth, drydown evolution, signature moment).

## Why Sauvage Wins, And Where It Leaves Room

| Sauvage strength | The actual mechanism | Why it works for the masses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambroxan skin warmth (~3% neat EDP) | Skin-depot molecule activates with body heat; massive aerial projection | Reads as "expensive" and "masculine" without a complicated brief |

| Iso E Super transparent woody | Single material doing structural work; no fat-cedar mass | Modern, easy, doesn't read "old man" |

| Sauvage weakness | The actual mechanism | Why it's an opening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linear after 30 min | Sauvage skips the heart; goes top → Ambrox slab | A real heart creates surprise and depth |

**The strategic conclusion:** do not try to outdo Sauvage at being Sauvage. Beat it by adding the four things Sauvage refuses to do — a real aromatic heart, mineral-incense texture, multi-layer drydown, and a signature surprise — while matching its core (Ambrox + pepper-citrus + Iso E) at parity.

## The Four Acts

The bottle is designed as a four-act olfactory architecture, not a three-tier pyramid. Sauvage is a three-tier (top, weak heart, base). Vol d'Aurore is four acts because the *bridge* is the differentiator.

### Act I — The Cold Awakening (0-15 min)

`Bergamot FCF` + `Lime Distilled` + `Cardamom FTEC` + `Eugenol` + `Beta-Pinene` + `Juniper Berry` + `Petitgrain` + `Spike Lavender` (camphoraceous accent).

**What it does:** matches Sauvage's pepper-bergamot opening at parity. The eugenol + cardamom delivers the pepper-bite OR signal; beta-pinene + juniper add the cold-conifer sparkle Sauvage uses pink pepper for. Spike lavender adds a tiny camphoraceous shimmer that signals "aromatic-masculine" in 5 seconds.

**Sauvage parity check:** equal on first impression. The user should not be able to tell they're not smelling Sauvage at minute 2.

### Act II — The Real Heart (15 min - 2 h)

`Lavender EO` + `Linalyl Acetate` + `Clary Sage` + `Geraniol` + `Hedione (massive)` + `Phenethyl Alcohol` + `Ethyl Linalool` + `Cardamom (continuing)`.

**What it does:** this is the act Sauvage skips. Where Sauvage transitions directly from top to Ambroxan base, Vol d'Aurore stops to *show* a fully built aromatic heart. Lavender + clary sage + geranium is the classical aromatic triangle. Hedione at `~8%` of concentrate is the radiance pump — it activates the same OR networks associated with serotonin-pleasure response and gives the bottle its emotional warmth.

**Sauvage parity check:** wins. At minute 30, where Sauvage starts going flat, Vol d'Aurore is at peak heart bloom.

### Act III — The Mineral Bridge (1-3 h)

`Frankincense EO` + `Cypress EO`* + `Vetiver EO` + `Patchouli EO` + `Nagarmortha Oil` (trace).

**What it does:** this is the textural surprise. As the heart fades, a cool-mineral resin layer rises. Frankincense gives the dry-cold incense facet that signals "expensive niche perfumery." Vetiver + patchouli + nagarmortha give an earth shadow underneath. Cypress adds coniferous coolness at the right moment.

**Sauvage parity check:** wins decisively. Sauvage has no equivalent. This is the act that establishes the bottle as *not Sauvage* in a way that reads as *better*, not as *different and worse*.

\* Cypress EO is in V2 (recommended). V1 substitutes additional Petitgrain + Juniper at this position.

### Act IV — The Layered Drydown (3+ h)

`Ambrox Super` (matched to Sauvage at ~2.0% neat) + `Iso E Super` + `Cedarwood Virginia` + `Sandalore` + `Timberol` + `Cashmeran` + `Habanolide` + `Galaxolide` + `Coumarin` (trace) + `Norlimbanol Dextro` (trace).

**What it does:** the Ambrox tier matches Sauvage. The difference is what surrounds it. Sauvage's drydown is mostly Ambrox + Iso E. Vol d'Aurore adds two cedars + sandalwood + Timberol + Cashmeran for a layered woody base where each note reveals at a different VP timing. Coumarin at trace adds tonka warmth without becoming gourmand.

**Sauvage parity check:** parity on Ambrox projection; wins on drydown evolution. At hour 6, where Sauvage is essentially the same as hour 2, Vol d'Aurore is in a different woody-musky-resinous zone.

## V2 — The Ideal Version (Recommended New Material Acquisitions)

V1 is good. V2 is what makes it *exceptional* — what moves the bottle from "great Sauvage alternative" to "art-object aromatic fougère."

### Three Materials To Acquire

| Material | Cost (Thai supplier estimate) | Why it elevates Vol d'Aurore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pink Pepper EO** (`Schinus molle` or `Schinus terebinthifolius`) | ~800-1,500 THB / 10 mL | Real pink pepper signal; replaces eugenol-cardamom substitute. Sauvage's actual top-note material. Authentic and distinctive. |
| **Saffron CO2** or **Safranal** | ~2,000-4,000 THB / 5 mL | The signature memorable note no mass-market masculine has. Saffron + lavender is one of the most elegant accords in modern perfumery (used in Penhaligon's Halfeti, Thameen, etc.). This is the *art* differentiator. |
| **Cypress EO** | ~600-1,000 THB / 30 mL | Mineral-coniferous coolness in the bridge act. Adds dimension Sauvage cannot match. Elegant and modern. |

Total acquisition cost: **~3,500-6,500 THB** for materials that will support multiple compositions, not just this one.

### V2 Recipe Adjustments (Replaces V1 Materials)

| Position | V1 | V2 (adjusted) | Δ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pink Pepper substitute | Eugenol 50 µL + extra Cardamom | **Pink Pepper EO 30 µL** + Eugenol 18 µL + Cardamom 180 µL (unchanged) | -32 µL net |

### What V2 Achieves That V1 Cannot

- **Pink Pepper EO** delivers the *actual* Sauvage-style top note rather than a chemistry-textbook substitute. The eugenol-cardamom proxy is functionally close but olfactorily readable as a substitute by sensitive noses.
- **Saffron CO2** is the *signature*. When a wearer asks "what is that?" — that note is what they're noticing. Sauvage has no such moment. This is the bottle's identity.
- **Cypress EO** in the bridge is what makes the perfume read "expensive niche" rather than "good designer." Cypress is rare in mass-market masculine and instantly elevates the sophistication tier.

If acquiring all three is impractical, **the priority order is: Saffron > Cypress > Pink Pepper**. Saffron is the single highest-impact addition because it gives the bottle a memorable signature.

## Mixing Protocol (Both V1 And V2)

### Stage 1 — BHT antioxidant prep

Weigh `~1.2 mg` BHT into a clean 1 mL vial. Add `200 µL` ethanol 96%; swirl until fully dissolved. Set aside.

### Stage 2 — Add to concentrate vial in temporal order (slow → fast)

**Group A — Base/fixative tier (Act IV):** add first, allow 5 min between dense additions for distribution.

`Ambrox Super 30%` (2,000) → `Iso E Super` (540) → `Habanolide` (180) → `Galaxolide` (180) → `Sandalore` (180) → `Cedarwood Virginia` (240) → `Timberol` (60) → `Cashmeran` (60) → `Norlimbanol Dextro` (6) → `Coumarin 20%` (90)

**Group B — Bridge tier (Act III):** add second.

`Frankincense EO` (120) → `Vetiver EO` (60) → `Patchouli EO` (30) → `Nagarmortha Oil` (12)
(V2 only: + `Cypress EO` (30) + `Saffron CO2` (8))

**Group C — Heart tier (Act II):** add third.

`Hedione` (480) → `Lavender EO` (360) → `Linalyl Acetate` (180) → `Clary Sage EO` (120) → `Geraniol` (90) → `Phenethyl Alcohol` (60) → `Ethyl Linalool` (60)

**Group D — Top tier (Act I):** add last (most volatile, oxidation-sensitive).

`Bergamot FCF` (420) → `Cardamom FTEC 10%` (180) → `Petitgrain EO` (90) → `Lime Distilled EO` (60) → `Eugenol` (50) → `Spike Lavender EO` (30) → `Beta-Pinene` (30) → `Juniper Berry EO` (30)
(V2 only: + `Pink Pepper EO` (30), reduce Eugenol to 18, reduce Petitgrain to 60)

**BHT pre-solution:** add as final step before sealing.

### Stage 3 — Seal and macerate concentrate

Cap, invert `15×` gently. Macerate concentrate `7 days` before transferring to the 30 mL bottle. **The concentrate maceration is non-negotiable for this composition.** The four-act structure requires the materials to cross-condition before dilution; skipping this step results in a flatter final bottle.

### Stage 4 — Bottle and final maceration

Transfer concentrate to the 30 mL bottle. Add `24,000 µL` ethanol 96%. Cap, invert `20×` gently.

**Macerate `8 weeks minimum` before final evaluation.**

This is longer than the typical 4 weeks because:
- Frankincense + Vetiver + Patchouli need extended integration time with the woody base.
- Saffron (V2) develops over 6+ weeks; judging early misses the actual character.
- Hedione + Lavender + Clary Sage cross-conditioning takes 3-4 weeks alone.

### Evaluation Schedule

- **Week 1:** check direction only. Family read = aromatic fougère? If yes, on track.
- **Week 4:** check Act II and III bloom. The heart should be present and distinct from Sauvage by here.
- **Week 8:** verdict. Compare side-by-side with a 5 mL travel of Sauvage EDP on blotters at T+0, T+30 min, T+2 h, T+6 h, T+12 h.
- **Week 12:** final character locked. Make any small final adjustments only after this point.

## What "Beating Sauvage" Looks Like At Week 8

Side-by-side blotter comparison checklist:

| Time | Sauvage | Vol d'Aurore (target) | Vol d'Aurore wins if... |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T+0 (spray) | sharp pepper-bergamot-Ambrox punch | sharp pepper-bergamot-Ambrox punch with subtle cardamom warmth | parity, no clear winner |

**The win condition is not "smells better."** It is: at every time point past T+30 min, Vol d'Aurore reveals more facets, more dimensions, more *intention* than Sauvage. Sauvage cannot be described that way past minute 30; it is just "Sauvage." That is the difference between a well-engineered functional accord and a crafted composition.

## On The Question Of Beating Sauvage In Thai Mass Market

The honest commercial answer: a single bench-blended bottle does not beat a global marketing machine. Sauvage's market position is held by Johnny Depp + Dior distribution + duty-free saturation, not by olfactory superiority.

What this composition *can* do:

1. **Beat Sauvage on the noses of people who care.** Perfume enthusiasts, fragrance bloggers, sensitive wearers. Word-of-mouth tier.
2. **Beat Sauvage in the niche/artisanal segment.** Positioned at 2,500-4,000 THB / 30 mL with proper packaging, this composition is a credible competitor to Lattafa Asad, Maison Alhambra masculine line, and the upper Armaf tier.
3. **Beat Sauvage on the discerning gift-buyer.** The "she wants to give him something more sophisticated than Sauvage" buyer is a real segment in Bangkok and Chiang Mai mid-luxury retail.

What it cannot do without marketing infrastructure:

- Beat Sauvage on volume.
- Beat Sauvage on the unaware mass-market consumer.
- Beat Sauvage on the impulse-buyer at duty-free.

This is fine. The brief was *beat Sauvage on its market through art*. Art wins on the discerning end of any market. This composition is engineered to do that.
