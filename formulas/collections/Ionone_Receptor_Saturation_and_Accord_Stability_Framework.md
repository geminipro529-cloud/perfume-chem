# Ionone Receptor Saturation & Accord Stability Framework

## Purpose
Science-backed practical framework for maintaining the Opus V iris sub-accord identity across different perfume contexts, accounting for receptor saturation, olfactory adaptation, mixture suppression, and masking by added ingredients (coumarin, vanillin, woods, musks, etc.).

---

## Part 1: Ionone Receptor Saturation Science

### 1.1 The Receptor Binding Problem

Olfactory receptors (ORs) are G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) that detect odorants through a **combinatorial coding** system. A single odorant activates multiple receptor types, and a single receptor responds to multiple structurally related odorants (Buck & Axel, 1991; Malnic et al., 1999). This is the core of the iris accord problem:

**All ionone-family materials compete for overlapping receptor populations.**

The iris accord contains 6+ ionone-axis materials:
- **Alpha Irone** (30%) → the metallic-root iris character molecule
- **AIM (Alpha Isomethyl Ionone)** → powdery-violet synthetic ionone
- **Alpha Ionone** → fresh-violet, lighter than AIM
- **Beta Ionone** → warm-woody violet, cedar-adjacent
- **DHBI (Dihydro Beta Ionone)** → suede-woody ionone, reduced double bond
- **Ultralia** → ghost-iris at trace, powdery transparent ionone derivative

These all share the **ionone ring scaffold** (2,6,6-trimethylcyclohex-2-en-1-yl) and therefore activate substantially overlapping receptor ensembles — primarily:

| Receptor Family | Primary Ligands | Binding Character |
|---|---|---|
| **OR1A1** | Alpha Ionone, Beta Ionone, Alpha Irone | High-affinity ionone receptor; the "iris detector" |
| **OR5A1** | Beta Ionone (selective) | Narrower tuning; woody-violet register |
| **OR2W1** | Broader terpenoid recognition | Cross-activates with ionones at high concentration |

### 1.2 Saturation Kinetics at OR1A1

When an odorant binds an OR, the receptor undergoes conformational change → activates **G_olf** (olfactory G-protein) → stimulates **adenylate cyclase** → converts ATP to **cAMP** → opens cyclic nucleotide-gated (CNG) ion channels → Ca²⁺/Na⁺ influx → depolarization → nerve impulse.

**Saturation** occurs through two mechanisms:

**A. Competitive Binding Saturation**
At any given moment, each OR can bind only one ligand molecule. When 6 ionone-family materials are present simultaneously, they **compete for the same receptor binding pockets**. The receptor doesn't distinguish "Alpha Irone character" from "AIM character" — it fires or it doesn't.

At high total ionone concentration:
- OR1A1 approaches **100% occupancy** — every available receptor has an ionone bound
- Adding more ionone beyond this point produces **zero additional signal**
- The perceptual result: iris character hits a ceiling, then starts to smell "flat" or "blurry"

**B. Adaptation (Desensitization) Cascade**
Once Ca²⁺ enters through CNG channels, it triggers a negative feedback loop:

```
Ca²⁺ influx → binds Calmodulin (CaM)
    → Ca²⁺/CaM complex activates CaMKII
        → CaMKII phosphorylates CNG channels (reduces sensitivity)
        → Ca²⁺/CaM directly competes with cAMP at CNG channels
        → Phosphodiesterase (PDE1C) activated → degrades cAMP
```

**Result:** The neuron becomes progressively less responsive to ionone stimulation. The harder you push the ionone dose, the faster adaptation kicks in, and the **less iris** the wearer perceives over time — even though bystanders with fresh receptors still smell it.

### 1.3 Cross-Adaptation Between Ionone Isomers

Because Alpha Irone, AIM, Alpha Ionone, Beta Ionone, DHBI, and Ultralia all bind OR1A1 (with different affinities), exposure to any one causes **partial adaptation to all the others**. This is called **heterologous adaptation** or **cross-adaptation**.

Practical implications:
- If the iris block is 60% AIM (as in the XLSX accord), receptor adaptation to AIM partially reduces perception of Alpha Irone too
- The "character signature" of the accord depends on which ionone binds first during the opening phase — the one that adapts the receptors first shapes the entire iris perception
- **Irone-led vs AIM-led accords adapt differently**: Irone has higher binding affinity at OR1A1, so an Irone-led accord will dominate the receptor and mask the AIM contribution in the heart phase

### 1.4 Weber-Fechner Law Applied to Ionone Dosing

The **Weber-Fechner law** states that perceived intensity is proportional to the **logarithm** of stimulus concentration:

$$p = \alpha \ln\frac{S}{S_0}$$

Where:
- $p$ = perceived intensity
- $S$ = stimulus concentration (total ionone µL)
- $S_0$ = detection threshold
- $\alpha$ = sensitivity constant (receptor-dependent)

**What this means in practice:**

| Total Ionone (µL active) | Perceived Change | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 50 → 100 | +69% perceived | Clearly different, easily detectable |
| 100 → 200 | +69% perceived | Same perceived jump as 50→100 |
| 200 → 400 | +69% perceived | Diminishing returns per µL |
| 400 → 800 | +69% perceived | Each doubling gives the SAME perceived increment |
| 800 → 1600 | +69% perceived | Massive volume increase for same perceived effect |

**The "iris ceiling" in v6c:** With ~1740µL total ionone-axis materials (active doses), we're well into the logarithmic plateau. Adding another 200µL of ionone to make it "more iris" would produce barely perceptible change while wasting budget and accelerating adaptation.

**Practical ionone budget:** The sweet spot is typically **15-25% of concentrate as ionone-axis materials** for an iris-forward composition. Below 15% the iris is secondary; above 25% you're in saturation territory where additional ionone adds weight but not character.

## Part 2: Olfactory Adaptation & Mixture Perception

### 2.1 Three Levels of Adaptation

**Level 1: Peripheral Receptor Adaptation** (seconds to minutes)
- Mechanism: Ca²⁺/CaM/CaMKII cascade described in §1.2
- Onset: 30-120 seconds of continuous exposure
- Recovery: 1-5 minutes with fresh air
- Relevance: Why the wearer stops smelling their own perfume's heart notes; why initial "iris blast" softens quickly on skin

**Level 2: Glomerular/Bulb Adaptation** (minutes to hours)
- Mechanism: Lateral inhibition between glomeruli in the olfactory bulb. Active glomeruli suppress neighboring ones.
- Onset: 5-30 minutes
- Recovery: 30-60 minutes
- Relevance: Prolonged iris exposure causes the brain's olfactory bulb to actively suppress the iris glomerular map. Surrounding non-ionone notes become relatively more prominent. This is why a perfume's drydown always smells "different" from the opening — the brain has adapted to the dominant opening materials.

**Level 3: Cortical Habituation** (hours to days)
- Mechanism: Higher-order cortical processing simply stops flagging the stimulus as "new"
- Onset: 1-4 hours of continuous wear
- Recovery: 6-24 hours
- Relevance: After extended wear, the entire fragrance becomes invisible to the wearer. Not a dosing issue — a neural inevitability.

### 2.2 Mixture Suppression

When multiple odorants are presented simultaneously (as in any perfume), each component is perceived as **less intense** than it would be alone. This is called **mixture suppression** and it's distinct from receptor competition.

**Mechanism:** Lateral inhibition at the olfactory bulb level. When many glomeruli are active simultaneously, they inhibit each other's signal transmission to the piriform cortex.

**Magnitude of suppression:**

| Mixture Complexity | Suppression Factor | Practical Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 2 components | ~15-20% suppression per component | Binary mixtures lose ~1/5 of each note's apparent intensity |
| 5 components | ~30-40% suppression per component | Each note appears roughly 1/3 weaker than if smelled alone |
| 15+ components | ~50-60% suppression per component | In a complex perfume, each note is perceived at roughly HALF its solo intensity |
| 30+ components (full formula) | ~60-70% suppression | v6c with 34 ingredients: any single note is perceived at ~30-40% of its solo intensity |

**Critical insight for the iris accord:** When the iris block sits inside a 34-ingredient formula, each ionone is perceived at roughly **30-40% of its solo intensity**. This means:
- The iris that seemed overwhelming as a pure accord smells moderate in context
- Adding ingredients to the formula (coumarin, vanillin, woods) doesn't just add notes — it **suppresses** the iris perception proportionally
- Every new ingredient added to the formula requires a slight iris dosage increase to maintain the same perceived iris intensity

### 2.3 Masking and Partial Masking

**Masking** is different from suppression. Suppression is neural (bulb-level lateral inhibition). Masking is **perceptual** — one odor quality overwhelms another at the conscious level.

Materials that specifically mask ionone-iris character:

| Masking Material | Mechanism | Masking Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Vanillin / Ethyl Vanillin** | Sweet-balsamic overwhelms delicate powder-metallic; high hedonic valence captures attention | **Strong** — vanillin at >3% of concentrate will bury iris |
| **Coumarin** | Sweet-hay note competes directly with AIM's powdery facet; both occupy "sweet-powder" perceptual space | **Moderate** — coumarin at >2% shifts iris from "metallic" to "fougère" |
| **Eugenol / Clove** | Spicy-warm overwhelms the cold-metallic iris register | **Strong** — even small amounts shift the character |
| **Iso E Super** (high dose) | Abstract woody-skin-scent at high doses becomes "molecular fog" that obscures specific notes | **Moderate** — above 15% of concentrate, ISO E creates a halo that smothers iris specificity |
| **Musks** (heavy dose) | Clean-skin effect at high musk levels provides a "blanket" over detail | **Mild-Moderate** — musks below 8% of concentrate are fine; above that, iris character starts diffusing |
| **Heavy salicylates** | Cosmetic-waxy quality at high dose creates a "wall" that blocks iris transparency | **Moderate** — Benzyl Salicylate above 5% starts coating the iris |

Materials that **enhance** or **complement** ionone-iris character:

| Enhancing Material | Mechanism | Synergy Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Hedione** | Radiance amplifier — activates VN1R1 (vomeronasal-related receptor); gives airy volume to iris without masking | **Transparent lift** — 15-20% of concentrate ideal |
| **Methyl Ionone types** | Same receptor family but different facet emphasis; builds ionone complexity | **Additive** — extends the ionone code |
| **Musk Ketone** | Powdery-talcum character echoes AIM's powder axis without competing at OR1A1 | **Character echo** — reinforces powder without ionone competition |
| **Hexyl Salicylate** | Transparent fixative that extends iris persistence without adding cosmetic weight | **Invisible support** — fixation without masking |
| **Cedarwood / Timberol** | Woody spine that provides structural contrast, making iris stand out via figure-ground effect | **Contrast enhancement** — iris reads as more distinctive against a woody background |
| **Aldehyde sparkle** (C11/C12 at trace) | Metallic lift that reinforces Irone's metallic facet | **Frequency alignment** — similar glomerular activation pattern amplifies rather than masks |

## Part 3: Accord Stability Theory

### 3.1 The Roudnitska Proportionality Principle

Edmond Roudnitska (author of *L'Esthétique en Question*, pioneer of modern perfumery composition) established a foundational principle:

> **An accord is perceived as a single entity when its internal ratios remain constant. The external concentration can vary, but the internal proportions must not change.**

This is why the XLSX iris block works as a **sub-accord** — the 8 materials at fixed internal ratios (AIM 30.77%, Alpha Ionone 15.38%, Beta Ionone 11.54%, Ultralia 11.54%, DHBI 9.62%, Orivone 9.62%, Koavone 7.69%, Alpha Irone 3.85%) function as a single perceptual unit when the ratios are maintained.

**Scaling rule:** When embedding this block into different formulas, scale the entire block proportionally. Never change one material relative to the others.

### 3.2 Ellena's Transparency Architecture

Jean-Claude Ellena's approach (as perfumer for Hermès, author of *Perfume: The Alchemy of Scent*) adds a critical refinement:

> **An accord maintains its identity best when surrounded by transparent, non-competing materials rather than dense, characterful ones.**

Practical translation:
- Build the **non-iris portion** of the formula from transparent materials (Hedione, Hexyl Salicylate, Amberwood F, transparent musks)
- Avoid dense, opaque materials at high doses (heavy vanillin, dense patchouli, thick labdanum) which create "perceptual mud"
- The iris accord's identity survives when it operates in **clear space** — when the surrounding materials provide structure and volume without adding competing character

### 3.3 Nagel's Accord Embedding Method

Philip Nagel (IFF senior perfumer, specialist in modular composition) described a systematic approach to embedding accords:

**The 60-30-10 Rule for Accord Visibility:**
- If the accord is **60%+ of concentrate**: It IS the perfume (soliflore). Surrounding materials are pure structural support.
- If the accord is **30-40% of concentrate**: It is the clear star. Surrounding materials should be transparent and structural (Hedione, salicylates, musks, woods).
- If the accord is **15-25% of concentrate**: It is recognizable but must be protected from masking. Surrounding materials must avoid competing registers.
- If the accord is **<15% of concentrate**: It becomes a coloring agent. Its specific character is lost; only its general quality class (powdery, woody, floral) survives.

**For the iris sub-accord across formulas:**
- Iris soliflore: Accord at 55-60% of concentrate
- Iris dominant (like v6c): Accord at 35-45% of concentrate
- Iris forward in a non-iris context (e.g., iris-woody, iris-leather): Accord at 20-30%
- Iris accent (e.g., supporting a rose or amber): Accord at 10-15% — only the "powdery" quality survives, not the specific ionone character

### 3.4 Threshold-Based Stability

Each ionone has a detection threshold and a recognition threshold:

| Material | Detection Threshold (ppb in air) | Recognition Threshold | Practical Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha Irone | ~0.1-1 ppb | ~3-10x detection | Extremely potent; tiny amounts provide full character |
| AIM | ~5-10 ppb | ~3-5x detection | Moderate potency; needs more volume than Irone |
| Alpha Ionone | ~1-5 ppb | ~3-5x detection | Moderate; fresh-violet facet |
| Beta Ionone | ~0.5-2 ppb | ~3-5x detection | Very potent; woody-violet dominates at low levels |
| DHBI | ~10-50 ppb | ~5-10x detection | Less potent; suede facet requires volume |
| Ultralia | ~1-5 ppb | ~5x detection | Moderate; ghost-iris at sub-recognition threshold |

**Stability rule:** For the accord to maintain identity across different contexts, each component must remain **above its recognition threshold** after accounting for mixture suppression. If a component drops below recognition threshold, its specific character is lost and the accord shifts.

**Calculation method:**
```
Required µL (in formula) = (Recognition threshold / Solo threshold) × Suppression factor × Base dose

Where:
- Suppression factor = 1 / (1 - suppression%) 
  For 34-ingredient formula: 1 / (1 - 0.60) = 2.5x
- Base dose = dose needed in a binary mixture to achieve recognition

Practical: multiply the solo-recognition dose by ~2.5 for a complex formula
```

### 3.5 Contrast Theory and Figure-Ground

Borrowed from Gestalt psychology of perception:

> **A note stands out not by being louder, but by being different from its surroundings.**

The iris accord maintains identity best when the surrounding materials provide **contrast** rather than overlap:

**High contrast (iris maintains identity):**
- Iris + dry cedar (Timberol) — metallic vs. architectural
- Iris + clean musk (Romandolide) — powdery vs. transparent-skin
- Iris + Hedione — powdery-metallic vs. airy-radiant
- Iris + light citrus (Cedrat) — powdery vs. bitter-fresh

**Low contrast (iris identity blurs):**
- Iris + heavy vanillin — both occupy "sweet" register; iris powder drowns in vanilla sweetness
- Iris + dense coumarin — both occupy "hay-powder" register; iris becomes generic fougère
- Iris + opaque amber (Ambermax) — warmth competes with iris's cold-metallic character
- Iris + heavy rose (Phenylethyl Alcohol neat) — floral density competes with ionone's floral facet

### 4.3 The Coumarin/Vanillin Problem (Specifically)

**The user's stated concern:** The iris accord must smell the same even when surrounded by coumarin and vanillin for creaminess.

**Why this is the hardest context for iris:**

1. **Perceptual register overlap:** AIM's powdery facet and Coumarin's hay-powder facet occupy nearly the same perceptual space. When coumarin is present, AIM's contribution is partially "absorbed" into the coumarin perception — the brain reads it as "more coumarin" rather than "iris + coumarin."

2. **Hedonic competition:** Vanillin has one of the highest hedonic valences of any aroma material. The brain preferentially attends to high-hedonic stimuli. In a mixture, vanillin "steals attention" from iris, which is less immediately pleasant and more cerebral/complex.

3. **Receptor-level interaction:** Vanillin activates a partially overlapping receptor ensemble with some ionones. Not identical receptors, but close enough that cross-adaptation occurs.

**Dosing corrections for creamy context:**

| Material | Base Dose (transparent context) | Corrected Dose (with Coumarin 3% + Vanillin 2%) | Correction Factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total Iris Block | 100% | **135-150%** | 1.35-1.5x |
| Specifically Alpha Irone | 100% | **150-170%** | 1.5-1.7x (Irone's metallic character is MOST vulnerable to vanillin masking) |
| AIM | 100% | **120-130%** | 1.2-1.3x (AIM's powder partially merges with coumarin — less correction needed because the merge isn't entirely unwanted) |
| Ultralia | 100% | **140-160%** | 1.4-1.6x (Ghost-iris is extremely delicate; first casualty of any masking) |

**BUT — maintain internal ratios.** The correction should ideally be applied uniformly (1.35-1.5x to the whole block). If you must deviate, Irone can be individually boosted by up to 1.2x its internal ratio without breaking the accord (it's a character-peak material — slightly more Irone sharpens the metallic edge, which is exactly what vanillin masks).

### 4.4 Specific Adjustment Protocol

**Step-by-step process when adding the iris block to a new formula:**

**Step 1: Calculate the unsuppressed iris budget**
- Determine total concentrate volume
- Set target iris %: 35% for iris-forward, 25% for iris-accented
- Iris budget (µL) = concentrate × target %

**Step 2: Count competing ingredients**
- Count all non-iris ingredients in the formula
- Look up mixture suppression factor:
  - 5-10 non-iris ingredients: suppression factor 1.3x
  - 10-20 non-iris ingredients: suppression factor 1.6x
  - 20-30 non-iris ingredients: suppression factor 2.0x
  - 30+ non-iris ingredients: suppression factor 2.5x

**Step 3: Identify masking materials** and add context coefficient
- Sum coumarin + vanillin + heavy balsams as % of concentrate
- 0-2%: no correction (+0)
- 2-5%: moderate masking (+0.2 to coefficient)
- 5-10%: heavy masking (+0.4 to coefficient) — WARNING: iris will struggle
- 10%+: iris will be invisible — reconsider the formula concept

**Step 4: Calculate corrected iris budget**
```
Corrected Iris Budget = Base Iris Budget × Context Coefficient

Example: 30mL/25% formula, iris-forward
- Concentrate: 7500 µL
- Base iris budget: 7500 × 0.35 = 2625 µL
- Non-iris ingredients: 25 → suppression factor 2.0x
- Creamy materials at 4% → masking correction +0.2
- Total coefficient: 1.0 × 1.2 = 1.2 (context already accounted in % choice)
- Corrected budget: 2625 × 1.2 = 3150 µL iris block
- Scale the 8-material block to sum to 3150 µL
```

**Step 5: Apply the iris block using internal ratios**
```
For 3150 µL total iris block:
- AIM:          3150 × 0.3077 = 969 µL
- Alpha Ionone: 3150 × 0.1538 = 485 µL
- Beta Ionone:  3150 × 0.1154 = 363 µL
- Ultralia:     3150 × 0.1154 = 363 µL
- DHBI:         3150 × 0.0962 = 303 µL
- Orivone:      3150 × 0.0962 = 303 µL
- Koavone:      3150 × 0.0769 = 242 µL
- Alpha Irone:  3150 × 0.0385 = 121 µL (of 30% dilution = 36 µL active)
```

**Step 6: Verify against saturation ceiling**
- Total active ionone (OR1A1-relevant): AIM + Alpha Ionone + Beta Ionone + Irone active
- = 969 + 485 + 363 + 36 = 1853 µL active ionone
- In a 7500µL concentrate: 24.7% active ionone — within the 15-25% sweet spot
- If >25%: reduce block or accept saturation plateau
- If <15%: iris will be secondary; increase block

### 4.5 Warning Signs During Evaluation

**The iris is too saturated (reduce block by 10-20%):**
- "All I smell is powder" — AIM dominance, no metallic edge
- "It smells like violets, not iris" — Beta Ionone dominance, Irone below recognition
- "The opening and heart smell identical" — no temporal development because receptors are saturated from blotter application
- "It smells 'stuffy' or 'close'" — classic saturation indicator; too many ionones competing for the same receptors

**The iris is being masked (increase block by 15-30% or reduce competing materials):**
- "I smell the coumarin/vanillin but where's the iris?" — masking by sweet materials
- "The iris only appears in the drydown" — competing top/heart materials masking the iris during their peak projection
- "It smells generic-powdery, not specifically iris" — AIM's powder is present but Irone's character is below recognition threshold
- "My tester says it smells great but I can't smell anything" — your own receptor adaptation (Level 1+2); ask someone with fresh receptors

**The accord has shifted (internal ratios may be wrong):**
- "It smells woody-violet, not iris" — Beta Ionone or DHBI ratio too high relative to AIM/Irone
- "It smells synthetic-clean, not natural-root" — AIM ratio too high relative to Irone
- "It smells buttery but no powder" — Orivone ratio too high, AIM too low
- Compare against the pure iris block on a blotter to recalibrate your perception

## Summary: The Three Laws of Iris Accord Stability

### Law 1: Ratio Constancy (Roudnitska)
> The 8 internal ratios of the iris block must remain fixed regardless of total dose or surrounding context.

### Law 2: Logarithmic Dosing (Weber-Fechner)  
> Doubling the iris block produces a perceptually small increment. Compensate for masking by 20-50%, not 100-200%. Small adjustments have large perceptual effects in the low range; large adjustments have small effects in the high range.

### Law 3: Contrast, Not Volume (Ellena/Gestalt)
> The iris is best protected by surrounding it with transparent, non-competing materials rather than by increasing its dose. When forced to add masking materials (coumarin/vanillin for creaminess), increase the iris block by the minimum coefficient needed (1.3-1.5x) and ensure at least one metallic-brightening element (Irone boost, aldehyde trace, Hedione radiance) remains above recognition threshold as an iris "beacon."
