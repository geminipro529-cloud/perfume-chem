## Concept Lock

The darkest classical amber in the set: labdanum, benzoin, olibanum, opoponax, myrrh, and Peru balsam under a dry tobacco-suede-saffron identity. Guaiacwood, patchouli, and cedar form the rear shadow. Modern amberwoods provide pressure and persistence but do not replace the resin amber.

Every macro-plane contains a miniature sequence of deep seed, body, contour, upper veil, and return thread. The completed loop is:

Deep benzyl-musk residue → dry woody body → guaiac-resin rear contour → luminous classical amber → tobacco-suede identity → saffron-irone veil → peppered mandarin glint → evaporation reveals a transformed saffron-incense aura above the residue.

## Material Selection Rationale

- **Classical amber:** Labdanum provides the leathery amber seed, Siam Benzoin the warm balsamic body, and olibanum the luminous return. Opoponax, myrrh, and Peru balsam continue that resin structure into the darker rear plane.
- **Tobacco identity:** Tonkarome supplies coumarinic tobacco body, Kephalis gives dry architectural tobacco-amber pressure, and Suederal adds worn texture. Heliotropal and the two ionones create the powder-violet contour connecting tobacco to saffron.
- **Saffron recursion:** Alpha Irone forms a violet-suede seed, Safraleine provides the dry leathery saffron edge, and the Red Saffron Infusion supplies the colored as-supplied saffron identity. Hedione and linalool raise it into an upper aura.
- **Dark rear contour:** Guaiacwood is the smoky wood shadow, not the principal resin. Patchouli supplies earth, cedar dryness, myrrh breath, and the balsams dark rounded mass.
- **Modern amber supports:** Amber Xtreme, Ambrox Super, and Azarbre provide three distinct registers—pressure, crystalline return, and warm cedar contour. They support the resins rather than redefining amber as an amberwood block.
- **Single musk:** Ethylene Brassylate was selected for soft, creamy residue diffusion. A clean projection musk would weaken the aged tobacco-resin image.
- **Fixative gradient:** Benzyl Benzoate anchors the residue while Benzyl Salicylate carries it upward toward saffron and olibanum.

## Recursive Compounding Procedure

1. Use one clean amber vial and add P7 through P1 in the formula-table order.
2. After every completed plane, cap and gently roll until homogeneous. The planes are compositional relationships, not literal unmixed liquid layers.
3. After the concentrate reaches 6,000 µL, mix until uniform.
4. Add approximately 22 mL ethanol 96%, mix, then add ethanol q.s. to the 30.00 mL final-volume mark.
5. Inspect clarity and color immediately, at 24 hours, 7 days, and 14 days. The saffron and resin materials are expected to make this the darkest concentrate, but haze or sediment must still be recorded rather than assumed acceptable.
6. Evaluate on blotters at 0, 5, and 30 minutes; 2, 6, 24, and 48 hours. The decisive DPP question is whether the dark residue regenerates a recognizable saffron-olibanum aura rather than ending as a flat woody amber.
