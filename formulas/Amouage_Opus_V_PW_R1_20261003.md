# Amouage Opus V / Woods Symphony — PW research interpretation R1

Date: 3 October 2026. Mode: RECONSTRUCTION / NEW DESIGN HYPOTHESIS. Basis: w/w.

A dry iris–rhum perfume with quiet rose/jasmine and a secondary oud–civet wood base. This is an original, testable interpretation of the marketed architecture, **not Amouage's disclosed formula, a certified clone, or a physically compounded bottle**. The canonical dose/stock specification is [the accompanying JSON](Amouage_Opus_V_PW_R1_20261003.json); source decisions and the full campaign work-order review are in [the research record](../docs/research/Amouage_Opus_V_PW_Research_20261003.md).

## Reference lock

[Amouage's current page](https://amouage.com/en-ca/products/opus-v-woods-symphony) names orris/rhum, orris concrete/rose/jasmine, and agarwood/civet/dry woods. The [2023 Amouage FAQ](https://intercom.help/amouage/en/articles/6281737-the-library-collection-faqs) identifies Jacques Cavallier and describes a name/packaging-only relaunch. It says 20% oils, whereas the current product page says 24%. Neither specifies a mass/volume convention. We therefore use our own **24% w/w source-product-equivalent evaluation load**, not an authenticated original concentration.

Keep root/butter/wax iris readable over dry woods. Rhum must be a dry spirit/fermented-fruit/cask accent, not maple syrup. Animality must sit underneath the iris, not become a fecal perfume. Rose and jasmine are background texture. Negative space: no rose-oud, sweet vanilla gourmand, generic suede reconstruction, citrus cologne, or large amberwood/musk bomb.

### If the bottle must be 30 mL

**30 g is not 30 mL.** Prepare sufficient perfume at the chosen w/w loading, then measure 30 mL of the homogeneous finished perfume and retain any remainder. For example, scaling this recipe to 40 g uses 12.9168 g master and 27.0832 g additional alcohol; measure the final volume rather than asserting 40 g will always fill a particular vessel. Do not use “7.2 g oils + alcohol to 30 mL” and call that 24% w/w; that would be a mass-per-volume recipe with a different definition.

## Working-stock preparation

All strengths here mean mass of the **specified supplied product** divided by final stock mass. Use the identical supplied product to preserve the intended product target.

- **10% w/w stocks:** 0.200 g product + 1.800 g named carrier = 2.000 g stock. Needed for Orris Liquid, Alpha Irone, Carrot Seed EO, Ethyl Butyrate, Ethyl 2-Methylbutyrate, Rose de Mai, Jasmine Absolute, Cashmeran, and Civet Art in TEC; Vanillin uses perfumer alcohol.
- **1% w/w stocks:** 0.050 g product + 4.950 g TEC = 5.000 g stock, separately for Damascenone and Guaiacol.
- **50% w/w Guaiacwood in TEC:** 1.000 g product + 1.000 g TEC = 2.000 g. Warm the nonflammable material/carrier container indirectly only as necessary, away from ignition, and mix fully. Do not assume a cooled heterogeneous stock remains doseable as a homogeneous solution.
- **Civet Art is a synthetic mixture, not civetone.** No animal-derived civet is requested. Never replace it by the same mass of pure civetone, IBQ, skatole or castoreum.
- **Orris Butter** is weighed directly into the master. Blend into the larger woody/ester portion until uniform. If wax separates or the diluted perfume hazes, stop and investigate a weighed solubility trial; filtration is not allowed to erase an unknown amount of formula unnoticed.

Use only perfumery-grade materials and appropriate ventilation/gloves/eye protection. No open flame, ingestion or concentrate skin application. These dilution recipes are not safety clearance.

## Mixing order

1. Prepare and label only the required working stocks. Verify homogeneity; do not infer preparation from a recommendation.
2. Weigh the larger wood/body materials: Iso E Super, Vertofix, AIMI, Vetiveryl Acetate, Kephalis, Hedione and Benzyl Salicylate.
3. Disperse Orris Butter and dissolve the **weighed Ambrox crystals** into that matrix with the carrier-containing additions. Do not heat alcohol over a flame; use time/agitation or a verified pre-dissolved stock and recalculate its carrier.
4. Add the remaining iris materials, cedar/patchouli/oud/cypriol/Guaiacwood, then rose/jasmine and the musks.
5. Add trace stocks and rum/volatile esters last, cap promptly, and mix until genuinely homogeneous. For the shared-core trials below, keep the four withheld additions separate instead.
6. Weigh the chosen master aliquot; add alcohol to the target **finished mass**. Check immediately and again after cooling/rest for crystals, settling or haze.
7. Compare after an initial 48–72 hour integration, again around two weeks, and, if useful, around four weeks. These are practical observation points, not a proof that a proprietary six-week factory ageing regime has been reproduced.

No invented requirement to wait two minutes between every ingredient, no claim that sequence alone creates molecular “synergy,” and no automatic bottle/inventory commit.

Code the samples, use the same blotter/application procedure, and compare with an authentic Opus V sample if available. Evaluate 5 minutes, 30–60 minutes, 4 hours, and next-day blotter. Ask:

- Can you identify iris/root/butter rather than only violet powder?
- Is the rum distinguishable from ordinary sweetness or tropical fruit?
- Does the civet add a background undertone, or only obvious dirt?
- Does the two-musk lead outperform both equal-total single-musk alternatives?
- Does dry wood support iris, or erase it?

Reject/revise on overt fecality, maple/vanilla gourmand drift, pineapple/coconut, rose-oud, dominating suede/amberwoods, thin root identity, or unstable/hazy samples. If an extra layer does not help blind, remove or replace it in a **new version**, not in the historical tested record.
