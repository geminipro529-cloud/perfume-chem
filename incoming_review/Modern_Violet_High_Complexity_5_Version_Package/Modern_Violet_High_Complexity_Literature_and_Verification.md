## Design doctrine

The original 31–41-row formulas are preserved as low-complexity controls. The canonical high-complexity formulas contain 74–82 distinct odor materials and were rebuilt rather than padded add-only.

Complexity is divided among:

- ionone and irone violet spectrum;
- root and orris texture;
- living petal accord;
- leaf, dew and mineral air;
- variant-specific woods;
- variant-specific musks;
- resin, suede, powder or confectionery texture;
- volatile opening and aromatic bridges.

A row is not allowed to count merely because it appears on a spreadsheet. Ablation classifies each row as identity-critical, structural, textural, or microtexture requiring physical confirmation.

## Inventory reconciliation

The formulas do not use Clearwood, Amber Core, or Amber Xtreme. Cedramber is dosed as **neat Cedramber**, not as an imaginary 10% stock. Javanol uses the confirmed **20% DPG stock**. Sandalwood EO uses the confirmed **10% DPG stock**. Generic Rose EO is not used; Rose de Mai Absolute 10% is used where a rose-absolute function is required.

Product-basis materials remain product basis. Orris Liquid, Sandalwood Base X3, Turkish Storax tincture, Vietnamese Benzoin tincture, Nympheal, Azabre, and Tuberose EO Volume Grade do not receive invented exact active fractions.

## Honest complexity boundary

The coarse computational model can identify structural and textural contributors, but it cannot prove that every one-part microaddition will be perceptible in the physical mixture. Those rows are explicitly marked **MICROTEXTURE / PHYSICAL CONFIRMATION** rather than receiving invented certainty.
