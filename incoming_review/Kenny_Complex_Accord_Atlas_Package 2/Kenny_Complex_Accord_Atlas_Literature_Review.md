## Purpose

This atlas turns the current inventory into reusable high-complexity accord systems where every row has a distinct job. Complexity is accepted only when it changes identity, diffusion, texture, phase movement, persistence or failure-mode control.

## Literature conclusions

1. **Time and matrix matter.** HS-SPME and chromatographic studies show that perfume release changes with time, surface and sampling conditions. Accord evaluation therefore uses staged top, heart and base modelling and requires physical review from 0 minutes to 24 hours.
2. **Natural smells are multi-odorant systems.** Jasmine, rose, vetiver, sandalwood, apple and peach studies identify families of key odorants rather than a single molecule. The accord formulas therefore use recognizer, texture, air, persistence and contrast layers.
3. **OAV is not reconstructed from liquid percentage.** No strict empirical OAV is released because compatible measured gas-phase concentrations are absent.
4. **Patents are precedent, not formula proof.** Irone, muguet and multi-musk patents support material-function plausibility only.
5. **Stock identity is part of the formula.** Product-basis materials remain product basis, Javanol is used only at 20% in DPG, Sandalwood EO only at 10% in DPG, and unavailable Clearwood or generic Rose EO are not inserted.

## Computational gate

Each accord must:

- close at exactly 1,000 supplied-stock parts;
- use no gap, banned or out-of-stock material;
- preserve its primary recognizer groups;
- contain at least ten non-carrier rows and at least three meaningful sensory groups;
- keep any one non-carrier row below 30% in the nominal formula;
- survive 5,000 lognormal stock/dosing perturbations with at least 90% pass probability;
- remain separated from every sibling in its category;
- expose all product-basis and VERIFY LABEL locks;
- document material ablation as identity-critical, structural, textural or micro-polish.

## Literature register

### METHOD-01 · Time-resolved perfume evaporation

A modular in-vivo/in-vitro device uses headspace sampling and chromatography to compare perfume release across surfaces and time.

**Program use:** Supports staged top/heart/base modelling and physical follow-up design.  
**Authority:** Peer-reviewed method  
**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39265418/  
**Boundary:** Method only; not a measurement of these accords.

### METHOD-02 · Residual fragrance on skin

HS-SPME recovery depends on fiber and collection method, while substantive odorants can persist to 24 h.

**Program use:** Supports 5 min–24 h review schedule and uncertainty bands.  
**Authority:** Peer-reviewed method  
**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39054565/  
**Boundary:** No strict headspace values are claimed here.

### MIX-01 · Mixture interaction

Odor mixtures can show additive, suppressive or synergistic effects, especially around threshold.

**Program use:** Supports Monte Carlo structure testing instead of summing percentages as smell.  
**Authority:** Peer-reviewed background  
**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32247502/  
**Boundary:** Does not prove a specific accord smells good.

## Physical review protocol

1. Mix 1 mL first, where 1 part equals 1 µL.
2. Rest according to the accord sheet.
3. Smell neat on blotter and at the low end of the intended use range.
4. Record 0 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 8 h and 24 h.
5. Reject an accord that matches its note name but fails its texture or phase goal.
6. Make at most two bounded revisions. A third failure forces a clean architecture.
7. Run current supplier-specific safety and stability review before skin use.
